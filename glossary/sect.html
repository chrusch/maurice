A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

sect

party · sectarian

Frequency in the corpus: sect and sects 286; sectarian and sectarianism 26; party and parties 175. The central source is the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “God the Uprooter of Sects”, on Matthew 15:13.

In brief. A body of men bound together by their own choice around certain opinions, badges and objects, as against the Church, which God has planted and which witnesses to his love for all mankind. A sect chooses Christ for his doctrine, and then chooses a teacher with the best form of it, so that Christ himself is lost in opinions about him. A party is the same thing in English form, a sect organized with flags and watchwords. Maurice believes every such plant will be rooted up, but that the work is God’s, not ours; the worst sin is to form a new party to overcome the old ones. Each sect may yet bear witness to a truth.

Shades of meaning

  1. Planted by men, not by God. The Pharisees, though “emphatically the religious sect” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “God the Uprooter of Sects”), were a plant God had not planted: “That had been established by men with a kind of notion that it might further His glory, that it might preserve His Name from being forgotten” (same). Moses and David founded no sect; they bound a people together under a Deliverer and a King. See nation.

  2. The sect against the Church. The sect’s profession is a voluntary bond: “We have bound ourselves together for the purpose of promoting certain great objects; we have adopted certain distinct badges of our faith; we are pledged to oppose that which interferes with it” (same). By contrast, “Christ chooses the Church to be a witness of His love to mankind. The sect chooses Christ because it is convinced that His doctrine is better than that of the founders of other religions” (same). The result: “So the person of Christ becomes lost in certain opinions which the sects have taken up respecting Him” (same). See Church and opinions · notions.

  3. Sect or Universal Society. Israel’s crisis was whether it “should end in a sect or in a number of sects, or should unfold itself into that Universal Society, of which they and all the Prophets had believed that it was the germ” (same). The same choice faces every Church: the Romanist “had changed his Church into a sect” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter), and the religious world, “the counterfeit of the Church”, is “trying to reduce all Churches into a Babel of sects” (same). See society, sense 5.

  4. The English party. “Elsewhere the defenders of a system may merely form a school. In England, because by constitution we are politicians and not systematisers, they must form a party. The moment we have adopted a peculiar theory we begin to organise. We have our flags and our watchwords, our chiefs and our subordinates” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”). Even children “must become polemics before they can repeat their catechism” (same). See system.

  5. Rooted up by God, not by us. Each party applies the text to its enemy: “Each party turns to the one which it wishes to overthrow, and says solemnly” that its rival is a plant God has not planted (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “God the Uprooter of Sects”). But Christ added Let them alone: “If you try to destroy the sect which my Heavenly Father has not planted, you would destroy something of that Church which He has planted” (same). The task is to show “the members of parties that when their mud houses fall down, there is a house of God not made with hands” (same). See judgment.

  6. No new party. “Above all, we must never be tempted to that greatest of all sins, the forming a new party for the sake of displacing or overcoming existing parties” (same). Maurice wrote “perhaps without approximating nearer than he did to the sentiments of any one of the parties which divide the Church” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Dedication”), believing “that Truth is above both” (same).

  7. What each sect witnesses to. Sects are not simply false: “each sect has been great when it has accepted a Gospel of Christ, and protested against some religion which was hiding Him from men” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). See witness, sense 5, and system, sense 5.

  8. Party spirit. The vice behind them is a temper: Maurice prays for the love that will “destroy the grovelling, petty desires, the party spirit, the self-seeking that have made us a world of sects instead of a church of brothers” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XV). At the cross the claim “that our sect or party is the way to God” is exposed (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Great Sacrifice and the Feast of Deliverance”). See self-will.

Neighbouring words

  • Church, Catholic (what a sect counterfeits). Senses 2 and 3; see Church and Catholic.
  • system, school (its intellectual form). Sense 4; see system.
  • opinions, notions, watchwords (its bond). Sense 2.
  • religious world (the sum of the sects). Sense 3.
  • unity (what it breaks). See unity, sense 3.
  • witness, protest (its truth). Sense 7.
  • Pharisee (its type). Sense 1.

Scripture. Matthew 15:13–14, Every plant, which my heavenly Father hath not planted, shall be rooted up. Let them alone, the text of “God the Uprooter of Sects”; 1 Corinthians 1:12–13, I am of Paul; and I of Apollos; and I of Cephas … Is Christ divided? (sense 2); 2 Corinthians 5:1, an house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens (sense 5); Acts 24:5, a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes.
