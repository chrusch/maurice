A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

love

charity

Frequency in the corpus: love 1,671; charity 205.

In brief. The nature of God and therefore the law of the universe. Maurice holds that “God is love” is not a pious feeling but the starting point of theology: the Church must “assert the love of God absolutely” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “Passion Week; its Dangers and Blessings”) and reason from it. Human love is not something we own but a share in his, received by submitting to him. Charity is the same word in the language of 1 Corinthians 13 and the Prayer Book, and he uses it for love as the ground of all things.

Shades of meaning

  1. The foundation of theology. “The passages, “God so loved the world,” “God is love,” are not rapturous utterances of a devotional sentiment; they are the scientific foundation of a Catholic and Evangelical Theology” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “Passion Week; its Dangers and Blessings”). The Church starts there: “her first duty is to assert the love of God absolutely, apart from all consequences that may flow out of it” (same paragraph).

  2. Charity as the centre. The Theological Essays open with the same claim in the Pauline word: “Charity is the ground and centre of the Universe: God is Charity” (Theological Essays, “On Charity”). A charity worth the name is not mere tolerance; it is “infinite Charity”, “bearing all things, hoping all things, enduring all things” (same essay).

  3. The law of the universe. Love is not extra virtue but obedience: “Love is the law of the universe; that in loving, we do not perform an act of supererogation, but merely submit to a duty” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV).

  4. The power of God. Love is what rules: “No power but Love can establish that harmony. The Revelation of Jesus Christ declares that Love is the power of God” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVIII). It does not abolish his strife with evil: “the revelation of God as universal love is not inconsistent with that prior revelation of Him, as the Being who is carrying on continual strife with whatever in our world resists and opposes law and order” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Objections of the Quaker”).

  5. Received, not owned. Human love flows from God’s: “His Love is the ground of our love; that we only love when we submit to His operations” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV). Perfection comes by giving up a love of our own: “we may abandon the thought of having a charity or love of our own, and so may be perfected in love” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XV). In prayer: “All the imperfect love I ever had for them has flowed from Thine” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Fatherly Character of God the Only Ground of Repentance, Consolation, Hope”).

  6. Universal. The love of God is for all, and the doctrine of atonement rests on it: “mine rests on the Eternal Love which overlooks all distinctions, which embraces the universe” (Theological Essays, “On the Atonement”).

  7. Love and truth. Love without principle is counterfeit; Lecture XIX speaks of “a principle apart from which love is a worthless and contemptible affectation” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIX).

Neighbouring words

  • Father (its source). Sense 5; see Father.
  • faith (its trust). Christ’s own faith rested on love: “Christ’s own Faith was in the love of His Father” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVI).
  • law, order (what it is and upholds). Senses 3 and 4.
  • sacrifice (its act). See sacrifice.
  • self-will (its opposite). See self-will.
  • tolerance (its counterfeit). Sense 2.
  • God (identity). “There is no love but of God, and God is love” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XX).

Scripture. 1 John 4:8, God is love, and John 3:16, God so loved the world, sense 1; 1 Corinthians 13:2, though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing (RV love), the text of “On Charity”; 1 Corinthians 13:7, Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things; 1 John 4:19, We love him, because he first loved us (sense 5).
