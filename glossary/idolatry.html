A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

idolatry

idols · idolater · the visible

Frequency in the corpus: idol, idols, idolatry and their forms in about 350 paragraphs. Spread through the Lincoln’s Inn sermons, Lectures on the Apocalypse, The Epistles of St. John and The Kingdom of Christ.

In brief. Idolatry is worship of the visible in place of the unseen Lord: making God a thing of sense, or the soul’s thirst turned to things unworthy or unreal. It is natural to every man, and the whole Bible is a testimony against it and a record of how prone men are to it. Idols are gods who are not deliverers but tyrants, and so idolatry ends in fear, slavery and loss of courage, though it begins as an escape from the awe of an invisible Presence. Modern idols are Pleasure, Money and Fame, and the idols of the mind. Those who will bow to no visible thing see that all visible things are good.

Shades of meaning

  1. The worship of visible things. Israel’s harlotry is idolatry: “It forgets its unseen King, and becomes the worshipper of visible things—it becomes a creature of sense” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XVII). The Greeks “were continually exalting the lower light above the higher light, and supposing the higher to come from the lower. This was their idolatry” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). See light.

  2. Every man’s proneness. “The Bible has been throughout a testimony against idolatry—a testimony, at the same time, of every man’s proneness to idolatry” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXII). Even St. John, seeing the divinest truths, “assures us twice that the temptation had not been overcome in him” (same). “The impulse to this Heathenism lies in us all” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Thirst of Human Beings in All Ages”).

  3. Gods who are not deliverers. The prophet “sees why they must put down idols; why idolatry has been the sin and the curse of his nation. He sees that Gods who are not deliverers, Gods who” are tyrants, are what his people turn to (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Perfect Revelation”). See Deliverer.

  4. It ends in fear. “Superstition and idolatry terminate in universal fear; but they begin with the effort to get rid of fear. A man does not like the feeling of an Invisible Presence, of One Who is near him by night and day, spying out all his ways” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God”). “Idolatry is said to be the great destroyer of courage” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Courage”). See fear.

  5. Organised into a system. Babylon was the land “where idolatry was organised into a system; where it was the obvious and confessed support of a universal tyranny” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XVII). The nation “is now, as it ever was, pledged to treat idolatry as its curse and destruction” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Sacredness of Common Morality”). See nation and system.

  6. Pleasure, Money and Fame. “Who shall say that the Goddess of Pleasure is worshipped in St. Giles’s more than in Belgravia; the God of Money more in cellars and garrets than on the Exchange” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Thirst of Human Beings in All Ages”). “While we mourn over our gross idolatries, over the foul orgies of our Mammon-worship, we shall feel a confidence that all idols everywhere, of every kind, shall be utterly abolished” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “National Holiness”). See world.

  7. Idols of the mind. Teachers need to learn “how prone we all are to worship idols of the cave and idols of the forum” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System”). The modern man “does not become an idolater of the powers of nature; but he worships its evenness and persistency” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Scriptures”). See opinions · notions.

  8. Bowing to no visible thing. “Those who have purged their hearts of idolatry, who will bow to no visible thing, must have the rich reward of seeing that all visible things are good and do homage to the Eternal King.” And “wherever idolatry prevails, there will be Manichæism, the belief that some part of the Creation is evil” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). Those who find Christ at the altar and also in the study gain “help to preserve them from all idolatry of any outward things whatever, whether they be the elements of bread or wine” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Advent of Christ, and the Eucharist”). See sacrament and Eucharist.

  9. The unclean spirit returning. The house swept of one unclean spirit is “the spirit, as most have said, and, I doubt not, truly, of idolatry; the spirit that tempts us to seek God in visible forms” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XV). See evil spirit.

Neighbouring words

  • visible, sense (its sphere). Senses 1 and 8.
  • heathenism (its name among the nations). Senses 2 and 6.
  • Mammon (its modern god). Sense 6.
  • superstition, fear (its fruits). Sense 4.
  • tyrant (what its gods are). Senses 3 and 5; see Deliverer.
  • Manichæism (its shadow). Sense 8.
  • the invisible, the unseen King (what it forgets). Sense 1.

Scripture. Exodus 20:3–5, Thou shalt have no other gods before me. Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image (senses 1 and 3); 1 John 5:21, Little children, keep yourselves from idols (sense 2); Revelation 19:10 and 22:8–9, John falling to worship the angel (sense 2); Daniel 3, the image on the plain of Dura (sense 8); Matthew 6:24, Ye cannot serve God and mammon (sense 6); Matthew 12:43–45, the unclean spirit returning (sense 9).
