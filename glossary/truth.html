A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

truth

the Truth · God of Truth · Spirit of Truth

Frequency in the corpus: truth and true in well over a thousand paragraphs. The country sermon “Truth”, the Lincoln’s Inn sermons and The Epistles of St. John are the main sources.

In brief. Truth, for Maurice, is first a character in God: he is the God of Truth, who hates every lie and is pledged to destroy lies. Christ is the Truth as a living Person, not a proposition, and the Spirit is the Spirit of Truth, who guides men into all truth. So truth is something men seek and are received into, never something they make. It is required in the inward parts, against every self-deception and evasion. Living truths stand opposed to abstract notions and to the opinions of parties. The worst decay of a nation is when its teachers make a principle of not speaking the truth.

Shades of meaning

  1. The God of Truth. A man may abstain from false witness through prudence, but to abstain because God has commanded it is possible “only if he thinks of God continually, as a God of Truth, as the hater of every false way and false word, as pledged to destroy lies as well as to lay them bare” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture V). Christ “came from the God of Truth that He might deliver us from our lies, and might enable us to walk in His truth” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXI, “Truth”). See righteousness.

  2. A living Truth in a Person. The Name overthrew the old divided worship “because it was felt that there was one inclusive Truth revealed to mankind, a Truth which we cannot comprehend, but which comprehends us; a living Truth, which speaks itself out in a Person not in a proposition” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Uniting Name”). “Christ, Who is the way and the truth and the life, has ascended on high that there might be a way for us into the presence of the Eternal Living Truth” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Sacredness of Common Morality”). See person and Name.

  3. Sought, not made. The reason “is only in its proper state when it is seeking for truth and does not think that it can make truth” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). The exhortations to seek truth “are sound and wholesome, because God is a God of Truth, and because He is our Father, and because He has promised His Spirit” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XV). See knowledge.

  4. Truth in the inward parts. The psalmist’s prayer means: “Thou wantest me to be a true man; Thou wantest me to be true in my dealings, true in my speech, true to those that are above me and below me and my equals” and “true in my own self, true in all that I plan and purpose” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXI, “Truth”). We cannot keep ourselves true, so we ask God “to reform me, because I cannot reform myself” (same). See conscience and repentance.

  5. The truth in us. “There is a truth in him, in the man who confesses his sin. There is a truth against which the sin is fighting” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIX). That truth is God’s: “the ‘truth in us’ is His truth; that it is there because we are related to Him” (same). See sin, sense 4.

  6. The Spirit of Truth. “He can enable you to dispense with other teaching. He alone can make other teaching profitable to you. For He does not deceive you. He is the Spirit of Truth” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture X). Those who cannot depend on their own truthfulness “ask the Spirit of Truth to guide them into all truth” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture IX). True originality, Maurice says, comes from a longing “to find what is not dependent on ourselves or on human caprice which, I believe, is awakened in us by the Spirit of Truth, and by Him only” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). See Spirit.

  7. Living truths and abstract notions. He finds it unsurprising that an old argument “should be repeated in a day when abstract notions have been so much substituted for living truths” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System”). Experience leads back to the conviction “that we have here the living Truth which underlies and sustains both the natural and the spiritual world” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Bond of Peace”). See opinions · notions and system.

  8. The truth spoken out. “When its teachers not only dare not speak out the truth, but actually make a principle of not speaking it”, and think “that there is a higher and better way of serving the God of Truth than by being true”, then “we may be sure that society has become radically corrupt” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Why Christ Could Not Make His Authority Known to the Pharisees”). Ministers of the God of Truth “must wish anything untrue we have said to be detected” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “How Sin Pays, and How God Gives”). See witness.

Neighbouring words

  • lie, falsehood, evasion (its opposites). Senses 1 and 8; the Devil is the father of lies (see evil spirit).
  • righteousness, justice (its companions in God). Sense 1.
  • Person, Name (its living form). Sense 2.
  • seeking, inquiry (the mind’s right relation to it). Sense 3.
  • truthfulness, veracity (its human form). Senses 4 and 8.
  • opinions, notions, propositions (its substitutes). Sense 7.
  • Spirit of Truth (its teacher). Sense 6.

Scripture. Psalm 51:6, thou desirest truth in the inward parts, the text of the country sermon “Truth” (sense 4); John 14:6, I am the way, the truth, and the life (sense 2); John 16:13, the Spirit of truth … will guide you into all truth (sense 6); 1 John 1:8, the truth is not in us (sense 5); John 8:32, the truth shall make you free; Ephesians 4:25, putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour (sense 8).
