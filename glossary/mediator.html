A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Mediator

one Mediator

Frequency in the corpus: Mediator 79.

In brief. Christ as the one in whom God and man actually meet and are united. Maurice’s Mediator is not a third party who shields men from an angry God, but the eternal Word in whom God made the worlds and in whom he has always held converse with men. Because there is one Mediator for all men, no priest, saint or system can stand between man and God, and every human ministry takes its meaning from his. Maurice thinks the denial of a real Mediator has consequences for politics as well as for religion.

Shades of meaning

  1. Where God and man meet. Christ’s death proves him “to be the Mercy-seat, the living Mediator, in whom God meets with His creatures, and in whom His creatures meet Him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon X). “He is still, as ever, the Mediator, in whom we are at one with Him, in whom we may draw nigh to Him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVI).

  2. Eternal. The office did not begin at the Incarnation. The Cross showed “the Eternal Mediator, the Living Word, in whom God had created the worlds, in whom He had held converse with the sons of men” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII). The resurrection declared him “the Mediator and High Priest who had always united God to man” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture IV).

  3. Not a shield from God. Maurice names the false notion plainly: that “we want a deliverer, not from Atheism, but from God; a Mediator to prevent Him from fulfilling His cruel designs against us; not a Mediator to reveal to us His love and to purge us from our own cruelty, that we may be partakers of it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Cry for God’s Judgments”). He traces the error to the Reformers: “The idea of an absolute will, with which man must be brought into reconciliation by a Mediator, lay at the base of all Luther’s thoughts” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Pure Protestantism”).

  4. One for all men. To believe in communion between earth and heaven “is to confess that there is one Mediator for all men” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV). All other mediators and priests derive “their worth and meaning” from him, and are “nothing” without him (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture IV). A priest’s office should witness to “a direct communication between men and a living God through the ascended Mediator” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Christian Priest”).

  5. Advocate, not accuser. In the victory of the Cross, “we are sure that there is not an accuser, but a Mediator between us and our Father” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV).

  6. The key to all facts. The union of God with man in a Mediator interprets everything else: “if once we receive that fact of God’s union with our race in the Person of a Mediator as the interpretation of all other facts,—as the kernel mystery of the Universe” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Parables of Christ”).

  7. Political consequence. Without a Mediator there is only despotism: “When the Incarnation is denied,—when a Mediator between God and man is utterly rejected—you must have an absolute Caliph or Sultan” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Ministry of Sovereigns”).

Neighbouring words

  • Head (his relation to the race). See Head.
  • Advocate, High Priest, Reconciler (fellow titles). Senses 2 and 5.
  • union, communion (what he makes). Sense 1; see member · union.
  • priest, priesthood (derived from him). Sense 4.
  • sacrifice (his act). See sacrifice.
  • absolute will, sovereignty (the false God he would shield us from). Sense 3; see self-will.
  • Incarnation (his manifestation). Sense 7.

Scripture. 1 Timothy 2:5, there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, behind sense 4; Hebrews 12:24, Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, quoted often; 1 John 2:1, we have an advocate with the Father, the text of Sermon XVI in The Doctrine of Sacrifice; Revelation 12:10, the accuser of our brethren is cast down (sense 5).
