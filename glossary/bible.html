A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Bible

Scripture · the Scriptures

Frequency in the corpus: Bible 596; Scripture and Scriptures 698. The main discussions are The Kingdom of Christ II, “Section VI. The Scriptures”, and the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “The Sacramental Character of the Bible”.

In brief. For Maurice the Bible is not a book of doctrines or a code of rules but the history of God’s revelation of himself to a family, a nation and mankind, in which the events are interpreted as his acts. It is the record of a kingdom, and so it belongs with the Church: each explains the other. Its authority rests on what it reveals, a living Person, not on any theory of its letters. Maurice therefore holds fast to its plain sense and resists both the system-builders who turn it into propositions and the critics who turn it into a mere collection of documents.

Shades of meaning

  1. A history of revelation. The Bible tells of God making himself known: “The postulate of the Bible is, that man could not be what he is, if God did not hold converse with him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon I). Of the meaning of sacrifice gradually unfolding to Israel’s seers: “The Bible is a history of the discovery” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII). See revelation.

  2. Facts interpreted as signs. The history is ordinary history read in God’s light: “What makes the history a Bible is, that all these facts are interpreted. They are shown to be signs” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Sacramental Character of the Bible”). So it is “a book of business and of life, of plain practical morality” (same), not “a book of riddles and allegories”. See sign.

  3. The book of a society. Its subject is a family, a nation and a kingdom, not detached souls. Men must “read it now, not less than in the old time, as a book concerning a society” (same), tracing “the progressive history of God’s revelations to a family, to a nation, and to mankind” (same). It is “remarkably a book of laws, a book explaining the Divine order of the universe; if it be not this it is nothing” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Scriptures”). See family, nation and kingdom.

  4. Bible and Church as mutual interpreters. “I have supposed the Bible and the Church to be mutual interpreters of each other. The Church exists as a fact, the Bible shews what that fact means. The Bible is a fact, the Church shews what that fact means” (same). Romanism erred by hiding the Bible “on purpose that the Church might be exalted” (same). See Church.

  5. Not merely a book. The standard Maurice sought as a young man “must be a Life. It must be set forth in a living Person” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). Hence: “If the Bible was merely a book, though it might be the best of all books, though it might be a book without a flaw, I could not hope to find in it that which I was seeking” (same). The Church needs “not an infallible book; an infallible Person” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Faith of Catholics”), a phrase Maurice quotes and accepts. See revelation.

  6. Its central word. What the Bible says, against every religion of appeasement, is that God seeks man: “The Bible says that man has cast off God, and that God is seeking to bring him into union with Himself” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Faith of Protestants”).

  7. The letter of Scripture. Maurice will not allegorize the text to draw morals from it: “I think that it is our duty, and therefore our wisdom, to adhere to the letter of Scripture” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Hungry Filled; the Rich Sent Away Empty”). He also insists that the Bible is unsystematic, and that this is not a defect: it is “commonly acknowledged by religious persons, that the Bible is remarkably unsystematic” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “The Scriptural View of This Constitution”).

Neighbouring words

  • revelation (what it records). Sense 1; see revelation.
  • sign, sacrament (how it reads events). Sense 2; the Lincoln’s Inn title calls its character “sacramental”. See sign.
  • Church (its counterpart). Sense 4; see Church.
  • Creed (its summary in act). The Creeds are the Bible’s facts confessed; see Creed.
  • the Word, the living Person (what it points to). Sense 5.
  • system, theory (what it is not). Senses 5 and 7.
  • letter, criticism, inspiration (the controversies over it). Sense 7.

Scripture. Romans 15:4, whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning, the text behind the Advent collect Maurice preaches on; John 5:39–40, Search the scriptures … and they are they which testify of me. And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life (sense 5); 2 Corinthians 3:6, the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life, which Maurice cites against both allegorists and literalists.
