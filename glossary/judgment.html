A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

judgment

judge · Judgment Day

Frequency in the corpus: judgment and judgement 532; judge 272. The main sources are Theological Essays, “Essay XII. The Judgment Day”; the Lincoln’s Inn sermons “The Judgment Day an Object of Intense Desire” and “Cry for God’s Judgments”; and The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XV.

In brief. God’s judgment is first discernment: the bringing to light of what is true and what is false. It is the work of Christ as Light, and it is going on now; the last judgment is the full unveiling of a tribunal before which men already stand. The Psalmists longed for it as the earth’s deliverance, and Maurice wants the Church to long for it again. The Judge and the Saviour are one Person with one purpose.

Shades of meaning

  1. Discernment, not sentence. The office of a judge is chiefly to find the truth, as any jurist knows: “to discern who is right and who is wrong; amidst a multitude of shifting, distracting appearances, to find out the fact; to detect the lie which is hidden under the plausible, coherent story” (Theological Essays, “The Judgment Day”). In the Bible “the idea is kept before us of judgment, in its fullest, largest, most natural sense, as importing discrimination or discovery” (same).

  2. A tribunal standing now. The judgment-seat of Christ is not to be set up in a distant future but is “one before which we, in our own inmost being, are standing now, and that the time will come when we shall know that it is so, and when all which has concealed the Judge from us will be taken away” (same). Paul’s word for the coming is apocalypsis, “unveiling” (same). See revelation.

  3. God’s judgments in history. The last judgment is of one kind with every crisis in which God judges a nation. Paul “compels us to look upon every one of what we rightly call “God’s judgments” as essentially resembling it in kind and principle” (same). The Bible calls such crises days of the Lord, and its language about them is “not metaphorical, but most real” (same). It will come “in the council chamber, in the exchange” and “through Indian mutinies, through commercial panics” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Judgment Day an Object of Intense Desire”). See nation.

  4. Judgment desired. The prophets longed for it: “They desired, earnestly desired, that God would arise to judge the earth” (same sermon), and “They looked for this judgment as its deliverance from its miseries and its bondage” (same). So should we: “shall not we learn also that it is good for us to be judged; most miserable for us to escape judgment?” (same).

  5. The Judge is the Saviour. Christ “passed sentence on no man, no Scribe, Pharisee, Publican, Adulteress” (same), yet his coming was the Advent “of the true King, and Head, and Judge of Men” (same). Maurice rejects “a feeling that the Judge and the Saviour are different beings, or the same being pursuing different purposes” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Cry for God’s Judgments”). Tyrants of every kind “dread the appearing of Him Who is the true Judge of men, because the sufferer for them and with them” (same). See Mediator.

  6. Judgment within. Self-examination is safe only if God is the judge in the conscience: “Is not the judge on the side of the criminal? Not if St. John speaks truly; not if we dwell in God and God dwells in us. Then He is the judge Himself” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XV). That is the ground of boldness in the day of judgment.

  7. The judgment of men. Against God’s judgment stands the tyranny of human opinion, the “oppressive judgments of human beings” from which men seek escape (Theological Essays, “The Judgment Day”). Faith in Christ’s judgment frees them from it.

Neighbouring words

  • light, manifestation, unveiling (its means). Senses 1 and 2: “that which doth make manifest, is Light” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Judgment Day an Object of Intense Desire”).
  • righteousness (its law). God judges Israel and its enemies “by the same eternal law of righteousness” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Cry for God’s Judgments”). See righteousness.
  • Advent (its season). The two Advents, of humility and of glory, are one coming (sense 5).
  • responsibility (the moral question it answers). The essay begins from it (sense 7).
  • Creed (its confession). From thence he shall come to judge the quick and the dead; see Creed.
  • wrath, vengeance (its popular image). Maurice keeps them but subordinates them to discernment.

Scripture. Psalm 82:8, Arise, O God, judge the earth: for thou shalt inherit all nations, the text of the Lincoln’s Inn sermon; 2 Corinthians 5:10, we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; 1 John 4:17, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment; John 3:17, God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; Hebrews 4:12, a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart (sense 1).
