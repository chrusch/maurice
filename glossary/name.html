A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Name

the Name · into the Name · Hallowed be thy Name

Frequency in the corpus: name 1,379; names 152; hallowed 53; into the name 22; in the name 84.

In brief. A name, for Maurice, is the disclosure of a character, and the Name of God is God made known: as Deliverer to Israel, and as Father, Son and Holy Spirit in the baptismal command. That Name is the foundation of the Church and of any universal society, the object of the Creeds, and the first thing the Lord’s Prayer asks for. To be baptized into the Name is to be taken into the relation it reveals.

Shades of meaning

  1. A character revealed. A name tells who someone is. Maurice has the Israelite reason: “My God is a Deliverer, a deliverer of slaves from the hand of a tyrant. That is the name by which He wishes me to remember Him; that must denote what His character is” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture V). Of the beast’s mark in the Apocalypse: “The name or character of the great tyrant of any age” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XIII).

  2. The Name of Father, Son and Spirit. The full Name, given with the command to baptize, gathers up the earlier ones: “as the ultimate basis of the universal society, a Name is proclaimed, in which the name that had been revealed to Abraham, and that more awful one which Moses heard in the bush, are combined and reconciled” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. III, “The Scriptural View of This Constitution”).

  3. The foundation of the Church. “The Church to which I belong, the Church which I read of in the Bible, is baptized into the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Ghost. This name is its foundation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). Maurice’s into the Name anticipates the Revised Version’s reading of Matthew 28:19; see baptism.

  4. The object of the Creeds. The Creeds are “belief in a name, and not in notions” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Creeds”); see Creed, sense 1.

  5. Hallowed. The first petition of the Lord’s Prayer puts knowledge of God’s Name before every human need. Superstition “springs from ignorance of the name of God; it consists in and by that ignorance” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon II, “Hallowed Be Thy Name”). In The Kingdom of Christ the petition is a lesson against private religion: “Dost thou desire to be very holy? Yet this must not be thy petition; thou must say, “Hallowed be thy name.”” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Forms of Worship”).

  6. The bond with God. Prayer and forgiveness rest on Christ’s Name: “His Name is the only bond to God; that to lose hold of it is to fall back into our selfishness” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VII).

  7. A name that is not one man’s. The Name Christ bears is mankind’s: “the Name which He bears is not that of some separate individual, but of the Man, the Brother and Head of Men” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIX). The same holds, at a lower level, of a nation’s name: “to be a nation—to feel that the name is a reality and not a fiction, this is hard” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture VII).

Neighbouring words

  • character (gloss). Sense 1.
  • revelation (its act). A name is what revelation gives; see revelation.
  • Father (its centre). “The Name of Father, which Christ proclaimed, becomes the Name which interprets all others, which includes all others” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “Christmas Charities”).
  • notions, titles, abstractions (opposite). Abstract titles for God, “such names as ‘The Divinity,’ or ‘Providence,’ or ‘Omnipotence,’ become offensive to us when once we think that the fulness of the Godhead dwelt in the Son of Man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “Christmas Charities”).
  • baptism, Creed (its sacrament and its confession). Senses 3 and 4.
  • Church, universal society (what it founds). Senses 2 and 3.
  • Son of Man (Christ’s own name). Sense 7; see Son of Man.

Scripture. Matthew 28:19, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost (RV into the name); Matthew 6:9, Hallowed be thy name; Exodus 3:14–15 and 6:3, the names given to Moses and to the patriarchs (sense 2); 1 John 2:12, your sins are forgiven you for his name’s sake (sense 6).
