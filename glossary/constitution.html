A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

constitution

order · divine order

Frequency in the corpus: constitution and constitutions 293; order 833 (many in the ordinary senses). The key sources are The Kingdom of Christ* I, “Indications of a Spiritual Constitution” and “Signs of a Spiritual Society”, and the Preface to* The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven.

In brief. The structure God has given to human life and to the universe, which men find and live within but do not invent. Maurice speaks of a “spiritual constitution” of mankind, made of family, nation and a universal society, and of a Divine order beneath all the disorders of history. Christ is its revealer and assertor; his miracles restore it rather than break it. Men can rebel against the constitution, and can build a counterfeit order out of their own lusts; but the true order is never changed, and every judgment reveals it. Maurice’s order is the positive term against which system is measured.

Shades of meaning

  1. A spiritual constitution. Family relations already show “some of the indications of a spiritual constitution; that is to say, we have the marks of a state which is designed for a voluntary creature; which is his, whether he approve it or no; against which, he has a nature or inclination to rebel” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Indications of a Spiritual Constitution”). A constitution is given, not chosen. See family.

  2. Family, nation, and the universal. “We have observed the traces of a spiritual constitution for mankind. We have observed that the two parts of this constitution, which are united by family relationships and by locality, depend upon a third part which is universal” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”). The universal part has two possible forms, “one of which is destructive of the family and national principle, the other the expansion of them” (same): the world and the Church. See nation, world and Church.

  3. The constitution of our race in Christ. Baptism “assumes Christ to be the Lord of men; it assumes that men are created in Him; that this is the constitution of our race; that therefore all attempts of men to reduce themselves into separate units are contradictory and abortive” (same). See baptism and ground · root · foundation, sense 4.

  4. The Divine order beneath history. In Isaiah’s day Israel’s permanence did not depend on its signs or forms: “There was a Divine King and a Divine order beneath all and above all. He was holy, whatever else was unholy. There was right at the foundation of things, however power might seem to trample upon right” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture IV). See king and righteousness.

  5. Christ its revealer; miracles its restoration. The Evangelists claim for Jesus “that He was the revealer and assertor of the Divine order, that He entered into conflict with the anomalies which disturbed that order” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). He was not breaking law in his cures: “Was He not doing His Father’s will by banishing the diseases which had interrupted the order of His Creation?” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture VII). See sign and nature.

  6. The world an Order, and its perversion. St John’s word world means order: “though there are a number of disorders in the world, yet that the world itself is an Order” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VIII). The world John forbids us to love is something else: “The world, the order which is fashioned out of these, is not God’s order, but a perversion of God’s order,—that which we have substituted for His” (same). See world.

  7. Its root in the Father’s will and the Son. “This eternal and absolute Will of God is the root from which all the order of the universe proceeds” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVII). The revelation of the primal unity of Father and Son “is the revelation of an order which sustains all the intercourse and society of men” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XII). See Trinity and society.

  8. Unchanged, and revealed in judgment. The Apocalypse unveils the order and does not alter it: “There has been no change in the Divine order; no conversion of any one thing into its opposite. We are reading of a revelation, not of a scheme” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXII). Human systems are each “a miserable, partial, human substitute” for it (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”); see system, sense 3, and judgment.

Neighbouring words

  • system, scheme (its human counterfeit). Senses 6 and 8; see system.
  • kingdom (its personal form). Sense 5; see kingdom.
  • law (its expression). The laws of the kingdom are “more fundamental, less capable of any suspension or modification, than the laws that were written on stone” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface); see law.
  • family, nation, Church (its parts). Sense 2.
  • disorder, anomaly (its disturbance). Senses 5 and 6.
  • ground, foundation (what it stands on). Sense 4; see ground · root · foundation.
  • society (what it sustains). Sense 7.

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 14:33 and 40, God is not the author of confusion … Let all things be done decently and in order; Colossians 1:17, by him all things consist (sense 7); 1 John 2:15–17, Love not the world … the world passeth away (sense 6); Isaiah 6:1, I saw also the Lord sitting upon a throne, behind sense 4.
