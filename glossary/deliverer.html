A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Deliverer

deliver · deliverance

Frequency in the corpus: Deliverer (capitalised) 212, deliverer 123; deliver and its forms over 900. Most frequent in the country sermons and The Doctrine of Sacrifice.

In brief. The first name by which God made himself known to Israel, and so for Maurice the key to the character of God. The God of the Ten Commandments speaks as the Lord thy God which brought thee out of the house of bondage, the deliverer of slaves from a tyrant. Christ is the Deliverer from sin, death and the evil spirit, who sets free body and spirit alike. Men are always tempted to think of God as the oppressor from whom they need deliverance; Maurice’s constant answer is that they know him only by the name of Deliverer.

Shades of meaning

  1. The name God chose. “My God is a Deliverer, a deliverer of slaves from the hand of a tyrant. That is the name by which He wishes me to remember Him; that must denote what His character is” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture V), says the Jew who would keep the first commandment. The Israelite was to hear each commandment as “I, the Lord thy God, thy Deliverer, say to thee, thou shalt not kill” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XI, “The Deliverer from Crime”). See I AM, sense 4, and law.

  2. Known only by that name. In the Passover Israel answers the heathen: “Why! we know Him only by that name of Deliverer. We were in bondage to a tyrant; He broke our chains” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IV). Yet belief in God as Deliverer is not natural to men: “To say that belief in God as a Redeemer and a Deliverer is easy and natural for men, that it is their tendency to accept and retain this belief, is to contradict the evidence of all history” (same). See redemption.

  3. Not the oppressor. Men “have supposed Him to be a Tyrant over men—governing them according to His fancies, one from whom they have need to seek deliverance. He proclaimed Himself to the Israelites as their Deliverer out of the house of bondage” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXI, “The New Covenant”). “When we are not taught that God is our Friend and our Deliverer, we are very apt to think of Him as our enemy and our oppressor” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXIII, “The Law of Liberty”). See Father.

  4. The deliverer of the spirit. “God is Himself the Redeemer, the Deliverer, of a spirit out of the fetters which it has forged for itself” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon X). The Jew’s curse becomes a blessing “if it has led him, in weariness and sorrow, to cry for a Teacher and Deliverer of his spirit” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII). See freedom and conscience.

  5. From sin and death. “Our spirits have fallen under the power of sin; our bodies have fallen under the power of death. Christ, our true king, has shown that He is mightier than both; that He is the deliverer from both” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XL, “Suffering and Glory”). At Nain the fear was “Why there is a Deliverer among us! There is one who is stronger than Death among us!” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXVIII, “God’s Visitations”). See death · eternal death and sin.

  6. Body and spirit together. The paralytic forgiven “could look up into the face of Jesus and be certain that He was the Deliverer” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, VI, “The Sick of the Palsy”), and then “found that the Deliverer had not done a half work” (same): the palsy was gone too. See body · resurrection.

  7. King and Deliverer. The Corinthians waited for “He who had been declared to be the deliverer of the world—who had proved Himself so by dying for it—who was proving Himself so in their hearts” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, IV, “Waiting for Christ”). “An eternal Deliverer is the only refuge from this great world-tyranny” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XIII). See king.

  8. Deliverer of the oppressed. Moses turned the Nile to blood “that Pharaoh might know the Lord of the earth to be the Deliverer of captives” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XI). The song of Moses “is the song of God’s victory over the oppressor, of God’s deliverance of the oppressed. It never can be obsolete as long as there are oppressors and oppressed in the creation” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XV). The mammon-god, by contrast, is “Not a helper, not a deliverer at all—but a bitter tyrant” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XVII, “God and Mammon”). See idolatry and justice.

Neighbouring words

  • Redeemer, Saviour (near synonyms). Senses 2 and 4; see redemption.
  • Friend (its constant pair). Senses 3 and 8.
  • King, Lawgiver (its other offices). Senses 1 and 7.
  • tyrant, oppressor, destroyer (its opposites). Senses 3 and 8.
  • bondage, chains, fetters (what it breaks). Senses 2 and 4.
  • Litany (its prayer). Good Lord, deliver us, which Maurice tells his hearers never to doubt (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXIII, “The Law of Liberty”); see Catechism · Articles · Prayer Book.

Scripture. Exodus 20:2, I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage (senses 1 to 3); Exodus 15:1–3, the song of Moses (sense 8); Romans 11:26, There shall come out of Sion the Deliverer (sense 7); Matthew 6:13, deliver us from evil; Psalm 18:2, The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer.
