A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Creed

Creeds · I believe

Frequency in the corpus: creed 207; creeds 107.

In brief. For Maurice the Creeds are acts of allegiance to a Name, said by a person, not summaries of doctrine. Their form is I believe; what they confess is a Father, a Son and a Spirit and what they have done. That is why they belong to worship and to every peasant and child, and why he sets them apart from the dogmatic Articles and confessions that systems draw up.

Shades of meaning

  1. Belief in a name, not notions. The central claim, from the section on the Creeds in The Kingdom of Christ: “A man is speaking in it. The form of it is, I believe. That which is believed in is not a certain scheme of divinity, but a name” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Creeds”). The Nicene Creed keeps the same principle: “It is still I believe; it is still belief in a name, and not in notions” (same section).

  2. An act of allegiance. “The Creed is evidently an act of allegiance or affiance; and since it has ever been connected with Baptism, one must suppose that from Baptism it derives its interpretation” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Creeds”). Baptism acknowledges us; the Creed is our answer: “by this act we claim our spiritual position, we assert our union with that Being” (same paragraph).

  3. Part of worship. In the Prayer Book the Creeds are said, not studied: “They are made parts of our worship; acts of allegiance, declarations by the whole congregation of the Name into which each one has been baptized; preparations for prayer; steps to communion” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Do the Signs of a Universal and Spiritual Constitution Exist in England?”).

  4. For everyone. The Creed has been “repeated by the peasants and children of the different lands into which it has come” and “given to them as a record of facts with which they had as much to do as any noble” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Creeds”). That even the Nicene Creed was not kept for theologians is, for Maurice, evidence that God’s name is “really the only ground of a universal society” (same section).

  5. A sign of the Church. The Creed is one of the facts that show “a spiritual and universal constitution of society for mankind” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Creeds”). See sign, sense 2.

  6. Not our opinions. Defending the Creeds as one’s own opinions turns them into weapons. Maurice traces the cruelty of churchmen to “the feeling that they were their Creeds, the utterance of their opinions” (What is Revelation?, Letter IX).

Neighbouring words

  • Name (its object). Sense 1; see Name.
  • Baptism (its interpreter). Sense 2; see baptism.
  • Articles, confessions (contrast). The Thirty-nine Articles “do not take the living forms of the creeds, they constitute a set of distinct dogmatic propositions; they would be ridiculous if introduced into worship” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Do the Signs of a Universal and Spiritual Constitution Exist in England?”).
  • system, scheme, notions (opposite). Sense 1.
  • opinion (its corruption). Sense 6.
  • worship, prayer (its setting). Sense 3.

Scripture. Romans 10:9–10, with the mouth confession is made unto salvation; Matthew 28:19, the baptismal Name the Creeds unfold (sense 2); 1 Timothy 6:12, a good profession before many witnesses.
