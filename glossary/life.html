A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

life

the life of God · Lord and Giver of life · the Living God

Frequency in the corpus: life in several thousand paragraphs; the life of God, the Living God, Giver of life and similar phrases in about 450.

In brief. Life, for Maurice, is derived. Man’s life is not his own possession but flows from the life of the Son of God, the Lord of every man, in whom is life, and whose life is the light of men. It becomes a vain show when a man wishes to have a life of his own, seeking it in the things he should rule. To love is the state of life and to hate the state of death, because love is communion with the life of God. Eternal life is the knowledge of God, and that knowledge does not earn the life; it is the life. The Living God, not a God read of in a book, is the Bible’s name for him, and the Spirit is the Giver of life.

Shades of meaning

  1. Derived from the Son. “The life of man, so the Gospel declares, is not vanity, for it is derived from the life of the Son of God. He is the Lord of every man. In Him is life, and His life is the light of men” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “God’s Government of Us; its Ground and its End”). See Word and Head.

  2. A life of one’s own. “The life of a man becomes a vain show, just because he does not confess his relation to this Fountain of Life; just because he wishes to have a life of his own; just because he seeks that life where it is not to be found,—in the things which he is to rule, not in the Lord who rules him. From this vanity Christ redeems him” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “God’s Government of Us; its Ground and its End”). See self-will and redemption.

  3. Love the state of life. “Is it not the state of Death to hate? Is it not the state of Life to love? Did we not hate because we had been separated from the life of God? Are we not able to love, because we are in communion with the life of God?” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIII). The hater “is cutting himself off from the universe; he is at war with its law” (same). See love and death.

  4. Knowledge constitutes the life. Christ did not say we obtain eternal life by knowing God, but that this is life eternal, to know him: “The knowledge does not procure the life, but the knowledge constitutes the life” (Theological Essays, “On the Trinity in Unity”). See knowledge and eternal.

  5. The perfect life in his death. “The perfect law of God, because the perfect life of God, is set forth in Him; specially in His death” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIII). The heresy St. John fought “said that Christ had not shown forth the eternal life of God, in the acts, words, death of a man” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XX). See sacrifice and law.

  6. The Living God. “The greatest temptation, perhaps, of this age, is to think of the Most High rather as one about whom we read in a book, than as the Living God, the name by which the book always speaks of Him” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon I). “The weakest, who trusts in the living God, may defy earth and hell to confound him” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “God the Justifier of Man”). Those weary of opinions and sects “wanted a Living God” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV). See person and Bible.

  7. The Life and the Giver of Life. St. John “speaks of Christ as the Life and the Giver of Life”, as often as he calls him the Light of the World (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXII). The heathen were “doing their best to vindicate the faith of their countrymen in a living Being, a source of life, and to separate this faith from the unbelief which turned that Being into an author of death” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “Christ the Lord of Life and the Conqueror of Death for All Times”). See Spirit and death.

  8. Life more than meat. On Take no thought for your life: “If we are fidgetty and restless about it, we shall do ourselves no good. But if we trust it with God, then we shall go manfully forth to do all that we have to do, knowing that we are safe in His hand” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXVII, “Life and Raiment”). See faith · trust.

  9. The common life of a nation. The nation’s life “had been associated again and again by the Psalmists and Prophets with the life of trees, of the fig-tree and the vine especially” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XVII). The disciples, abiding in “the life-giver to all”, became able “to become sufferers with their fellows, to bring them life” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Vine and the Branches”). See nation and member.

Neighbouring words

  • Fountain of Life (its source in the Son). Senses 1 and 2.
  • vanity, vain show (life cut off from its source). Sense 2.
  • death (hatred and separation). Sense 3; see death.
  • eternal life (its true name). Sense 4; see eternal.
  • Living God (the Bible’s name). Sense 6.
  • vine, branches (its shared form). Sense 9.

Scripture. John 1:4, In him was life; and the life was the light of men (sense 1); 1 John 3:14, We know that we have passed from death unto life, because we love the brethren (sense 3); John 17:3, this is life eternal (sense 4); John 15:1–5, the vine and the branches (sense 9); Matthew 6:25, Is not the life more than meat? (sense 8); Psalm 42:2, My soul thirsteth for God, for the living God (sense 6); Ecclesiastes 1:2, vanity of vanities (sense 2).
