A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

peace

the Lord of Peace · My peace · peace-maker

Frequency in the corpus: peace in about 300 paragraphs. The sermons “Peace: What it is” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6) and “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5) are the main sources, with The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII.

In brief. Peace for Maurice is not ease, quiet, or freedom from conflict. Christ gave His peace on the night of His betrayal, and the Apostles went at once into the worst disquiet of their lives. His peace is that by which He passed through opposition and agony: trust in the Father. It belongs to the very nature of God, the Lord of Peace, who is also the Lord of Hosts. It is not bought by prayers or good deeds but given directly. It harmonizes all energies instead of stifling them, demands the destruction of what is base, and makes peace-makers of those who receive it.

Shades of meaning

  1. Not exemption from strife. Christ’s peace cannot have meant peace with the rulers, for “He had come into the world with a sword. His Gospel would divide men asunder, in the same nation, in the same household” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “Peace: What it is”). “The peace then which He spoke of must be something different from external ease and quietness” (same). Nor did it mean freedom from inward temptation, since the Apostles wrestled with principalities and powers. “If ease and sunshine come, it is well; but we are not to look for them” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “Peace: What it is”). See world.

  2. My peace: the peace of the Passion. Christ’s peace was His own: “My peace then is not exemption from any of these trials; and if you wish for one, you cannot have the other” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “Peace: What it is”). In the Garden and on the Cross, “see there the Peace which lasts on, not only when it is threatened without, but when it seems to have perished within” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace”). “It is there because trust is there” (same). “When He rose, the pledges of it were His wounded hands and side. They are always the pledges of it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “Peace: What it is”). See sacrifice and faith.

  3. The Lord of Peace is the Lord of Hosts. The heathen had a God of War and longed for a Lord of Peace; the Jew was taught “that the Lord his God was one Lord, that the God of armies was the same as the Lord of Peace. The acts of His Power were the manifestations of His Righteous Will” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace”). Because He is merciful He is pledged to destroy evil. See righteousness and unity.

  4. Given, not purchased. “You do not purchase it by prayers or faith or good deeds. Your prayers, if they are true prayers, are acts of submission and surrender to Him” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace”). No preacher can give it: “He gives you the thing itself; we present you with the seals and sacraments of it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace”). See sacrament.

  5. Peace itself, in its full meaning. It is “not a peace which depends upon pacts and bargains among men, but which belongs to the very nature and character and being of God. Not a peace which is produced by the stifling and suppression of activities and energies, but the peace in which all activities and energies are perfected and harmonized. Not a peace which comes from the toleration of what is base or false, but which demands its destruction” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace”). See love and constitution.

  6. Peace in one body; peace-makers. Christ is the peace-offering for mankind, who has broken down the middle wall of partition (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII). So “we are called to Peace in one body, and therefore that we may let that Peace, the Peace of Christ, rule in all our hearts” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “The Faith of Catholics”). We are to desire it “in every place, that in every place we may be peace-makers, and so may be called the children of Him who is the Great Peacemaker” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace”). See Atonement, Church and Eucharist.

Neighbouring words

  • reconciliation · atonement (ground). Peace made between God and man, and man and man.
  • ease · quietness (false neighbours). What the world gives and Christ does not promise.
  • sword · division (the paradox). The Prince of Peace came to divide.
  • righteousness · joy (companions). The kingdom is righteousness, peace and joy.
  • trust (root). Peace is present wherever the sufferer still trusts.

Scripture. Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you (John 14:27). The Lord of peace himself give you peace always by all means (2 Thessalonians 3:16). He is our peace, who hath made both one (Ephesians 2:14). Blessed are the peacemakers (Matthew 5:9).
