A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

conscience

Frequency in the corpus: conscience and consciences 631.

In brief. The faculty in man that answers to God. Maurice denies that it is “a power of its own”: it is a witness to Someone who speaks, commands and judges. The conscience tells each man that he is a person and responsible; it protests against his self-will; and it is what revelation addresses. It can be burdened, fettered or awakened, and Christ’s sacrifice is said to purify it. Maurice also appeals often to “the conscience of mankind” as a common court of judgment.

Shades of meaning

  1. A witness, not a power of its own. Maurice tells his hearers how they “were driven at last by facts, and in defiance of the speculations of eminent men, men worthy of all reverence, to think that the conscience is not a power of its own, but is a witness to us of some One speaking to us, commanding us, judging us” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). See witness.

  2. The sense of being a person. “The conscience is that which tells each man he is a person, making him feel that which he has done in past time to be his own, giving him an awful assurance of identity, responsibility, permanence” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Eucharist”). This was Protestantism’s truth, and the Reformers sought above all “The emancipation of the conscience” (same).

  3. What revelation addresses. “That all revelation is to the conscience, to the inner man, and that when that conscience is not awake, when that inner man is buried, the revelation is not really made, most persons, under some form of language or other, are ready to confess” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Pure Protestantism”). See revelation.

  4. Related to heaven. On Hebrews 9:14: “The conscience is that thing which is set in contrast with the flesh. This is that in man which is related to heaven, to the invisible, to God the Judge of all” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XI). The blood of Christ purges your conscience from dead works to serve the living God. See sacrifice.

  5. Protest against self-will. The conscience says what a man is meant to be: “his Conscience is continually protesting against his selfwill” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Pure Theocratist”). Against the selfish man one may appeal “Because it is a course of selfishness, and because you were not meant, and your conscience tells you you were not meant, to be selfish” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Pure Protestantism”). See self-will.

  6. Its witness to union. The sense of guilt is itself evidence of a bond: “your conscience tells you that you are wrong for being separate from God, seeing that your right and proper state is one of union with Him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVI). See member · union.

  7. The conscience of mankind. Maurice appeals to a common moral sense against theories that offend it: “I appeal to the conscience of mankind against this language” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”). On self-sacrifice as ordinary duty, “surely the conscience of mankind confirms his judgment, however the pride of men may rebel against it” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIII). See Atonement, sense 4, where the popular theory is tested by the same court.

Neighbouring words

  • witness (what it is). Sense 1; see witness.
  • person, responsibility (what it tells a man). Sense 2.
  • duty (its demand). The lecture moves from duty to conscience (sense 1).
  • revelation (what speaks to it). Sense 3.
  • flesh (its contrast). Sense 4.
  • self-will (what it protests against). Sense 5.
  • judgment (its office). God is “the judge Himself” in the conscience; see judgment, sense 6.

Scripture. Hebrews 9:14, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God (sense 4); Romans 2:15, their conscience also bearing witness, behind sense 1; 2 Corinthians 4:2, commending ourselves to every man’s conscience in the sight of God (sense 7).
