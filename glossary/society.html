A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

society

social · universal society

Frequency in the corpus: society 885; societies 72; social 135. The key sources are The Kingdom of Christ, especially I, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System” and “Signs of a Spiritual Society”, and the Lincoln’s Inn sermons on national life.

In brief. The common life of men, which Maurice treats as a reality given by God and not a contract or construction. Society has its ground in God’s righteous nature and is held together by sacrifice; it cannot rest on opinions, on interest or on individual rights. Men feel the need of a universal society, and every attempt to construct one by will has failed; the true universal society is the Church, constituted in the Person of Christ, which expands family and nation instead of destroying them. Against it stands the world, the society bound together by each man’s selfishness.

Shades of meaning

  1. A reality, not a construction. Of the reformer who has grown wiser since the revolutionary years: “He has been convinced that society is a reality, that it would not become at all more real by being unmade and reconstructed, and therefore he has begun to inquire what are the grounds of its reality” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Dedication”). See constitution · order.

  2. Grounded in God’s nature. We must tell the people “that society is not a combination of mutual fears and hatreds; that it has its ground in His Nature, Who is absolutely Righteous, in Whom is no darkness” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “National Holiness”). It needs “an invisible spiritual reality; not any scheme or theory” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System”) for its ground. See ground · root · foundation, sense 6.

  3. Held together by sacrifice. Sacrifice “lies at the very root of our being; that our lives stand upon it; that society is held together by it” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon III). See sacrifice.

  4. Not founded on rights or will. The French Revolution ended by “laying the foundation of society in a declaration of individual rights” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Political Movements”). Its lesson is “that every modern attempt to construct a universal society had been defeated by the determination of men to assert their own wills” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System”). See self-will.

  5. A universal society needful to man. The modern schemes are each “the shrine of a feeling which it cannot satisfy, and of a conviction which it labours to stifle—the feeling, I mean, that a universal society is needful to man” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Political Movements”). Such a society “must be involved in the very idea of our human constitution, say rather, must be that constitution, by virtue of which we realize that there is a humanity” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System”). See mankind and Catholic.

  6. A society for men as spirits. Paul “discovered that there is a human society—a society for men as men—and that the foundation of this society must be spiritual; that men become members of it, not because they have bodies, but because they have spirits” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “Earth and Heaven”). See Spirit and Church.

  7. The Church and the world as two societies. The Christian assertion “rests upon another, that there is a society for mankind which is constituted and held together in that Person, and that he who enters this society is emancipated from the World—the society which is bound together in the acknowledgment of, and subjection to, the evil selfish tendencies of each man’s nature” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”). See world and Head.

  8. Universal and national together. Maurice “maintained that there is a spiritual and universal society in the world: that there are also national societies in the world: that the Universal Society and the National Society cannot, according to the scheme of Providence, be separated from each other” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The English Church and the Systems Which Divide It”). “I do not think national society is a lie or stands upon a lie” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “On the Relations of the Church with National Bodies”). See nation.

Neighbouring words

  • constitution, order (its shape). Senses 1 and 5; see constitution · order.
  • Church, kingdom (the universal society). Senses 6 and 7.
  • world (the counterfeit society). Sense 7; see world.
  • nation, family (its particular forms). Sense 8.
  • fellowship, communion (its inward life). See fellowship · communion.
  • rights, interest, contract (false foundations). Sense 4.
  • humanity, mankind (what it makes real). Sense 5.

Scripture. Ephesians 2:19, ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God (senses 6 and 7); Ephesians 4:16, the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth; Acts 17:26, hath made of one blood all nations of men (sense 8); Hebrews 11:10, a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God (sense 2).
