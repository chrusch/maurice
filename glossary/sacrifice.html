A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

sacrifice

self-sacrifice

Frequency in the corpus: sacrifice 1,463; sacrifices 303; self-sacrifice 52.

In brief. The giving up of oneself to God, which Maurice finds at the root of all true life: in the Son’s eternal submission to the Father, in Christ’s death, and in every right human act. True sacrifice comes from God and expresses his will; false sacrifice is man’s attempt to buy God off. The Doctrine of Sacrifice (1854) traces the word through the Bible, and nearly every other doctrinal term in Maurice is touched by it.

Shades of meaning

  1. The meaning of the whole Bible. “The Bible is, from first to last, setting forth to us the meaning of Sacrifice. If we cannot preach that that meaning has been accomplished, that the perfect Sacrifice has been made for the sins of the whole world, that God has made peace with us by the death of His Son, I do not see that we have any gospel from God to men” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter).

  2. Two contrary sacrifices. The governing distinction: “the sacrifice which manifests the mind of God,—which proceeds from God, which accomplishes the purposes of God in the redemption and reconciliation of His creatures, which enables those creatures to become like their Father in Heaven by offering up themselves;—and the sacrifices which men have dreamed of in one country or another, as means of changing the purposes of God” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). The second kind he calls “a Cain sacrifice; there is no submission in it, no trust in it” (Sermon II, “Noah’s Sacrifice”).

  3. Surrender, from God’s side first. True sacrifice originates in God: “God is the Author of every true sacrifice; that it originates in His will, and therefore fulfils His will” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII). Man’s part is the answer of surrender: “he comes to make surrender, he brings the sacrifice which is the expression of his surrender” (Sermon II).

  4. The ground of the universe, in the divine nature. Sacrifice belongs to God’s own being: the apostles “lead us back to a ground of sacrifice in the divine nature; in that submission of the Son to the Father, that perfect unity of Purpose, Will, Substance, between them” (Sermon VII, “The Lamb Before the Foundation of the World”). Christ’s death shows “that entire filial union with the Eternal Father, which is the ground of the universe and the ground of our humanity” (Sermon VII).

  5. The root of human life and society. What Abraham learnt: sacrifice is “no one solitary act” but “lies at the very root of our being; that our lives stand upon it; that society is held together by it; that all power to be right, and to do right, begins with the offering up of ourselves” (Sermon III, “The Sacrifice of Abraham”). In the family: “sacrifice is the only bond which can keep fathers and children, husbands and wives, brothers and sisters, at one” (Sermon VII).

  6. The ground of morality. Morality “consists, as I believe, in the giving up of ourselves. All immorality consists in self-seeking, self-pleasing, self-glorifying” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter).

  7. The law of sacrifice, embodied in a Person. Paul “treats that fact as the manifestation of an eternal law of Sacrifice; of a Person in whom that law was perfectly realised” (Sermon VIII, “Christ’s Sacrifice a Redemption”).

  8. Propitiation set forth by God. Maurice keeps the word propitiation but reverses its direction: the heathen notion seeks “something to make God propitious” (Sermon X, “The Sacrifice of Christ a Propitiation”); Paul’s propitiation is one God “hath set forth” to declare his righteousness, and the opposite theory, of “a propitiation, not set forth by God, but devised to influence His mind”, is the one he rejects (Sermon X).

  9. Self-sacrifice against self-will: the question of power. In the Apocalypse lectures it becomes the question of history: “Is the root of power, of that power which actually does rule the world, of that power which all shall one day confess, self-will or self-sacrifice?” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture VI). The Lamb that was slain is “The discovery that the highest Godhead is manifested in self-sacrifice” (Lecture V).

  10. The baptized as sacrificed. Sacrifice reaches each Christian through baptism: “then and there we were sacrificed to Him and signed with the sign of sacrifice” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IV, “Sacrifice of the Passover”).

Neighbouring words

  • self-will (opposite). Sense 9. Self-will is the root of sin; sacrifice its undoing. See self-will.
  • obedience, submission, surrender (its inward form). Senses 3 and 4.
  • propitiation, atonement, reconciliation (doctrines it governs). Sense 8; see atonement.
  • Lamb (its figure). The title of Sermon VII, from 1 Peter 1:19–20 and Revelation 13:8.
  • Eucharist (its continuing sign). See Eucharist.
  • baptism (its first sign). Sense 10.
  • morality (its consequence). Sense 6.
  • king (its paradox). Only the one who is the sacrifice can be King: the Jews did not “know why only such an one could be the King, because only such an one could be the sacrifice” (Sermon VI, “David’s Sacrifice”).

Scripture. 1 Peter 1:19–20, the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb … who verily was foreordained before the foundation of the world (KJV); Psalm 51:17, The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; Romans 3:25, Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation, the text of Sermon X.
