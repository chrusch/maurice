A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

evil spirit

devil · Accuser · spirit of selfishness

Frequency in the corpus: devil, evil spirit, Satan, Tempter and Accuser in about 450 paragraphs. The essay “On the Evil Spirit” in Theological Essays, Sermon XV of The Doctrine of Sacrifice, “Christ’s Death a Victory over the Devil”, and Lecture XII of The Epistles of St. John, “The Devil and His Works”, are the main sources.

In brief. Maurice holds the doctrine of an Evil Spirit openly, against Unitarian scorn, because he finds it explains experience better than the alternatives: that evil comes from circumstances, or that human nature is itself depraved. The Devil is above all the Accuser or Slanderer, who whispers against God, against our brethren and against ourselves. He is the spirit of selfishness, tempting each man to make himself his own centre. He does not force anyone to sin. Belief in him keeps sin in the will and keeps God wholly good. Christ has answered his accusations by his death.

Shades of meaning

  1. Characteristic of the Gospels. “The acknowledgment of an Evil Spirit is characteristic of Christianity” (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”). There “first the idea of a spirit directly and absolutely opposed to the Father of Lights, to the God of absolute goodness and love, bursts full upon us. There first we are taught that it is not merely something in peculiarly evil men which is contending against the good and the true” (same). The Devil is spoken of more in any single Gospel than in the whole Old Testament (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XII).

  2. Not a rival creator. In the Gospels “the belief that Evil may be a rival creator, or entitled to some worship” is put to flight; instead comes “the vision of a mere destroyer, a subverter of order, who is seeking continually to make us disbelieve in the Creator, to forsake the order that we are in” (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”). Sin is departure from a law that came first: “Whoever sinned first, confessed Good to be” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XII). See constitution · order.

  3. The Accuser. “The Accuser, or slanderer of God and of the brethren, is the title which appertains to this passage” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV). “This spirit is the slanderer and accuser, not only of our brethren, not only of ourselves, but of God” (same). Maurice speaks from experience: “Now there come to me every day whispers not received through the ear but heard in the heart, that God is not the Being whom Jesus Christ manifested” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XII).

  4. The spirit of selfishness. By “setting forth the Spirit of Selfishness as the enemy of man”, the Gospel explains wherein pravity consists: “that it is the inclination of every man to set up himself, to become his own law and his own centre” (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”). Christ in his life “was contesting with the Spirit of selfishness, He was redeeming our nature from its tyranny” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). See self-will.

  5. Against depravity and circumstance. The Gospel concedes depravity as a fact of experience, but “the horrible notion, which has haunted moralists, divines, and practical men, that pravity is the law of our being, and not the perpetual tendency to struggle against the law of our being, it discards and anathematises” (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”). The reformer who blames circumstances must be told “that the Spirit of Selfishness is the common enemy, and that he has been overcome” (same). See sin, sense 7.

  6. A Spirit speaking to my spirit. “I believe it is the spirit within me which feels the inclination; I believe it is a Spirit speaking to my spirit, who stirs up the inclination. That old way of stating the case explains the facts, and commends itself to my reason” (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”). “I cannot believe in a Spirit which is tempting me into falsehood and evil, without believing that God is a Spirit” (same). See Spirit.

  7. Tempter, not compeller. “St. John never for a moment says or dreams that he who commits sin commits it because the devil or the accuser obliges him to do it” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XII). The sin “must belong to the man himself” (same). See conscience and will.

  8. The witness against a divided God. The old divines, believing “that an Evil Spirit was drawing them away both from mercy and righteousness”, had “a practical Witness against” the modern notion of a war in God between Justice and Mercy (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”). When men stop saying “We are engaged in a warfare with an Evil Spirit”, they talk instead of the depravity of nature, and harsher theories follow (same). See Atonement.

  9. Overcome by the Son. That the Son of God was manifested to destroy the works of the devil “is the characteristically Gospel or New Testament doctrine” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XII). “The accuser of the brethren is no longer able to tear the creature from the Creator. They are made one in the well-beloved Son” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XII). See Deliverer and justification.

Neighbouring words

  • Tempter, Adversary, Destroyer (his other titles). Maurice asks that they not be “mixed carelessly together” with Accuser, since each names a different characteristic (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV).
  • father of lies (his falsehood). Senses 3 and 6.
  • selfishness, self-will (his spirit in us). Sense 4.
  • depravity, pravity (the rival explanation). Sense 5.
  • circumstances (the other rival). Sense 5.
  • Advocate, Justifier (his answer). Sense 9; see justification.
  • death (his weapon). See death · eternal death.

Scripture. 1 John 3:8, For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil (senses 1 and 9); Revelation 12:10, the accuser of our brethren is cast down (senses 3 and 9); Hebrews 2:14, that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil (sense 9); Matthew 4:1–11, the temptation (sense 7); Ephesians 6:12, we wrestle not against flesh and blood (sense 6).
