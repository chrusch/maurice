A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

sign

signs · token · sign and pledge

Frequency in the corpus: sign 435; signs 386; token and tokens 162; sign and pledge 13.

In brief. A thing or act that shows a fact which already holds and does not depend on the person who sees it. Maurice uses the word for Christ’s miracles, for the sacraments and other ordinances of the Church, and for events in history. A sign never creates what it signifies, and it does not merely stand for an idea: it testifies to a present reality, above all to a Person.

Shades of meaning

  1. Signs of the kingdom. Christ’s works show what kind of kingdom has come. Of the stilling of the storm: “He has given this sign and proof of His dominion” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture VII, “Powers and Signs of the Kingdom of Heaven”). Of a healing on the sabbath: “Here is surely a sign of the Kingdom of Heaven; a sign of its nature; a sign that it was the fulfilment of the Law” (Lecture VIII). See kingdom, sense 1.

  2. Signs of a spiritual society. In The Kingdom of Christ, the ordinances by which the Church can be found in the world: Baptism, the Creeds, forms of worship, the Eucharist, the ministry, the Scriptures. Of Baptism, the writers of the Gospels and Acts “believed this to be the sign of admission into Christ’s spiritual and universal kingdom” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”). The dispute with the Baptists “is not whether the existence of such and such a sign indicates the existence of a kingdom, but what that kingdom is which it should indicate” (same chapter).

  3. Sign and pledge. The sacraments as assurance of a union already made. Of the first Eucharists: “That was the sign and pledge that they were one with Him, though He was gone out of their sight” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXII, “Ministers of Christ”). The wedding feast says the same: “Receive these tokens that the marriage is not to be made, but that it is made” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, VII, “The Marriage Feast of the King’s Son”).

  4. Not dependent on the receiver. A sign does not wait for the receiver’s experience. Maurice faults the Anabaptist view that “each individual ought to be conscious of his own adoption into God’s covenant, before he received the sign of it” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. II, “Pure Protestantism”). See baptism.

  5. Signs of a Person. Signs are better than abstract words because they point to someone present. “If the Revelation is indeed to be of a Person, may not the signs denote his actual presence, while abstract words would only express some notions about him?” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture I). The Catechism’s phrase is taken in this way: “how outward visible signs may betoken an inward spiritual grace” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII).

  6. Signs in history. Events, too, are signs of God’s government, chiefly the fall of Jerusalem, which Maurice reads as the sign that the kingdom had come; Lecture XXV of The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven is titled “The Fall of the Temple the Sign That the Kingdom of Heaven Was at Hand”. In the Apocalypse lectures Jerusalem was to be “a sign and witness of the evil that is possible upon the earth, which is only possible when it is the corruption of the highest good” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XI).

Neighbouring words

  • pledge, token (near-synonyms). Pledge adds assurance for the future; token is the homelier word of the country sermons (sense 3).
  • witness (what a sign does). The pairing sign and witness (sense 6); the Church’s signs are its witness (see Church, sense 5).
  • sacrament (the chief kind). See baptism, Eucharist.
  • fact (what it shows). A sign shows a fact, not an opinion (sense 4).
  • notion, abstraction (opposite). Sense 5.
  • miracle, power (the signs of the Gospels). Sense 1.

Scripture. Luke 11:29, they seek a sign; and there shall no sign be given it, but the sign of Jonas the prophet, discussed in Lecture XV of The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven; Luke 21:25–32, there shall be signs in the sun, and in the moon, behind sense 6; John 2:11, This beginning of miracles (RV signs).
