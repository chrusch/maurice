A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

covenant

new covenant · Covenant of Promise

Frequency in the corpus: covenant and covenants 285. The main sources are The Doctrine of Sacrifice* and the country sermons “The New Covenant” and “God’s Covenant with the Nations”.*

In brief. A covenant is God’s act of binding a people to himself and declaring himself to them. It comes first; law and sacrifice follow and witness to it. God’s covenant with Abraham was a blessing for all nations, not a set of commands, and the curse of the law came later. The new covenant, sealed in the blood of Christ, is God’s promise to write his laws in men’s hearts by his Spirit; it is the covenant of baptism, made with the whole race in the Son. Nations, including England, stand under God’s covenant and fall when they forsake it for gods they can see.

Shades of meaning

  1. A blessing before law. “Was Abraham under a Law? No! he was under a blessing. The covenant with him did not mean, if Moses spoke truly, that he was commanded to do certain things, and to leave certain things undone. It meant that the Lord of all declared Himself to him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IX). St. Paul “insists upon the priority of the Covenant to the Law, of the blessing to the curse” (same). See law.

  2. The ground of the nation’s life. “Before he could understand anything of its meaning”, the Israelite child “was at once adopted into the covenant; he came in under this law of sacrifice” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IV). The people “may know that they are one, because He who has called them into covenant with Him is One” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon V). See nation and unity.

  3. Covenant and sacrifice. “It is assumed, therefore, that the nation is holy, and that God has claimed it as holy by taking it into covenant with Himself. The covenant cannot be separated from sacrifice” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VI). The sacrifices testify “that they are in that covenant, and that they have broken it” (same); to submit to God’s judging “was the man’s part of the covenant, was the sacrifice which God above all others demanded of him” (same). See sacrifice.

  4. The law in the heart. St. Paul “learnt was God’s new covenant or Testament” that God “does not merely give a law upon stones, but that He promises to write this law on our hearts, to put the love of it into them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, II, “The Gift of Hearing”). The preacher of it is “not sent to tell men of what they must do, but to tell them how they can do it” (same). See Spirit.

  5. The covenant of baptism. “Yes, my Friends, this is the new covenant—what we call the New Testament. It is the covenant of our Baptism. When you bring your children to be christened you claim this new covenant for them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXI, “The New Covenant”). God “has made this covenant with us in His Son who took the nature of us all, and died for us all, and rose again for us all” (same). See baptism.

  6. Sealed in the blood. At the Last Supper the disciples came to understand that Christ’s blood “was the seal of a new covenant between God and men that He would blot out their sins and give them a new Life” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXII, “The Last Supper”). “The New Covenant is a reality, and not a dream” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXI). See Eucharist.

  7. I will dwell in you. “Prophets and seers expected a covenant, the ground of which should be” God’s dwelling in men, and “this is the Covenant of which the Apostles and Evangelists believed themselves to be the heralds” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV). The new covenant is wider than the old, but “takes not less but more account of all the differences in men, of all the stages of their youth” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VII). See individuality in the MacDonald glossary.

  8. Nations under covenant. Jeremiah “could not tear himself from his nation. He was tearing himself from God if he did. God’s covenant was with Israel” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXVI, “God’s Covenant with the Nations”). For England: “That is the covenant which He has made with us in Christ. If we hold fast to that covenant, it will go well with us and with our seed after us” (same). See nation.

Neighbouring words

  • law, commandment (what follows it). Senses 1 and 4; see law.
  • blessing, promise (its content). Sense 1.
  • testament, constitution (its other renderings). Maurice uses “covenant, constitution or testament” for the Greek, “not meaning to determine which expresses best the sense of the original” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XI); see constitution · order.
  • sacrifice, blood (its seal). Senses 3 and 6.
  • baptism (its sign now). Sense 5.
  • circumcision (its old sign). The Galatians thought they “must have the sign of the covenant if they would have God’s favour” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XVIII, “The Glory of the Cross”).
  • nation, family (with whom it is made). Senses 2 and 8.

Scripture. Jeremiah 31:31–34 and Hebrews 8:10, I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts, the text of “The New Covenant” (senses 4 and 5); Galatians 3:17, the covenant, that was confirmed before of God in Christ, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul (sense 1); Psalm 50:5, those that have made a covenant with me by sacrifice (sense 3); Matthew 26:28, this is my blood of the new testament (sense 6); Jeremiah 22:8–9, the text of “God’s Covenant with the Nations” (sense 8).
