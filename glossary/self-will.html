A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

self-will

self-willed

Frequency in the corpus: self-will 93; self-willed 26; selfishness 182.

In brief. The root sin: the will that sets itself up as independent of the Father. Maurice opposes it to self-sacrifice as the two possible principles of power, and asks which one expresses the being of God. A self-willed God would be a false God; Christ reveals a God whose power is sacrifice. In men, self-will is the condition from which they need to be delivered, and the freedom of the will begins only there.

Shades of meaning

  1. The rival principle of power. “The question to be determined is this: Is the root of power, of that power which actually does rule the world, of that power which all shall one day confess, self-will or self-sacrifice? Does one of them or the other express the will and being of God?” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture VI). See sacrifice.

  2. A false God. Self-will cannot be ascribed to God. The doctrine of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world “saves us from the necessity and the horror of ascribing self-will to the Author of all” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII). The contents of St. John summarise a lecture on how the Apostles “regarded a Self-Willed, Independent God, as a false God” (The Epistles of St. John, Contents). See Father, sense 1.

  3. The sin of the world. “The sin of the world is its self-will, its self-gratification” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIV). Christ’s sacrifice works by “breaking down the barrier of self-will which made our world an exile from it” (same sermon).

  4. The tyrant’s temper. In rulers self-will is the claim of might against right: “Pharaoh was self-willed; he believed in might, not in right” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IV). The kingdoms of the Apocalypse are judged by it: Christ sets up “a kingdom of righteousness instead of a kingdom of self-will” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture V). See kingdom.

  5. Corrupted religion. Religion itself may serve it. Maurice warns against the tendency “to make Sacrifice itself the minister of man’s self-will, self-indulgence, self-glorification” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter).

  6. Bondage, not freedom. Self-will looks like independence but is slavery. The will “is only free when it is delivered from self-will” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). The boy’s “exhibitions of self-will and independence” are the first form of it (same lecture).

Neighbouring words

  • self-sacrifice, sacrifice (opposite). Sense 1; see sacrifice.
  • independence (its claim). Senses 2 and 6.
  • self-gratification, self-indulgence, self-glorification, selfishness (its forms). Senses 3 and 5.
  • might (its idea of power). Sense 4.
  • obedience, filial obedience (what replaces it). See Father, sense 1.
  • freedom (what it destroys). Sense 6.
  • world (its seat). Sense 3; see world.

Scripture. Revelation 13:8, the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world, behind sense 2; John 1:29, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world (sense 3); Exodus 5:2, Pharaoh’s Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice? (sense 4); Philippians 2:7, made himself of no reputation (RV emptied himself).
