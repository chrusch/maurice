A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

religion

our religion · the religious world

Frequency in the corpus: religion, religions and religious in several hundred paragraphs. The main sources are the Preface to The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “Christ, Not Christianity, the Deliverance of Mankind”, and the Dedicatory Letter to The Doctrine of Sacrifice.

In brief. Maurice is suspicious of the word. The Gospels do not announce a religion, and the word does not occur in them. They announce a King and a kingdom. “Our religion” is man’s conception of God and man’s way of carrying it out, and its history, taken by itself, is black. What saves it is a Christ ruling through it. A religion can be a scheme set against other schemes, a support to law for statesmen, a trading bargain, or a source of terror. The “religious world” is the counterfeit of the Church. Yet the religions of mankind express a real longing for fellowship with God, and the Bible gives a Gospel concerning God that meets it.

Shades of meaning

  1. The Gospels are not a religion. “But are the Gospels the announcement of a religion? Is that what they profess to be?” They profess to be “the account of One who was born King of the Jews”, whose “greatest opponents were those who professed a strict and exclusive religion” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). See kingdom.

  2. Man’s conception. “So far as Christianity is identical with “our religion,” so far as it is merely man’s conception about God and His Kingdom and His Church, and man’s attempt to realize these conceptions according to a method of his own, so far all is black and hopeless” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Christ, Not Christianity, the Deliverance of Mankind”). Hence the plea: “let us leave off thinking of Christ as the Founder of our Religion, lest we should think of Him as founding something which will perish when He appears” (same). See system.

  3. A Gospel, not a scheme. Protestants will be united again “whenever Protestants shall once more feel that they have a Gospel from God, and a Gospel concerning God,—not a scheme of religion to be set up against the scheme of religion which Romanists maintain” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). Missionaries go “to proclaim, not a religion of yours, not a master of yours, but the Son of Man and the Son of God” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Uniting Name”). See Protestant and Name.

  4. The religious world. “We shall be continually at war with the religious world, which is the counterfeit of the Church, and which is trying to reduce all Churches into a Babel of sects” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). There is “a fashionable world, a political world, an art world, a literary world, a religious world”, but the others “become, to all intents and purposes, worlds of fashion” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). See world and sect.

  5. Religion to keep order. There is a religion “which statesmen have asked for to keep the people in order; one which Churchmen have been able and willing to provide. But that it is the Religion—the Worship—which we read of in the Old and New Testaments, I think a person who is disposed to take them in their simple, literal signification cannot affirm” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Bible the Book of Freedom”). Statesmen learn “how inadequate religion is when it only assumes the character of a support to law” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Political Movements”). See freedom.

  6. A trading religion. When the things of the market master us, “we do not lose our religion, but it becomes a trading, bargaining religion” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXVIII, “The House of God”). In the same way, “we do not abandon our religion because we become sensualists; but our religion becomes one of terror and hatred” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Hungry Filled; the Rich Sent Away Empty”). Christ exposed “a scheme of religion which was grounded upon self-seeking and ended in self-seeking” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XX). See self-will.

  7. Heathen religion as thirst. “Heathenish does not mean irreligious; Heathens, in old times and now, have made religion a principal occupation of their lives. It means idolatrous religion; the worship of visible, or false or evil gods, the direction of the soul’s thirst to unworthy or unreal objects” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Thirst of Human Beings in All Ages”). “The religion of every people under heaven had meant a longing for intercourse or fellowship” with God (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). See idolatry and fellowship.

  8. Religion as sanctions. The popular view says: “There being a religion which men are required to accept, there must be sanctions to enforce it. They must consist of the most brilliant rewards for obedience, of the most tremendous punishments for disobedience” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). Maurice sets the Gospel of the Kingdom against this whole chain. See punishment and hell.

Neighbouring words

  • Christianity (its abstract name). Sense 2; Christ, not Christianity, delivers.
  • scheme, system (its human form). Senses 2 and 3; see system.
  • religious world (its social counterfeit). Sense 4; see world.
  • worship (what it should be). Sense 5; see worship.
  • Gospel, Kingdom (what the Bible gives instead). Senses 1 and 3; see kingdom.
  • heathenism, idolatry (its misdirected form). Sense 7.
  • sanctions, rewards and punishments (its machinery). Sense 8.

Scripture. James 1:27, Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this; Acts 17:22–23, ye are too superstitious … TO THE UNKNOWN GOD (sense 7); Mark 1:14–15, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God (sense 1); Matthew 21:12–13, the money-changers (sense 6).
