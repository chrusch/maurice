A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Son of Man

Frequency in the corpus: Son of Man 580; Son of God 566; Son of David 61.

In brief. The name Christ most often gives himself, which Maurice takes to declare him the Head and representative of mankind, not of one nation or of believers only. It answers what all nations had wanted, a ruler who is also one of them. Maurice nearly always pairs it with Son of God: the two names together say that God and man are united in one Person, and that men may see the Father in a man.

Shades of meaning

  1. Christ’s own name. “Our Lord Himself speaks much oftener of the coming of the Son of Man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Advent of Christ, and the Eucharist”). Before the Sanhedrin he will not use the name Christ: “He does not care to use that name before the Sanhedrim” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXVI, “The King in his Agony”), and answers instead with the Son of Man at the right hand of power.

  2. The desire of nations. What every people had sought: “Surely it was a Son of Man. It was One higher than men, Who could govern them. It was One acquainted with men, Who could feel as they felt, suffer as they suffered” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Advent of Christ, and the Eucharist”). “We want a Son of Man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Glory of the Chosen Nation Realized in the Illumination of the World”).

  3. The root of humanity. The source of all good in every man, Churchman or not: “there is a Divine root of humanity, a Son of Man, whence all the good in Churchman or man of the world, in believer or unbeliever, springs” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture X). See Head, sense 3.

  4. For all men, not one nation. The name breaks the national limits of Son of David. Those who called themselves Jews “would not believe that the Son of David could be the Son of Man” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture III); the Apostles’ battle was over “whether there was a Son of Man who might be announced by that name to Jews and Greeks, to Barbarians and Scythians, to bondsmen and freemen” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). The Church, in Maurice’s diagnosis, has believed in Christ as its own King but not “as a Son of Man—as the King of Men” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXII); see kingdom, sense 4.

  5. Son of Man and Son of God. The two names belong together, and denying their union was the Pharisees’ error: their principle “compelled them to reject the Son of Man; to deny that a Son of Man could be the Son of God” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture VIII). What the nations want is “a Christ, a Son of Man in Whom we may see the Father” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Glory of the Chosen Nation”). In the Atonement “the Son of God proves Himself to be the Son of man” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XII).

  6. The coming of the Son of Man. Maurice reads the coming foretold in the Gospels as fulfilled first in the judgment on Jerusalem: the Apostles “believed that the rejection of the Jewish people was the manifestation of the Son of Man; a witness to all nations who their King was” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture IX). He presses the question: “And did not the Son of man come as He says that He would in that generation?” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXV).

  7. In the midst of the Churches. In the Apocalypse the Son of Man walks among the candlesticks, and the Church lasts because of him: “The Church would last, even though particular Churches ceased to be; for the Son of Man was in the midst of it” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture II, “The Son of Man”).

Neighbouring words

  • Son of God (inseparable partner). Sense 5.
  • Son of David, Christ (narrower names). Sense 4 and sense 1.
  • Head, root (what the name declares). Sense 3; see Head.
  • King (his office). Sense 4; see kingdom.
  • brother, brethren (its consequence). The Son of Man is revealed “to those whom He is not ashamed to call His brethren” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXII).
  • humanity, mankind, race (what he represents). Senses 2 to 4.
  • Bridegroom (another name for the same relation). “The Bridegroom of Humanity had come forth” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture VII).

Scripture. Daniel 7:13–14, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven … and there was given him dominion; Luke 19:10, the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost; Matthew 24:30–34, this generation shall not pass (sense 6); Luke 22:69, Hereafter shall the Son of man sit on the right hand of the power of God (sense 1); Revelation 1:13 (sense 7); John 5:27, because he is the Son of man.
