A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

mankind

the race · humanity · the human race

Frequency in the corpus: mankind, humanity and the race in over 700 paragraphs. The Incarnation and Ascension essays in Theological Essays, the Lincoln’s Inn sermons and The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven are the main sources.

In brief. For Maurice, mankind is not a collection of individual souls from which some are to be saved. It is a race with a Head, and God redeems mankind, not certain elect souls out of mankind. Christ died and ascended for the race, and humanity is complete only in Him. So Maurice resists both the evangelical shrinking of redemption to the elect and the century’s worship of humanity, which raises man to the throne of heaven. The universal is seen in the individual, and every man is to be honoured as a member of the race that Christ has claimed.

Shades of meaning

  1. The race, not elect souls. Maurice distinguishes “the doctrine that God redeems mankind” from “the doctrine that He redeems certain elect souls out of mankind” (Theological Essays, “The Incarnation”). The Gnostic error was to believe Christ came to save certain elect souls “from the pollutions of the flesh and the death which was consequent upon them; not to save the human race” (Theological Essays, “The Incarnation”). See redemption and Head.

  2. For mankind, not for you. The believer is warned against a private gospel. “He has not died and ascended on high for you, but for mankind. He has not led your captivity captive, but the captivity of your race. He does not reign over you, nor even over your nation or your Church, but over all Powers in heaven and earth” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “Christ’s Death and Resurrection the Solution of the Problems of the Universe”). See Son of Man and kingdom.

  3. Humanity complete only in Christ. The Church is appointed “to testify of a Son of Man and a Son of God, to say that humanity is only complete in Him, to bid men claim the glory which He has claimed for them” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XVII). The plainest law of individual morality is bound up with “the great principle of human brotherhood”, and that is grounded “upon the divine and glorified humanity of Christ” (The Unity of the New Testament, “Epistle to the Philippians”). See Church and brotherhood.

  4. Against the worship of humanity. In “this century … in which humanity is becoming an object of worship” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture II), Mary’s song is a witness against “the disposition of humanity to exalt itself” and for “the redemption and glory of humanity in the person of its Lord” (same). God glorifies Himself “by raising to a divine life the humanity which, without Him, must sink into utter death” (same). See idolatry.

  5. The universal in the individual. The fulfilment of a prophecy to a few “is the sign and witness of the fulfilment of the prophecy to the whole nation, and through the nation to mankind. The universal is seen in the individual, according to the Scripture method” (The Unity of the New Testament, “St. Matthew”). Particular constitutions cannot be defended “unless we are willing to inquire whether there is a constitution for mankind” (Theological Essays, “On Regeneration”). See nation, constitution and society.

Neighbouring words

  • Head of the race (the ground). Christ, in whom the race is constituted.
  • family · universal family (form). Mankind as a family under one Father.
  • the elect (false limit). Redemption confined to souls taken out of mankind.
  • humanity-worship (corruption). The race exalted into the place of God.

Scripture. As in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive (1 Corinthians 15:22). God our Saviour; who will have all men to be saved (1 Timothy 2:3–4).
