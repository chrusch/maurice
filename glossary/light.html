A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

light

God is light · darkness · walking in the light

Frequency in the corpus: light in over 1,000 paragraphs; God is light and walking in the light or darkness in about 60. Lectures II and III of The Epistles of St. John are the main source.

In brief. God is light, and in him is no darkness at all is, for Maurice, the first proposition of Christian ethics. Light is the symbol of goodness, wisdom, justice and truth, and God, who is all of these, may be called by the one word. Light, unlike goodness, can never be held as a possession; we only walk in it and receive it. That is why St. John kept the living word. The heathen dream of a God of light was true, and Christ fulfilled it by showing the light in a person. Darkness is each man’s being shut up in himself. It is an utterly unsocial state, so walking in the light means fellowship with one another, and to deny our sin is to shut the light out.

Shades of meaning

  1. The first proposition. The divine nature is the foundation-stone of Christian ethics, and St. John’s message that God is light, with no darkness in him, “is the proposition from which all others start” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture II). This light “is not merely hidden inaccessible light; that it has shone out in a Person Who makes us understand what He is by doing human acts and bearing human sorrows” (same). See morality and manifestation.

  2. Goodness, wisdom, justice, truth. Light means “goodness, wisdom, justice, truth”, and darkness the opposite. Both heathens and Jews were taught “that Light, which is the great blessing of all in this creation of ours, is the symbol and witness of these, and that He, who is Himself Wisdom and Goodness and Justice and Truth, may be declared in one word to be Light” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). Men tend “to confine this Light by their own notions and imaginations; that is, in better and more intelligible language, by their own darkness” (same). See truth and righteousness.

  3. Never a possession. “A man may easily fancy that goodness, wisdom, truth, are possessions of his own”, held “just as he holds a freehold house or a purse of money. But you can never suppose that you hold light in this way. That I can never boast that I possess. When I go out and walk in the light, my eye receives it, my whole body is the better for it” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). See fellowship.

  4. The heathen dream fulfilled. St. John “did not think that these Ephesians had been wrong because they had dreamt of a God of Light. That was a true dream. Christ had come to fulfil it” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). Their error was “exalting the lower light above the higher light” (same). See idolatry, sense 1.

  5. Darkness is being shut up in oneself. “That darkness is in us, that it is possible to choose darkness rather than light—this every person can tell for himself.” “For this darkness comes to each man from his being shut up in himself, from his not seeking to get beyond himself. That is a thick darkness, a darkness which begins to be felt the moment a little light breaks in” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). See self-will and sin.

  6. Light is social. “The darkness of which St. John speaks is an utterly unsocial condition. A man thinks about himself, dwells in himself; the rest of the universe lies in shadow.” Such a man “has no fellowship with men, no fellowship with Nature. All the world is only a looking-glass which reflects himself” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). “The Light is all around us, while we are most dark” (same). See fellowship and brotherhood, sense 3.

  7. Shutting out the light. Fancying oneself without sin “is a proof that you are shutting out the light, for that would reveal to you your own inclination to fly from it and to choose the darkness” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). “We thrust away that Word which is shedding abroad His light in us; we bury ourselves in our own darkness” (same). See repentance and conscience.

  8. The Life that is the light of men. “The good is His light shining into men’s hearts; the evil has been the selfish darkness which has tried to extinguish it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Faith in the Trinity Not Faith in a Dogma”). Christ “is the Light of men on this side of death and on the other; but that there His Light will be confessed, not hidden” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XI). The judgment will reach all who have shared his light, “whether they have known His name or have never heard it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Judgment Day an Object of Intense Desire”). See Word and judgment.

  9. Light against darkness in history. “As the light is stronger and clearer, the darkness which it exposes must be thicker and deeper” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). The judgments of the Apocalypse “are verily for the separation of light from darkness, that the kingdom of light is to prevail for ever” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XV). A flash of truth in any man, though followed by darkness, “came from God; it belongs to God” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Eternal Not Opposed to the Present”). See history.

Neighbouring words

  • darkness (its opposite, self-enclosure). Senses 5 to 7.
  • fellowship (walking in it together). Sense 6.
  • symbol (light as the witness of goodness). Sense 2.
  • sun, moon (the lower lights). Sense 4.
  • Word, Life (its source). Sense 8; see Word and life.
  • confession (letting it in). Sense 7.

Scripture. 1 John 1:5–7, God is light, and in him is no darkness at all … if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another (senses 1 and 6); 1 John 1:8–10, If we say that we have no sin (sense 7); John 1:4–9, the life was the light of men … the true Light, which lighteth every man (sense 8); John 3:19, men loved darkness rather than light (sense 5); John 8:12, I am the light of the world (sense 8); Revelation 22:5, the Lord God giveth them light (sense 9).
