A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

manifestation

manifest · show forth

Frequency in the corpus: manifest and its verb forms 592; manifestation and manifestations 345. The main sources are The Epistles of St. John, The Doctrine of Sacrifice* and* The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven.

In brief. Maurice’s word, taken from St. John, for God making himself known in his Son. Christ’s manifestation does not change God’s mind or create his love; it shows forth what God eternally is, so that men may know him and have fellowship with his life. Every act of Christ is a manifestation, an epiphany; the Cross is the manifestation of God’s righteousness in forgiving sin; the coming of Christ at the end is the full manifestation that will make the sons of God what they are meant to be. It is close to revelation and unveiling, but stresses the showing forth of a Person.

Shades of meaning

  1. Showing forth, not creating. “What Christ did was not to create love in God, but to show it forth” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV), commenting on 1 John 4:9. See love and Atonement.

  2. The Life manifested. St. John’s language “about the Life and the manifestation of the Life” answers our questions: “The writer clearly means to tell us of a Person whom he has seen and handled, in whom he believes a divine and perfect life was shown forth, with which life, he says, he himself and those to whom he is writing, may have fellowship” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). Gospel preaching was “the announcement that Jesus Christ had manifested the Life of God, and had opened the kingdom of God to men” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV). See eternal and fellowship · communion.

  3. Answering the slanders. “The Son of God has been manifested to answer the accusations against God, which all men have heard and believed, which sorrow, sin, and death have appeared to confirm; He has manifested them to be slanders; He has shown forth God as He is; the Light in whom is no darkness” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XII). He has also been manifested “as the common Head of our race” (same), and to refute the accusations each man hears against himself. See Head and evil spirit.

  4. Sacrifice manifested. Christ was the Propitiation “because He was the manifestation of God’s righteousness in the forgiveness of sin” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVI). The Atonement “is the fulfilment and manifestation of His original purpose, when He created all things in Christ” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII). See sacrifice and righteousness.

  5. Epiphanies. “The Epiphany, or Manifestation of Christ to the wise men who came from the East, is the introduction to a number of Epiphanies or Manifestations which are commemorated in the Epistles and Gospels for these Sundays. All the acts of Christ upon earth … may be most fittingly contemplated under this aspect” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XI). In the Apocalypse his birth, Cross, Resurrection and Ascension “were steps in the manifestation of the true King and Conqueror of men” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XII). See king.

  6. Manifested in history. The fall of Jerusalem “was an unveiling of Jesus Christ—a clear manifestation of His dominion over men, of His presence in the midst of them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, IV, “Waiting for Christ”). The Apostles believed “that the rejection of the Jewish people was the manifestation of the Son of Man” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture IX). See judgment and revelation.

  7. The final appearing. Christ’s appearing “is the manifestation which is to make them what they are intended to be, by showing them what God is” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XI). St. John regards it “as not less certain than the appearing or manifestation of the sun in the morning” (same). Love casts out fear of it: there would be “no fear of any fuller manifestation of His glory; only a continual hope of it” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XV). See Judgment Day.

  8. The Light within and its manifestation. Maurice accepted the Quaker teaching on the Inward Light but rejected “that negative teaching which made the manifestation of this Light in the acts of the Son of God on earth and in heaven, of such small significance” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). The inward light and the historical manifestation belong together. See light.

Neighbouring words

  • revelation, unveiling, apocalypse (near synonyms). Senses 6 and 7; see revelation.
  • show forth, declare, exhibit (its verbs). Senses 1 and 4.
  • appearing, coming, Epiphany (its times). Senses 5 and 7.
  • Incarnation (its ground). Sense 2; see Incarnation.
  • Life, Light, Love (what is manifested). Senses 1 to 3 and 8.
  • Son of Man, Son of God (who is manifested). Senses 3 and 6.

Scripture. 1 John 1:2, For the life was manifested, and we have seen it (sense 2); 1 John 3:8, For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil (sense 3); 1 John 4:9, In this was manifested the love of God toward us (sense 1); 1 John 3:2, when he shall appear, we shall be like him (sense 7); Romans 8:19, the manifestation of the sons of God.
