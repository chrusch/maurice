A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

mercy

merciful · mercy and truth · the mercy-seat

Frequency in the corpus: mercy and merciful in about 240 paragraphs. The main source is the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men” on Proverbs 16:6, with “Theology and Morality”, “Excommunication” and Sermon X of The Doctrine of Sacrifice.

In brief. Maurice will not let mercy stand on one side of a balance with truth, justice or righteousness on the other. Solomon’s text, by mercy and truth iniquity is purged, assumes that the two work together and are both enemies of iniquity. Mercy as the Psalmists implored it is the mercy of being delivered from unrighteousness, asked of One who gives it because He is righteous; mercy that indulges sin they dared not ask for. So God, being merciful and gracious, will not clear the guilty, and much human mercy is cruel while severity may hide mercy. Christ showed mercy and truth united, divided only by the evil that would destroy both. The mercy-seat is God Himself, the Propitiator who puts away sin because He is righteous. Mercy in men is not a beautiful quality to admire but a power to love, given by the Spirit of mercy and forgiveness.

Shades of meaning

  1. Mercy and truth together. Solomon shows “no sense of a contradiction between these two qualities, no endeavour to show how they may be adjusted to each other. He assumes that they must work together, that one cannot exist without the other” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men”). “He does not say that Truth is to be tempered with Mercy, or that Mercy must not be carried too far lest it should interfere with Truth” (same); both “are equally enemies of iniquity; both are equally interested in its extirpation” (same). See truth and justice.

  2. Mercy as deliverance from unrighteousness. The Psalmists “implore Mercy; but they implore it of One Who they believe is willing to bestow it, because He is Righteous. They implore Mercy; but it is the Mercy of being delivered from unrighteousness; from lying lips and a deceitful tongue” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men”). “That view of Mercy in which it takes the form of indulgence of sins, tolerance of falsehood in others or themselves, they dare not cherish” (same). See righteousness and Deliverer.

  3. Merciful, therefore not clearing the guilty. “He was the Lord God, merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy; therefore would He not clear the guilty; therefore was all evil, everything unmerciful and oppressive, hateful in His eyes; therefore was He pledged to destroy it. There was no actual or implicit contradiction in His Nature” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Lord of Hosts and the Lord of Peace”). See punishment and peace.

  4. Cruel mercy, merciful severity. St. Paul’s handing over of the Corinthian offender was a duty owed to the offender himself. The maxim that a wrongdoer must taste the wormwood “points out how cruel much of our mercy is; what mercy may be at the bottom of severity. The maxim applies to our dealings with children and with wrongdoers. If we are to believe the Prophets, it applies to God’s dealings both with men and nations” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Excommunication”). See punishment and education.

  5. Mercy in jurisprudence and the nursery. Maurice preached at Lincoln’s Inn to lawyers, and his text raises “many opposing theories, in jurisprudence, education, theology” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men”). One school says criminals and children are reformed by mercy alone and arrives at the view “that the Divine character should be presented to men as one of pure unmixed mercy” (same); the other demands a stern law that executes itself, and sees in heaven only “a form of fixed eternal Justice and Right” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men”). He refuses both, and the compromise between them. See law and justice.

  6. Mercy and truth united in Christ. “Did any one see in Him that warfare of Truth with Mercy which we have so rashly dreamed of in the eternal Mind?” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men”). His warfare was “the warfare of Truth and Mercy against untruth and hardness of heart” (same), and “He showed that Mercy and Truth were divided only by the evil which seeks to destroy both” (same). Even Christ’s withering words to the Pharisees were mercy: “Was it merciful or unmerciful to remind him that the unseen God, the God of his fathers, demanded truth in the inward parts” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XV). See Son of Man and judgment.

  7. Not a sacrifice to one attribute. “We are not taught (in the Bible at least) that the Sacrifice of Christ was the sacrifice to one attribute for the sake of bringing it into agreement with another. We are taught that it was the sacrifice of a Son to a Father, Whose Will He perfectly delighted to do; Whose Will, because it was the true and merciful will, was perfectly manifested in the act of self-sacrifice” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men”). See sacrifice and Atonement.

  8. The mercy-seat. To the man burdened by his sin, what a dawn would come “if there was only a voice to say to him, God hath set forth His Son as a propitiation, and a mercy-seat, to declare His righteousness for the remission of the sins that are past” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon X): if God were declared “as the Propitiator—as the Being who puts away sins because He is righteous, because He would not have the man tied and yoked to evil” (same). In the forgiving of the sinful woman at Simon’s house he sees “the Epiphany of the God in whom there is mercy that He may be feared” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XI). See fear and redemption.

  9. Love mercy. Mercy shown in its fullest form is not necessarily loved; men may even prefer Barabbas. The theology of the Bible shows a Mercy with “a power of putting away sin, of covering it, extinguishing it—of transforming the creature who was the subject and slave of it, into a new creature who can love Mercy and do justly” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Theology and Morality”). The Kingdom belongs to “those who did not lift themselves above their fellows,——the meek, the merciful” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Sermon on the Mount: Its General Principle”). See forgiveness and kingdom.

  10. The death of mercy in ourselves. The real danger is “the second death of all trust, affection, sincerity, or, in Solomon’s words, of Mercy and Truth, in ourselves and in the world” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men”). It may come while our profession is fairest, when “we have been growing less merciful and less true, while we have been using seemly phrases and punctually fulfilling what are called religious duties” (same). See death and religion.

Neighbouring words

  • truth (its partner in Proverbs). Both enemies of iniquity; divided only by evil.
  • justice and righteousness (never its opposites). God is merciful because He is righteous.
  • indulgence and tolerance (its counterfeits). Mercy that leaves the sin is cruelty.
  • forgiveness (mercy in act). A power that puts away sin.
  • propitiation and mercy-seat (its sign in the sacrifice). God the Propitiator.
  • severity (sometimes its form). Mercy may lie at the bottom of severity.
  • compassion and sympathy (its human feeling). The merciful man still needs a standard.

Scripture. By mercy and truth iniquity is purged: and by the fear of the Lord men depart from evil (Proverbs 16:6). The Lord, The Lord God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth … and that will by no means clear the guilty (Exodus 34:6–7). Mercy and truth are met together (Psalm 85:10). Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood (Romans 3:25). Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy (Matthew 5:7).
