A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

grace

gracious · the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ · means of grace

Frequency in the corpus: grace and gracious in about 345 paragraphs. The main source is the farewell lecture of the Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII, “Parting Words”, on Revelation 22:21. The others are “The Eucharist Considered as Interpreting All Other Acts of Devotion” in the Lincoln’s Inn sermons and the country sermon “The Grace of God”.

In brief. Maurice refuses to let grace become a technical word. The grace we admire in a face, a movement or a character always points to the living person within, and it is human, not merely individual. The other common sense, a favour from a superior, cannot be separated from the first, for an unrighteous king cannot be gracious. A gracious government is one that produces in its subjects the quality that was first in the ruler, and that is what Scripture means by a kingdom of grace: a Ruler in whom all grace dwells, acting on men’s wills to make them gracious and free. So the gracious state is the human state, not an exception for a few. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ is a forgiving Mind acting continually on ours, which an unforgiving habit shuts out. It surrounded the heathen before any preacher came, it can be resisted, and yet the divine Will has resources to subdue the most untractable will.

Shades of meaning

  1. Not a technical word. “When you hear the word Grace pronounced in a pulpit, you conclude at once that it has a technical signification; you cast aside, or try to cast aside, the associations which you have with it at ordinary times. Do not make this effort, my friends” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). Grace in a stone or on the face of a corpse testifies of life: “You are sure that the grace which appears in them is in that which you cannot see; in the person who looks and who acts” (same). See Bible and sign.

  2. Human grace. The grace we delight in seems peculiar to a few, yet “Is it not a human grace which you recognise in them?” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). Dull sameness is not grace: “it is the opposite of grace, for it is an approach to death” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). Grace is a harmony in which those commend themselves most “who seem to understand most clearly what their work is; who devote themselves to that; who best appreciate other men’s capacities and callings” (same). See mankind and duty.

  3. Favour and righteousness. We also speak of grace “as bestowed by a superior on an inferior; we connect it with royal condescension” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). The two senses belong together, for a favour given from mere liking for one person over another is immoral: “An unrighteous king, we are sure, cannot be a gracious king” (same). St. Paul ascribes the preservation of the remnant to “the grace of that loving, gracious Being from whom all good in His creatures proceeds; not by some works or deserts of theirs” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The Epistle to the Romans”). See righteousness and covenant.

  4. The kingdom of grace. A perfectly gracious government would be “one which had the effect of producing the very quality in the subjects which existed first in the ruler” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). That is the reign of grace: “an actual Ruler, in whom all grace dwells, has taken us under His dominion, that He may act upon us to make us gracious” (same). “Are we not taught that His operations are upon our wills, to make them obedient, to make them free?” (same). See kingdom and freedom.

  5. The gracious state is the human state. Each man ought to know “that a gracious kingdom is compassing him round, that gracious powers are acting upon him from his birth onwards” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). “The gracious state is the human state, the selfish deathly state is the inhuman state” (same). The grace was with the heathen of Asia before any Gospel reached them: “The grace was there; the whole Creation would have withered if it had not been there” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). See regeneration and baptism.

  6. The whole name. The means of grace “are the gracious powers which proceed from Him, or they are nothing” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). The benediction “comprehends all that is contained in this Divine revelation” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII): a Father adopting men, sacrifice as the bond of divine and human, a society with Christ as head, and “distinct graces flowing from the one perfect fountain of grace, which shall enable each member to fulfil its place in the body” (same). See Church and sacrament.

  7. Grace and forgiveness. “We sometimes limit the idea of the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ to His Forgiveness” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Eucharist Considered as Interpreting All Other Acts of Devotion”). Yet the condition that we forgive is no limit on mercy: “if the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ is the continual influence and operation of a forgiving Mind upon ours, cannot we understand, do we not feel, that an unforgiving habit is precisely the thing which opposes itself to that operation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Eucharist Considered as Interpreting All Other Acts of Devotion”). And grace draws us past its gifts to their source, “that they may be drawn into an infinite region, that they may aspire after an infinite blessing” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Eucharist Considered as Interpreting All Other Acts of Devotion”). See forgiveness and prayer.

  8. Grace resisted and victorious. “We say, and say truly, that the grace of God is directed to the will of man; that it comes with no force like that which might move a stone; that it can, therefore, be resisted” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XII). But the sower also fits the ground, for “if our wills be ever so untractable, there are resources in the divine Will by which it can subdue them to itself” (same). “We shall not doubt that there is a grace coming forth from Him which can make even us willing in the day of His power” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXX, “The Grace of God”). See will and Catholic.

  9. Grace for all. The benediction is spoken to named churches, “But because it is so definite—because it is meant for creatures of our flesh and blood—it cannot be contracted to any region or any time” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). St. Peter’s faith “stands on no ground of superiority in us to other men; the moment we set up such a pretence the faith disappears” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXX, “The Grace of God”). Every baptism, marriage and grave repeats the blessing of grace and peace (same). See faith and mankind.

Neighbouring words

  • gracious and graciousness (the quality). In a ruler, the power to make others gracious.
  • favour (the debased sense). Grace given from liking for one over another, which is immoral.
  • graces (the distributed gifts). Many, from the one fountain, each fitting a member to its place.
  • means of grace (sacraments and ordinances). Nothing unless they are powers proceeding from Christ.
  • forgiveness (its nearest form). The operation of a forgiving Mind on ours.
  • peace (grace’s companion). Joined with it in the apostolic benediction.
  • gracefulness (the ordinary sense). Not to be cast aside in church; it points to the person within.

Scripture. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen (Revelation 22:21). Grace and peace be multiplied unto you through the knowledge of God, and of Jesus our Lord (2 Peter 1:2). For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men (Titus 2:11). And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us … full of grace and truth (John 1:14). But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound (Romans 5:20).
