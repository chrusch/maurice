A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

knowledge

knowledge of God · to know God

Frequency in the corpus: knowledge, know and knoweth in several thousand paragraphs; the knowledge of God and to know God in over 100. The central sources are the Theological Essays, especially the Concluding Essay, “On the Trinity in Unity” and the note on the Athanasian Creed, and The Epistles of St. John.

In brief. For Maurice the knowledge of God is eternal life, as Christ says (John 17:3), and eternal death is the loss of that knowledge. It is knowledge of a person, gained as one knows any person, by sympathy, fellowship and love. It is not knowledge about mysteries, possessed by a few. St. John ties it to keeping the commandments, so that it belongs to humble people and grows with obedience. Only the Spirit of God can give it. Reason cannot make its Maker, but by its own law must receive the knowledge of him. The knowledge of God and the knowledge of oneself go together, and every sign that men are seeking it is to be welcomed.

Shades of meaning

  1. Eternal life. The deepest theology before the Reformation bore witness “that eternal life is the knowledge of God, who is Love, and eternal death the loss of that knowledge” (Theological Essays, Concluding Essay). Eternal punishment may be “the punishment of being without the knowledge of God, who is love, and of Jesus Christ, who has manifested it” (same). He kept the Athanasian Creed “because I looked upon it as a witness that eternal life is the knowledge of God, and that eternal death is Atheism, the being without Him” (Theological Essays, “Note on the Athanasian Creed”). See eternal and death.

  2. Knowing a person. Eternal life is “the entering into His mind and character, the knowing Him as we only can know any person, by sympathy, fellowship, love” (Theological Essays, “On the Trinity in Unity”). To know Him is, in the Apostle’s words, “to know that love which passeth knowledge” (What Is Revelation?, Sermon VII). The Church’s knowledge is shared: “He that knoweth God knoweth us” meant that those weary of opinions and sects “and wanted a Living God” would find its doors open (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV). See person and fellowship.

  3. Not a knowledge of mysteries. Some in the early Church “exulted in their knowledge of mysteries, who identified this with the knowledge of God” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VI), and St. John dealt them hard blows “when he connected the knowledge of God with keeping the commandments”, so vindicating “this knowledge for humble people” (same). “Even in these little things obedience led to a deeper knowledge of God” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VII). See obedience and law.

  4. Given by the Spirit. “None can make us know God but the Spirit of God; even as none can know what is in a man but the spirit of man” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The First Epistle to the Corinthians”). The partial cannot comprehend the universal, “But is the knowledge of God therefore impossible?” (same). See Spirit and revelation.

  5. Given to reason, not made by it. From Coleridge he learned “that by the very law of the Reason the knowledge of God must be given to it; that the moment it attempts to create its Maker, it denies itself” (The Kingdom of Christ I, Dedication). The same teacher led him “to feel, that a knowledge of The Being is the object after which we are to strive, and that all pursuit of Unity without this is the pursuit of a phantom” (same). See Absolute and unity.

  6. Knowing God and oneself. Men want to learn “how they may know God, in whose image they are formed, and so know themselves; how they may worship God, and so not worship themselves” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Steps to Faith in the Holy Trinity”). The Evil Spirit exults in “all the noise and clamour which turn men away from the knowledge of themselves and the knowledge of God” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Christ the Purifier of Priests and Laymen”). See worship.

  7. In the face of Jesus Christ. St. Paul wants his readers to know that God is shining into their hearts “to give them the light of the knowledge of God in the face of Jesus Christ” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXIX, “The Eternal Weight of Glory”). The spirit “could not know God, it could not be released from the self-will which is contrary to God, if He did not meet it in sacrifice” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon X). See sacrifice and manifestation.

  8. Every seeker welcome. If there is a message from God to man, “then it seemed that we must rejoice in every indication which we found anywhere that men are seeking after the knowledge of God, and cannot be content without it” (What Is Revelation?, Letter V). The knowledge of God is what God formed “not sages or saints, but men” for (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Gospel of Hope”). See seeking.

Neighbouring words

  • eternal life (what it is). Sense 1; see eternal.
  • fellowship, sympathy, love (how it is had). Sense 2.
  • mysteries, gnosis (its counterfeit). Sense 3.
  • obedience, commandments (its path). Sense 3.
  • notions, conceptions (knowledge about God). See opinions · notions and Absolute.
  • self-knowledge (its companion). Sense 6.

Scripture. John 17:3, this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ (sense 1); 1 John 2:3–4, hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments (sense 3); 1 John 4:6–8, He that knoweth God heareth us … he that loveth not knoweth not God (sense 2); 1 Corinthians 2:11, the things of God knoweth no man, but the Spirit of God (sense 4); 2 Corinthians 4:6, the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ (sense 7); Ephesians 3:19, the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge (sense 2).
