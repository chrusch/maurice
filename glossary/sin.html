A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

sin

sins · sinner · sense of sin

Frequency in the corpus: sin and sins about 1,000 paragraphs. The essay “On Sin” in Theological Essays, Lecture III of The Epistles of St. John, and Sermon XII of The Doctrine of Sacrifice, “Christ Made Sin for Us”, are the main sources.

In brief. Sin is not first a crime against society or a bad habit of character. It is a wrong against a Being to whom one is bound, and it is known only when a man can say, Father, I have sinned against thee. Its essence is distrust: the suspicion of God, the denial that we are his children, the claim to be independent of him. So it is also separation from our fellows. Sin is unmanly and inhuman, not something proper to human nature. It is a contradiction, and no definition reaches it. Maurice refuses to make sin the starting point of theology. Christ, not Adam, is the ground, and the Deliverer is stronger than the sin.

Shades of meaning

  1. Against a Being. The man who has found that “Evil lies not in some accidents, but in me” has entered a narrow inward circle, and he can only emerge from it when he begins to confess that he has sinned “against some Being,—not against society merely, not against my own nature merely, but against another to whom I was bound.” The freeing is not complete until he can say, with the prodigal, Father, I have sinned against thee (Theological Essays, “On Sin”). See conscience and Father.

  2. No definition reaches it. “I am convinced that if there is one word of which a definition will tell us nothing, unless we have learnt the signification of it first in some other method, that word is ‘Sin.’” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). “All sin is contradiction; if you speak of it, you must denote it by words that cross and seem to confute each other” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XII). See system.

  3. Distrust. “By trusting Him we are delivered from the suspicion which is the very essence of sin” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). Death is horrible while it is linked “to that distrust of God which is Sin, and the root of all sins” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV). “The sin of all is the same—unbelief in God, distrust of His Goodness and Truth—that the remedy for sin is the same for all” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XLI, “Repentance and Conversion”). See faith.

  4. Denial of sonship. “The sin is the denial of this relationship; the setting up to be independent of Him. The truth which the sin rebels against is, first, the truth that is in God Himself; secondly, the truth that we are born of Him, that we are His children” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIX). “Sin is the very revolt of man’s will from the true will” (What Is Revelation?, Letter X). See self-will.

  5. What separates. “Sin is what separates us from each other. Sin is what prevents us from understanding each other. Sin is what every man hugs in himself, and which keeps him away from other people” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, VI, “The Sick of the Palsy”). The word “expresses the fact of our disposition to be false, and of our disposition to be at war with our fellow-creatures”, and we feel its full power only when we believe “that God has created us all to be at one” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture III). The sense of sin “is essentially the sense of solitude, isolation, distinct individual responsibility” (Theological Essays, “On Sin”). See unity and family.

  6. Unmanly, inhuman. “We must settle it in our hearts that Sin is the being unmanly, inhuman; that Holiness is the simple, natural, orderly condition of a creature living amidst creatures like himself” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Body and the Members”). Christ had no sin, and so he is the true man: “He would not be truly and perfectly a man if there had been” sin in him (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, VI, “The Sick of the Palsy”). See Son of Man.

  7. Not the starting point. Maurice laments the popular belief “that sin is the starting-point of our moral being, from which all the conditions and principles of it are to be deduced” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Sin of Man and the Justification of Man”). “We shall not put Adam in the place of Christ, or build our morality and divinity on the fall which proclaimed men sinners, when Christ’s obedience has proclaimed them righteous” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII). See Head.

  8. Made sin for us. Paul’s words that Christ was made sin are not to be softened into “Sin Offering”. Christ “may have entered into the sin of that poor man’s spirit, may have had the most inward realization of it, not because it was like what was in Himself, but because it was utterly and intensely unlike” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XII). See sacrifice and Atonement.

  9. A remitted race. Men think they belong to a guilty race but may get God to treat them differently and “procure a separate pardon.” Maurice answers: “No! that will not do. There is selfishness, there is separation from thy brother, there is the very essence of sin in that thought” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VII). The first lesson about God is “that He remits or sends away sins” (same). The forgiven publican could say, “He is my Lord; this sin is not my Lord, and shall not be” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XIV, “The Publican”). See absolution and justification.

Neighbouring words

  • crime, vice (its outer circles). Legislation deals with crimes and ethics with habits; sin lies in “a more inward circle” (Theological Essays, “On Sin”).
  • distrust, unbelief, suspicion (its essence). Sense 3.
  • self-will, independence (its form). Sense 4; see self-will.
  • separation, isolation (its effect). Sense 5.
  • death, evil spirit (its allies). See Deliverer, sense 5.
  • confession, repentance (its undoing). To confess sin is “nothing less than this disclaimer, this renunciation, of the sin” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIX).
  • holiness, righteousness (its opposites). Sense 6; see righteousness.
  • remission, forgiveness (God’s answer). Sense 9.

Scripture. Luke 15:18, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee (sense 1); Psalm 51:4, Against thee, thee only, have I sinned (senses 1 and 4); 1 John 1:7–9, the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin, the text of Lecture III (senses 2, 3 and 9); 1 John 3:4, sin is the transgression of the law, in the RV sin is lawlessness; 2 Corinthians 5:21, he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin (sense 8); 1 Corinthians 15:56, The sting of death is sin (sense 3).
