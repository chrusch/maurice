A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

kingdom

Kingdom of Heaven · Kingdom of God · Kingdom of Christ

Frequency in the corpus: kingdom 3,274; Kingdom of Heaven 346.

In brief. The actual government of God over men, revealed and set up in Christ, under which all men are already living whether they acknowledge it or not. It is Maurice’s name for the whole of what the Gospel announces. It is not chiefly a future state, an inner experience, or a religious society alongside other societies; it is the order of the world as God has constituted it, with a King at its centre.

Shades of meaning

  1. The subject of the Gospel. The kingdom is what the good news is about, and Christ’s acts are its signs: “The Kingdom of Heaven is the subject of the Gospel. The acts of Christ in healing the sick and casting out devils were signs of the Kingdom of Heaven” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture X). Christ came “to emancipate slaves from the yoke of a tyrant, not to discourse about a religion” (Lecture XXIV).

  2. A present and actual order. The commonest emphasis: the kingdom is here, set up, and men live under it now. “Once again may we be told that the Kingdom of Heaven is not to appear some day with observation, but is among us and in us, that we may submit to it and live in it; that it may subdue all kingdoms to itself!” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “How Sin Pays, and How God Gives”). The Apostles’ doctrine “has reference to an actual kingdom to which men were invited to belong, or of which they had already become members” (The Unity of the New Testament, Lecture II).

  3. A kingdom grounded in a person. The kingdom has its foundation in the union of God and man in Christ, not in any human association: “That Kingdom of which John spoke as at hand, is declared to have its foundation in a living Person. He who had been always ruling it, is now revealed” (The Unity of the New Testament, Lecture I). The word always matters: Christ does not begin to reign at the Incarnation but is revealed as the King he was.

  4. A kingdom for mankind. Universal in scope, so that the Church is the kingdom confessed rather than a part of mankind set apart: “the Catholic Church is emphatically a kingdom for mankind, a kingdom grounded upon the union which has been established in Christ between God and man” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”). The Church’s failure, in Maurice’s diagnosis, has been to believe in Christ “as its own King” but not “as a Son of Man—as the King of Men” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXII).

  5. A spiritual constitution. In The Kingdom of Christ, kingdom is close to constitution: the given order of man’s life, which the Bible records rather than invents. Maurice supposes “the Bible were the revelation of a spiritual kingdom or constitution of man” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Scriptures”).

  6. One kingdom, not two. Against the idea of a visible kingdom and an invisible real one, Maurice insists there is a single “heavenly, spiritual, real kingdom, against which all evil men, just in so far forth as they are evil, are rebelling; and into subjection to which all converted men, in so far forth as they are converted, are brought” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV). Sin is rebellion within the kingdom, not exclusion from it.

  7. The ground of civil order. The kingdom does not stand against earthly kingdoms but is their foundation: “There is no opposition between the Kingdom of Heaven and any kingdom of earth, except what is produced by this selfishness, which is the enemy of both” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture X). The laws of the Sermon on the Mount are therefore “principles which were the foundation of civil order and obedience” (same paragraph, Lecture X).

  8. The ground of preaching and conscience. Because the kingdom is real, the preacher can speak to every calling: “The belief in a Kingdom of Heaven established over men, of a King of Heaven who is ruling in the midst of them, must be the safest ground for all appeals to the consciences of men respecting their special callings and special transgressions” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Homily III: On Preaching to the Poor”).

  9. The Kingdom of Christ and the Kingdom of God. Usually the phrases are interchangeable. Reading 1 Corinthians 15, Maurice can distinguish them: “The vision of a kingdom of God, an eternal kingdom, for which the kingdom of Christ is a preparation,—the kingdom where principles are in conflict” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The First Epistle to the Corinthians”). Here the Kingdom of Christ is the present order of conflict and the Kingdom of God its consummation, in which the Son does not disappear.

Neighbouring words

  • King, Head, dominion (ground). The kingdom is defined by its King, and Head gives the same relation in the figure of the body: the nation rejected Christ “because He claimed to be the Head of all nations” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXII).
  • Church (the kingdom confessed). The Church is the kingdom confessed and made visible, its witness rather than its boundary (sense 4). See Church.
  • world (opposite within the same humanity). The world is mankind refusing the kingdom it lives under (sense 6), “the society which is bound together in the acknowledgment of, and subjection to, the evil selfish tendencies of each man’s nature” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV). See world.
  • constitution, order, society (near-synonyms). The vocabulary of The Kingdom of Christ, in which the kingdom is a given constitution (sense 5).
  • nation, family (earlier orders it completes). Maurice says the Church “grew out of a family and a nation, of which social states it proved itself to be the proper and only foundation” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV). See nation, family.
  • sign, sacrament, Baptism (instruments of declaration). The means by which the kingdom’s existence is declared to men: “I look upon Baptism as the first sign of the existence of a Catholic Church or Kingdom of Christ in the world” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV). See baptism, sign.
  • religion (opposite). The usual opposite: the kingdom is announced, a religion is discussed or invented (sense 1). See religion.
  • Son of Man (the King’s title). The title that makes the kingdom human and universal (sense 4). See Son of Man.

Scripture. Matthew 4:17, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand (KJV), is the form of words he quotes most often (thirty-two times in the corpus); Luke 17:20–21, The kingdom of God cometh not with observation … behold, the kingdom of God is within you (KJV; RV margin in the midst of you), lies behind sense 2, and Maurice’s among us and in us holds both readings together.
