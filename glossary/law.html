A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

law

laws · the law of love

Frequency in the corpus: law 1,592; laws 380.

In brief. Two things in Maurice. The first is the written, regulative law, the Ten Commandments and the Mosaic code. That law is good and divine, but it presumes separation between God and man and cannot give life. The second is the law of man’s being, which is love and self-sacrifice. That law is as real as gravitation, and Christ embodied it. The law of liberty is the law God enables men to keep.

Shades of meaning

  1. Grounded in deliverance. The Decalogue begins with a gospel: Maurice asks “How comes it that these words stand at the threshold of the Ten Commandments, the ground and sanction of them all, “I am the Lord thy God, Which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage”?” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Bible the Book of Freedom, Personal, National, Universal”).

  2. The law that presumes separation. The Mosaic law “presumes wrongdoing, the separation of those to whom it is sent from Him who has chosen them” (The Unity of the New Testament, “Epistle to the Galatians”). The written law is “a mere law—a regulative Revelation—which had never reached, which never could reach, the spirit of man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Christ Casting Out the Evil Spirit”).

  3. The schoolmaster. Its purpose is to lead to Christ: “The Law is our schoolmaster, to bring us ’to the knowledge of that Mediator in whom the promise was made” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IX). See Mediator.

  4. The law of humanity. Christ’s law is not new precepts but the law men were made for, “the law which His Father in heaven had formed all His human, intelligent voluntary creatures to obey, just as much as He had formed mere natural creatures to obey the law of gravitation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Law of Christ the Law of Humanity”).

  5. Love and self-sacrifice as law. That law is love: “Love is the law of the universe; that in loving, we do not perform an act of supererogation, but merely submit to a duty” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIV). It is equally self-sacrifice: “Self-Sacrifice is the law of the Universe” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Christian Services and Christian Rewards”). See love and sacrifice.

  6. The law of liberty. God’s law is liberty because he gives power to keep it: St. James calls it “a law of liberty. How is that? Because the God who commands us not to kill will enable us to overcome the desire to kill” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXIII, “The Law of Liberty”). Of a man who did what the voice within bade him: “He yielded to a law, but it was a law of love, a law of liberty, and in that law there was life” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XIV).

  7. The law of love that binds. At Christ’s coming it will be impossible “to escape from that divine law of love which binds man to man, which binds earth and heaven together” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Advent of Christ, and the Eucharist”).

Neighbouring words

  • covenant, promise (what it serves). Sense 2.
  • Gospel (its complement). The Reformers complained that “A mere law has been substituted for it. The Law shows us our evil. The Gospel shows us how God emancipates us from it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Christ the Purifier of Priests and Laymen”).
  • love, self-sacrifice (its content). Sense 5.
  • liberty (its true character). Sense 6.
  • curse (its effect without the Mediator). Sense 2.
  • righteousness (what it cannot give). See righteousness, sense 4.
  • self-will (what it resists). See self-will.

Scripture. Exodus 20:2, I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, sense 1; Galatians 3:19–24, it was added because of transgressions … the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, senses 2 and 3; Galatians 6:2, Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ, the text of “The Law of Christ the Law of Humanity”; James 2:12, So speak ye, and so do, as they that shall be judged by the law of liberty, the text of “The Law of Liberty”; Galatians 3:13, Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law.
