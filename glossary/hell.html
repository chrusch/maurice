A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

hell

the kingdom of Hell · death and hell · descended into hell

Frequency in the corpus: hell in about 150 paragraphs. The main sources are Theological Essays, especially “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”, the sermon “The Devil and God” and Lectures on the Apocalypse.

In brief. For Maurice, hell is not first a place of future torment but a state: the want of righteousness, truth and love, separation from God, the darkness of those who love darkness. Because Christ reveals heaven, He necessarily makes hell known as its opposite. The kingdom of hell is about us now, as the kingdom of heaven is, and each man at each moment may be earning the wages of its ruler. Christ descended into hell and rose from it; He has the keys of hell and of death, and in the end death and hell are cast into the lake of fire. On these words see also eternal and death.

Shades of meaning

  1. The want of righteousness, truth, love. Christ and His Apostles “present Righteousness, Love, Truth, to us as substantial realities, as the Nature of the Living and Eternal God” (Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”). “And since they reveal Heaven to us, they of necessity make known Hell also. The want of Righteousness, Truth, Love, the state which is contrary to these, is and must be Hell” (same). The habit of self-deceit leads “to that habit of falsehood, that state of falsehood, which is Hell” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 4, “The Book of Proverbs Illustrated by St. John”). See righteousness and truth.

  2. Hell is about thee. The Methodist preacher who roused the Kingswood collier did not move him by talk of future torment alone. He said, in effect, that the man was in a wrong state, that hell was about him, and that God would save him out of it (Theological Essays, “On Sin”). “He spoke of that which was true then, whatever might become true hereafter” (same). See sin and conscience.

  3. The kingdom of Hell about us. “The Kingdom of Hell is not far off, it is about us. Each one of us at each moment may be doing the work and earning the wages of its ruler. The Kingdom of Heaven is not far off, it is about us” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 1, “The Devil and God”). Preachers must be free to tell the poor “that the horror of the Kingdom of Hell consists in the absence of all knowledge of God’s Righteousness and Truth and Love” (What Is Revelation?, Sermon VI). See kingdom and evil spirit.

  4. Conquered by Christ. The Creed confesses a Son who “has been conceived, born, and died, and been buried, and gone down into hell, who has ascended” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System”). The excommunicated man is to feel his bondage so that he may turn and find “the emancipation which he came upon earth and went down into hell, to effect” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The First Epistle to the Corinthians”). The final judgment “reaches, so St. John says, into the very depth of hell. And life, he says, conquers. Death and hell are cast into the lake of fire” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XX). And “if death and hell are cast into the lake of fire, it is because He has done His work” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XX). See Deliverer and redemption.

  5. Not the ground of morality. Maurice refuses to base morality “upon the dread of some future punishments, upon the expectation of some future rewards. I believe the attempts to make men moral by such means have failed always” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). Hell is set before men “as the loss of this state, as separation from God, as the darkness into which those fall who love darkness rather than the light” (same). See morality and light.

Neighbouring words

  • eternal death (near synonym). Separation from the life of God, present and not only future.
  • heaven · kingdom of Heaven (opposite). Righteousness, peace and joy in the Holy Ghost.
  • darkness (image). The state of those who love darkness rather than light.
  • punishment (not the essence). Hell is the loss of good, not first a penalty.
  • lake of fire (the end). Where death and hell themselves are cast.

Scripture. I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death (Revelation 1:18). Death and hell were cast into the lake of fire (Revelation 20:14). Thou wilt not leave my soul in hell (Psalm 16:10).
