A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

redemption

redeem · Redeemer

Frequency in the corpus: redemption 311; redeem and its forms 384, including Redeemer 92. The central source is The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VIII, “Christ’s Sacrifice a Redemption”.

In brief. Redemption keeps for Maurice its simplest scriptural sense: deliverance out of a real bondage. God is the Redeemer; he has visited and redeemed his people, from Egypt and from the vain traditions, sins and false gods that enslave men. The sacrifice is the means God himself uses, not a price paid to change his mind; its costliness lay in the Son becoming the servant of all. Redemption is of mankind, not of a select few: God has redeemed mankind in his Son, and the Church exists to witness to it. It reaches the body and the whole creation.

Shades of meaning

  1. Deliverance from captivity. “When the Scriptures speak of Christ’s Sacrifice as a Redemption, you will find that they give the word its simplest and most natural force … God is assumed everywhere to be carrying out the deliverance of His people, who are, therefore, assumed, by some means, to have fallen into captivity. The sacrifice is a means to this end; the means which God uses; the power by which He ransoms the enslaved captive” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VIII). See sacrifice and Deliverer.

  2. God the Redeemer, not the oppressor. The heathen thought the oppression he felt came from the highest power, and sacrificed to be free of it. Maurice answers from the Old Testament: “the sacrifices were God’s sacrifices,—not merely in that they were offered to him, but in that He originated and prepared them” (same), and they commemorated “a redemption accomplished for them with a high hand, and a stretched-out arm, by the Lord God” (same). Israel’s calling “was the calling to confess a Redeemer of Israel, a righteous Being who had brought out their fathers from the house of bondage” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IV). See Atonement.

  3. An actual bondage. St. Peter’s thought is “You have been delivered out of an actual bondage, not an imaginary or a technical one” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VIII): bondage to vain traditions received from the fathers. “Remember, now and ever, that it is God Himself who has redeemed you out of bondage” (same). See freedom.

  4. The cost: becoming a servant. On a ransom for many: “We speak of the unspeakably costly ransom which Christ offered for the redemption of man, and we speak truly. He teaches us wherein the costliness of it consisted. He humbled Himself; He became a servant; He was the servant of all” (same). See ordination · episcopacy, sense 1.

  5. Of mankind. “I will exult in Him, because He has redeemed all mankind as well as me; because there is no creature in any corner of God’s wide universe whom He has been ashamed to call His brother” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XVIII, “The Glory of the Cross”). “If we think that we have some redemption or some victory over the grave, which does not belong to our whole race, we do not receive that Redemption and that victory through Jesus Christ our Lord. He is the Redeemer of Mankind” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “Christ the Lord of Life and the Conqueror of Death for All Times”). See mankind · the race and Head.

  6. What the Church witnesses. We must not prize the Church “because we suppose it is ours”, but as called “to be the witness of His revelation of Himself to mankind, of His redemption of mankind” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Glory of the Chosen Nation Realized in the Illumination of the World”). Missions fail where we have not made the heathen understand “that we believe He has united Himself to man, and has redeemed mankind in His Son” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Fatherly Character of God the Only Ground of Repentance, Consolation, Hope”). See Church and witness.

  7. Of the body and the creation. St. Paul “speaks of redemption, that is the forgiveness of sins; he speaks of a redemption of the body, which is burdened with the weight of sickness and death; he speaks of a redemption of the whole creation” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VIII). “The idea of a Redemption of Nature, as consequent upon a Redemption of Man, has often dawned upon the man of Science and upon the Artist” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “The Glory and Deliverance of the Animal Nature”). See nature and body · resurrection.

  8. Steps in Christ’s life. His birth, Cross, Passion and Resurrection “were steps in the redemption of humanity” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XII), and the Ascension was the highest of them. In the vision the Lamb is “the perfect Redeemer of the world from the dominion of false, hateful, cruel gods which they had imagined” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture V). See idolatry.

Neighbouring words

  • deliverance, Deliverer (its plain meaning). Senses 1 to 3; see Deliverer.
  • ransom (its price). Sense 4.
  • bondage, captivity, slavery (what it ends). Senses 1 and 3.
  • sacrifice (its means). Senses 1 and 2.
  • forgiveness, remission (one of its forms). Sense 7; Maurice wants these ideas kept distinct, warning against “a loose habit of confounding the ideas which these words express” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VIII).
  • mankind, humanity, the world (its scope). Senses 5 and 6.

Scripture. 1 Peter 1:18–19, ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold … but with the precious blood of Christ, the text of Sermon VIII (senses 1 and 3); Luke 1:68, Blessed be the Lord God of Israel; for he hath visited and redeemed his people (sense 1); Matthew 20:28, to give his life a ransom for many (sense 4); Titus 2:14 (sense 3); Romans 8:21–23, the redemption of our body (sense 7).
