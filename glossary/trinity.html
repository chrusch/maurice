A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Trinity

Trinity in Unity

Frequency in the corpus: Trinity 480, of which about a hundred are in Sunday names such as “Sunday after Trinity”.

In brief. The revealed name of God, Father, Son and Holy Ghost, and the ground of the Church and of humanity. For Maurice the Trinity is not an abstract dogma to be defended with proof-texts but a revelation of real Persons in real unity, made “substantially” in Christ. It is a mystery of love, and the foundation of every human unity. He holds it most firmly when he refuses to use it as a weapon.

Shades of meaning

  1. The ground of Church and humanity. “The Trinity is, as I believe, the ground on which the Church stands, and on which Humanity stands; Prayer and Sacrifice are, I believe, the means whereby the Trinity is made known to us: in the Trinity I find the love for which I have been seeking” (Theological Essays, “Eternal Life and Eternal Death”).

  2. Revealed, not deduced. “We say that in Christ the Trinity is revealed substantially. It is not a doctrine, unless it is more than a doctrine. Either real Persons are declared to us, or nothing is declared about those Persons” (Theological Essays, “On the Trinity in Unity”). He rejects the reduction to offices: the older Evangelicals said “Every true Christian … must needs recognise a Creator, a Redeemer, and a Sanctifier”, which led their sons to doubt “a distinction of persons” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Unitarianism”).

  3. A mystery of love. Belief in the Trinity makes “Prayer possible”, because “we are dwelling in a Mystery deeper than any of our plummets can fathom,—a Mystery of Love” (Theological Essays, “On the Trinity in Unity”). The kingdom rests on “a name which expresses the perfect Love, the ineffable Unity, the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “The Scriptural View of This Constitution”). See love.

  4. A foundation for all men. What he rests on he must offer to others: he “could not believe that a Trinity in Unity is a foundation for myself to rest upon, if I did not also regard it as a foundation for you and for all men” (Theological Essays, “On the Unity of the Church”).

  5. The uniting Name. The baptismal Name is “the very expression of all unity, which recognises and harmonises all partial conceptions, which is the spring of all free and practical life” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Uniting Name”). It makes the Sermon on the Mount possible, once a man knows “that the Father, whom he is to be like, has taken him into fellowship with Him; that the Son, in whom dwelt the perfect obedience, has made him a partaker of His nature; that the Spirit is with him” (same sermon). See Name.

  6. Faith, not dogma. Held as a weapon, the doctrine is lost: “While we use the doctrine of the Trinity in that way, I am certain we shall not believe it, whatever we may pretend” (Theological Essays, “On the Trinity in Unity”). He asks whether “the mystery of the Trinity in Unity … is not that true faith which justifies all that is true in” pantheism and “eliminates what is immoral and dangerous” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Faith in the Trinity Not Faith in a Dogma”).

Neighbouring words

  • Name (its revealed form). Sense 5; see Name.
  • Unity (its other half). Senses 3 and 4.
  • Father, Son, Spirit (the Persons). See Father and Spirit.
  • dogma, formula, notion (what it is not). Senses 2 and 6.
  • offices: Creator, Redeemer, Sanctifier (the reduced version). Sense 2.
  • Unitarian (the honoured opponent). Senses 2 and 6.
  • prayer, sacrifice (how it is known). Sense 1.
  • Creed (where it is confessed). See Creed.

Scripture. Matthew 28:19, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, the text of “The Uniting Name”; Ephesians 4:5–6, One Lord, one faith, one baptism, One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all, the text of “Faith in the Trinity Not Faith in a Dogma”; 2 Corinthians 13:14, the grace.
