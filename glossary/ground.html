A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

ground

root · foundation

Frequency in the corpus: ground and grounds 983 (many in the ordinary senses); root and roots 326; foundation and foundations 382. The key source is the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “The Foundation and the Building”, on 1 Corinthians 3:11.

In brief. What everything rests on and grows from. Maurice’s governing claim is that the foundation is laid, not made: no teacher or body of men put it there, and all they can do is point to it. That foundation is a Person, Christ, who is the root of humanity and the ground of society, of the universe and of all theology. Behind him stands the Father as an Absolute Root of truth and good. To build on anything else, opinions, self, or the will of a party, is to build on rottenness. The three words are nearly interchangeable in Maurice; root adds the sense of life drawn from below.

Shades of meaning

  1. Laid, not made. On Paul’s text: “He could not lay the Foundation. It is laid, as our translators have it. It is lying, as St. Paul says himself; intimating that he did not put it there; that no man put it there; that the beliefs of no body of men put it there” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Foundation and the Building”). The teacher’s whole office is to point to it. See unity, sense 1, where unity is kept and not made in the same way.

  2. A Person, not opinions. The Apostle “was conversant with men, not with notions” (same). Our failure is that “We have had opinions about Him, we have made much of those opinions, we have judged other men for wanting them. But we have not been resting on that foundation which is laid for us” (same). See opinions · notions, sense 3.

  3. A moral foundation. The foundation is righteous and makes a righteous demand: “it is a moral foundation which is laid for us all; that the Person on whom we stand, and on whom Society stands, is a Righteous Person, who speaks to a spirit in us, which hungers and thirsts after Righteousness” (same). See righteousness.

  4. Christ the root of humanity. Maurice’s characteristic phrase: “He is the living root of humanity, whence all its energies are derived, apart from which it is a name and not a reality” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Circumcision and the Uncircumcision Merged in Christ”). Justification by faith means “that this Son of God, and not Adam, was the true root of Humanity; that from Him, and not from any ancestor, each man derived his life” (Theological Essays, “On Justification by Faith”). The good in every man springs from “a Divine root of humanity, a Son of Man” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture X). See Head and mankind.

  5. The ground of the universe. The filial union of the Son with the Father “is the ground of the universe and the ground of our humanity” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII). The faith in the Trinity declares “that there is an Absolute Root of truth and good at the foundation of all things,—the Eternal Father” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). At the end “He in whom is light and no darkness at all, is proved to be the ground of all things” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XIX). See Trinity and Father.

  6. The ground of society. Christ “came as the true root of humanity, the real foundation of all religion, politics, morality, domestic affections, the only reconciler of them with each other, the only security for their permanence” (The Unity of the New Testament, “St. Luke”). The Jew who rested all these “upon a ground of self” saw them go to ruin (same). See society and nation.

  7. False grounds and rottenness. Sects tempt their sons “to ask whether the earth has no other foundations than those which the sects have laid—often to arrive at the miserable conclusion that its foundations are built on rottenness” (Theological Essays, “On the Unity of the Church”). Babel and Israel are “the kingdom which stood on the ground of self-will; the kingdom which stood on the ground of the God of Righteousness” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture IX). See self-will and sect.

  8. The root of sin. The same words name evil at its source: death is horrible while linked “to that distrust of God which is Sin, and the root of all sins” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV). The idea of an arbitrary God to be conciliated was “the false notion which lay at the root of all our discontent” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon I). See sin.

Neighbouring words

  • Head (the same Person from above). Christ is “the one living Head and Root of humanity” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The First Epistle to the Corinthians”); see Head.
  • opinions, notions (what cannot be a foundation). Sense 2; see opinions · notions.
  • building, edifice (what rests on it). Senses 1 and 6.
  • constitution, order (what grows from it). Sense 6.
  • self, self-will (the false ground). Senses 6 and 7.
  • Lamb slain from the foundation of the world (its eternity). Sense 5; the title of The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII.
  • principle (its intellectual form). Maurice calls Irving’s theocratic faith “the very ground of all theology” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter).

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 3:11, For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ, the text of “The Foundation and the Building”; Revelation 22:16, I am the root and the offspring of David; 1 Peter 1:20, foreordained before the foundation of the world (sense 5); Ephesians 3:17, being rooted and grounded in love; Matthew 7:24–27, the house on the rock and the house on the sand (sense 7).
