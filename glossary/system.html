A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

system

systematizer · method

Frequency in the corpus: system and systems 767; method 303. The key passages are in The Kingdom of Christ, especially I, “The Scriptural View of This Constitution”, and II, “Modern English Systems”.

In brief. A system is a scheme of thought made by men from one principle torn away from the rest, and then imposed as the ground of a party. Maurice refuses to build one and is suspicious of all of them, the most complete most of all. Against system he sets method: the living order that is found in history, in the Bible and in the Church, and that is discovered rather than invented. He reads each system for the truth it witnesses to, which is always a truth of the divine order it replaces.

Shades of meaning

  1. System against method. The two are not synonyms but opposites: “Now to me these words seem not only not synonymous, but the greatest contraries imaginable: the one indicating that which is most opposed to life, freedom, variety; and the other that without which they cannot exist” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “The Scriptural View of This Constitution”). The Bible shows it: the systematizer is “tormented every page he reads with a sense of the refractory and hopeless materials he has to deal with” (same), while the plain reader senses “some harmony, not in the words but in the history” (same). See Bible, sense 7.

  2. A principle torn from the rest. Of the sects: “they have made a system out of some principle which they have torn apart from the rest; that they have destroyed that principle by its separation” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”).

  3. A substitute for the divine order. The rival systems of the day, “Protestant, Romish, English, seem to me each to bear witness of the existence of a Divine Order; each to be a miserable, partial, human substitute for it” (same). So men must be “emancipated from the chains which they have made for themselves, and entering into the freedom of God’s Church” (same). See Church and kingdom.

  4. The more complete, the worse. One who would “substitute a Church for systems” must dread most “the attempt at a complete, all-comprehending system. Hating all systems, he hates those most which are most perfect, because in them there are the fewest crannies and crevices through which the light and air of heaven may enter” (same). Hence his harder judgment on Rome, “all-embracing and satisfactory, therefore more lifeless, inhuman, godless” (same). See Catholic, sense 5.

  5. The truth in each system. Maurice will not simply condemn them: “Our consciences, I believe, have told us from time to time that there is something in each of them which we ought not to reject” (same). The young man is told to hold fast what he was taught until he has mastered its truth, and then “do what you like with the system” (same).

  6. System and life. Doctrines that are only “parts of an anthropological or theological system” do not concern busy men (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “How We Learn to Fight”); what matters is whether they speak of an actual fight in which men are engaged. Faith comes “by being weaned from notions and systems” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Steps to Faith in the Holy Trinity”). See Creed.

  7. Not natural to England. “This system building is not natural to us” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”). The English divine’s habit of “keeping their places, and doing their work” is better than it looks (same). See nation.

Neighbouring words

  • method (its opposite). Sense 1.
  • principle (what a system distorts). Sense 2.
  • order, constitution (what it replaces). Sense 3.
  • sect, party, school (its social form). Senses 2 and 5.
  • notions, opinions, theory (its material). Sense 6.
  • Church, unity (what Maurice sets against it). Sense 4; see unity, sense 5.
  • Eclecticism, Syncretism (the charges he expects). He thinks these words “bugbears to frighten children with” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”).

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 1:12–13, I am of Paul; and I of Apollos … Is Christ divided?; and the words Maurice sets against all systems and parties, None who trusteth in Me shall be confounded (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”).
