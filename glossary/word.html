A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Word

Word of God · Eternal Word

Frequency in the corpus: the Word, Word of God, Divine Word and Logos together 217, besides many uses of word of God for Scripture or the prophetic message. The main sources are The Epistles of St. John, Lecture II, Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XIX, and The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIX.

In brief. For Maurice the Word of God is first a living Person, the Son, who utters what is in the Father as a word utters the thought that it makes known. He is the light that has lightened every man, the Teacher who speaks in the conscience and reason of men in all ages, the Creator by whom the worlds were made, and the Word made flesh. Maurice insists that the Word must be thought of as a Person and not a decree, and that the Word is known as the Lamb that was slain. Scripture is the word of God because it speaks of him.

Shades of meaning

  1. The utterance of God. If the life of a man perfectly expressed the mind and purpose of God, we should call him the Word of God. “You would not mean merely that something which he spoke proceeded from God, and declared what He intended or willed. You would mean that he, the whole person, was and is the Word of God” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture II). “He who shows forth the true life to man is as much one with God as the word is with the thought that it makes known” (same). See manifestation.

  2. Word and Son together. Of the two names Word of God and Son of God: “Neither gives forth a complete sense without the other” (same); the name Son guards against that, and the name Word guards the Son’s eternity. In the Trinity, the Eternal Word is “a perfect Utterer and Revealer of that truth and good” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). See Trinity and Father.

  3. A living Person. “If we do not habitually think of the Word of God as a living Person, it signifies little whether the first verse of the fourth Gospel, and all the rest of the writings which profess to set forth the operations of the Word, were derived from Philo or from anyone else” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). The prophets “conversed with this Word as with a living person” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture I). See person.

  4. The light of every man. Maurice would not give up the belief “that the Word of God before He came in the flesh was the light which lightened all men—a principle as much confirmed to me by the evidence of profane as of sacred history” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “On the Principles of the Quakers”). The Fathers told the philosophers “that the Divine Word had been speaking to the conscience and reason of men in all ages” (same). “The Gospel assumes, that all the light which is in thee, or in any man, proceeds from His light” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XIX). See conscience and light.

  5. The Word in the conscience. The young men of St. John’s letter “must believe that they have the Word of God dwelling in them; they must learn that there is One who is speaking to them in their consciences, who is quickening their hearts, who will go with them where they go” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VIII). Maurice was charged with mysticism when he “discoursed of the divine Word as the great Teacher of mankind” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). See witness.

  6. The Creator. “That Eternal Word is declared in St. John’s Gospel to be the same by Whom the worlds were created, and without Whom was not anything made that was made. The Divine Artist fashioned the whole universe” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VIII). So the world the young men are not to love is not God’s world but its counterfeit. See world.

  7. Known as the Lamb. In the Apocalypse the rider’s name is the Word of God, and his vesture is dipped in blood: “the divine Word can only be known as the Lamb which was slain; that the death and sacrifice of the Lamb are nothing except as they reveal that Filial Word, who was in the bosom of the Father” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIX). “When the world seems most desolate of God’s presence … the Word of God who created it is still upholding it” (same). See sacrifice.

  8. Our king. In the country sermons the doctrine becomes plain speech: “The Word of God, the Lord Jesus Christ, is stronger than all the bad people who can ever come near us; stronger than our own fancies and lusts. He is our king, as He was Matthew’s king” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXVI, “St. Matthew’s Day”). See king.

Neighbouring words

  • Son, Son of God (its companion name). Sense 2.
  • Light, Life (what the Word is to men). Senses 1 and 4; see light and life.
  • Teacher, Guide, Enlightener (its office). Senses 4 and 5.
  • Logos, Philo (the speculation it is not). Sense 3; “what they call the Logos doctrine” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XIX) Maurice reads through St. John, not Alexandria.
  • word of God, Scripture (the derived sense). In Psalm 119 “the word of God, the law of God, the commandment of God” are “used for each other” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VI); see Bible · Scripture.
  • Lamb (how the Word is known). Sense 7.
  • Incarnation (the Word made flesh). See Incarnation.

Scripture. John 1:1–14, In the beginning was the Word … That was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world … And the Word was made flesh (senses 1, 4 and 6); 1 John 1:1, of the Word of life; 1 John 2:14, the word of God abideth in you (sense 5); Revelation 19:13, his name is called The Word of God (sense 7).
