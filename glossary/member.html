A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

member

union · communion

Frequency in the corpus: member and members 661; union 297.

In brief. The state of every man as part of the body whose Head is Christ. “Member of Christ” is the Catechism’s phrase, and Maurice takes it as a fact about all the baptized, and in principle about all men, not a reward for the converted. Membership brings union with God and communion with every other member, and it binds men to all nations and classes. The opposite of union is separation, which is sin and death.

Shades of meaning

  1. The Catechism’s claim. The Catechism “tells them that they are members of Christ, children of God, inheritors of the kingdom of Heaven” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). He sets it against the view that men “are not members of Christ or children of God” until they have been “actually and consciously converted” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”).

  2. A right and duty of every man. Because Christ is Head of every man, “It must be the right and duty of every man to confess himself a member of Christ” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Blessing and Curse of the Law”). To refuse it is to live a lie: “a man believes and acts a lie who does not claim for himself union with Christ” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “The Scriptural View of This Constitution”). See Head.

  3. Union with God as life. Christ’s priesthood gives “the salvation from that death which consists in separation from God; the eternal life which consists in union with Him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVII). In the Communion, Christian men declare “that sin is the separation from God; that Christ is union with God” (What is Revelation?, Letter X). See eternal.

  4. Members of one another. Union with God carries communion with men: “fellowship or communion with each other is implied in this fellowship or communion with God and with His Son” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture II). The Eucharist is its sign: St. Paul “speaks of our being all members of one body, because we all eat of that one bread” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Sacramental Character of the Bible”). See Eucharist.

  5. Bonds across every division. Membership makes obligations wider than nation or class. The convert who “claimed to be a member of Christ and a child of God, he contracted affinities and obligations to Jew and Greek, barbarian and Scythian, bond and free” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Ministry of Sovereigns”). It changes how one sees one’s kind: “being members of His body we may see Christ in each and Christ in all” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon IX).

  6. The union of the Father and the Son. All human union has its original in God: “The union of the Father with the Son is involved in the very idea of God as it is declared to us in the New Testament” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture II). The final city is “grounded upon the union of God with man” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXI).

  7. A calling against the selfish society. The title is also a summons: “Members of Christ, who gave up Himself, you are tempted by a society which makes self its centre” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XVII).

Neighbouring words

  • Head (the one they are members of). Sense 2; see Head.
  • body (the whole). Sense 4.
  • child of God, inheritor (the Catechism’s paired titles). Sense 1.
  • separation (opposite). Sense 3.
  • fellowship, communion (union among men). Sense 4.
  • conversion (the condition wrongly made prior to it). Sense 1.
  • Mediator (the one in whom union is made). See Mediator.

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 12:26–27, whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it … Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular, the text of “The Body and the Members”; Romans 12:5, every one members one of another; Ephesians 5:30, we are members of his body; 1 Corinthians 10:17, we being many are one bread, and one body (sense 4); John 17:21, that they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me (sense 6).
