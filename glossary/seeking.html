A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

seeking

seek God · feel after Him · God seeking men

Frequency in the corpus: seek, seeking and seeker with God, truth or Him as object in about 160 paragraphs; feel after Him in about 15. The sermons and lectures on St. Paul at Athens are the main source, with “National Holiness” and “The Faith of Protestants” in the Lincoln’s Inn sermons.

In brief. Maurice’s seeking runs in both directions, and the second is the deeper. Men seek God, if haply they might feel after him and find him, and it was God who stirred up that search, ordering the bounds of the nations for it. Every honest seeker after truth is under his teaching. But the Bible is the history of God seeking men, not of men seeking God. The great reversal of conversion is to learn that God is not seeking to condemn but to justify, and to bring men into union with himself. Seeking goes wrong when men seek God where he is not, in visible things, or seek righteousness in themselves instead of in him. Self-seeking is its counterfeit.

Shades of meaning

  1. That they should feel after him. “That they may seek the Lord, if haply they may feel after Him, and find Him.” So the Apostle explains: “it was God Himself who stirred up the thoughts and inquiries of men about His Being and Nature” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “St. Paul at Athens”). Men were “created to feel after Him and find Him”, and so to cast him in any material shape was degrading (same). If we believed this, “many puzzles in history” would be solved (same). See history and nation.

  2. Gathered up in Christ. Christ’s acts of power “gather up the different functions which those who were seeking God, if haply they might feel after Him and find Him, had attributed to separate divinities” (What Is Revelation?, Sermon IV). Missionaries must declare “Who He was of Whom they had been dreaming, Whom they had been seeking for” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Uniting Name”). See Name and manifestation.

  3. God seeking men. The Scriptures “do not set forth the history of a man seeking for God, but of God seeking for men” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “National Holiness”). “A man supposes that God has cast off him, and that, in some way or other, he must seek to win back His favour. The Bible says that man has cast off God, and that God is seeking to bring him into union with Himself. All other differences lie in this one” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Faith of Protestants”). See Deliverer and revelation.

  4. Seeking where he is not. “The Gentile was seeking God where He was not to be found, invoking Him when He was not near. Conscience made a coward of him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII). So long as they were conformed to the world, “they sought God in the multitude of objects which they saw about them” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Conformity to the Mind of God”). See idolatry.

  5. Seeking in ourselves. Men want righteousness, love and truth, and miss them “because they are seeking them in themselves, and not in God, in whom alone they dwell” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Practice and Speculation”). Seeing the bad of every party in ourselves, “we shall be driven to seek our help in Him who desireth truth in our inward parts” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Daylight in the Heart the Preparation for the Day of Christ”). See righteousness and self-will.

  6. Seekers after truth. To the Magi, “earnest seekers after Truth”, Jesus in his cradle is discovered as the King they wanted (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Gospel the Manifestation of God”). It is good news “that God Himself was directing the thoughts of the student of the stars, and of all other students and seekers in every direction” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Glory of the Chosen Nation Realized in the Illumination of the World”). When the physical world yields its secrets “to the humble and faithful seeker”, that is a pledge the Apocalypse is not delusive (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXII). See knowledge, sense 8.

  7. The solitary seeker. Without holy days or assemblies, God “offers Himself to the solitary despairing seeker, as the foundation of all human fellowship” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Thirst of Human Beings in All Ages”). The Socratic philosopher was “the seeker after what is true and good, not as the receiver of an influence from it” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “On the Principles of the Quakers”), yet he confessed he needed more. See fellowship.

  8. Self-seeking. “The self-seeker has been separating himself from his kind—from his race,—or has only been using the members of it as instruments for obtaining his own ends” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Eucharist Considered as a Participation in the Unselfish Life of Christ”). The heartless, “anxious self-seekers” are “continually hunting after safety and comfort” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon II). See self-will and evil spirit.

Neighbouring words

  • feel after (its groping form). Senses 1 and 2.
  • find (its end, given by God). Sense 1.
  • conversion (the discovery that God seeks). Sense 3; see repentance.
  • thirst (its inward root). Sense 7; see idolatry, sense 6.
  • inquiry, search (its intellectual form). Senses 1 and 6.
  • self-seeking (its counterfeit). Sense 8.

Scripture. Acts 17:26–28, that they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him, and find him (sense 1); Luke 19:10, the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost (sense 3); Luke 15:4–10, the lost sheep and the lost coin (sense 3); Matthew 2:1–11, the wise men (sense 6); Matthew 6:33, seek ye first the kingdom of God (sense 5); Psalm 51:6, thou desirest truth in the inward parts (sense 5).
