A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

faith

believe · trust

Frequency in the corpus: faith 1,857; belief 708; trust 580. Important sources are The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVI, “Faith and Love”, and the Lincoln’s Inn sermons on justification.

In brief. Faith is trust in a living Person, God the Father revealed in his Son, and not assent to propositions or confidence in one’s own state. It is receptive, not creative: it answers a truth that is there already and calls it forth. It is God’s gift, and it saves a man by emptying him of himself. Maurice keeps the Reformation’s justification by faith and insists that it means faith in a God who justifies, not faith as a new kind of merit.

Shades of meaning

  1. Faith in a Person. “The Epistle bids us look from our faith to a Living Person Who is the only root of it, the only end of it. Our faith is not in itself, but in Him; if we think of it instead of Him, it perishes” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Christian Life”). The faith that conquered the ancient world “was faith in a Person who had come by water and by blood” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVII). See revelation.

  2. Faith that empties. “It is the renunciation of every claim in a man to do acts of his own, to exist by himself” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVI). It is not a possession: “It saves him, by emptying him. It delivers him from himself; it attaches him to another. He is just by faith, because he believes in a just God” (same). See self-will.

  3. Greatest when least. The mustard seed shows it: “It is the greatest when it is least. The man feels that he has nothing, and can do nothing; therefore he relies on One who has all” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXI). “Once let him think that he has any power of his own—call it faith, or what you please—the reliance vanishes; the essence of faith is gone” (same).

  4. Like sight: receptive, not creative. “Faith is as much that exercise in which the spirit is and lives, as sight is the exercise in which the eye is and lives” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Eucharist”). The error is “to suppose that faith is not a receptive, but a creative power; that it makes the thing which it believes” (same). Faith “is but the answer in me to the truth which calls it forth” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Hatred Necessary to Love”). See Eucharist.

  5. God’s gift. “Truly, faith is the gift of God. He awakens it in us, as He awakens everything in us that is living” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVI). “My faith is given by Him” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Hatred Necessary to Love”).

  6. Justification by faith. Faith does not replace works as a merit: “Their faith is not Righteousness; for faith, like sight, is nothing in itself” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “God the Justifier of Man”). Luther’s message was “that union with Christ was deliverance from sin and condemnation; that that union was claimed and maintained by faith; that faith was therefore justification” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Pure Protestantism”). Its failure is when “the faith was in itself, not in God” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Old and New Paradise”). See righteousness.

  7. The ground of obedience. The lesson “of the whole Bible” is “that Faith is the only foundation of obedience, that it is impossible truly to obey a Being whose character you do not entirely trust” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Blessing and Curse of the Law”). A faith that does not issue in right acts “is not really faith in the living God” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXIII, “The Law of Liberty”). See law.

  8. Christ’s own faith. Faith and love meet in the Son: “Christ’s own Faith was in the love of His Father” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVI). See love and Father.

Neighbouring words

  • trust (its plainest synonym). Sense 1.
  • belief, opinion, notion (what it is not). Faith is in a Person, not in propositions; see Creed.
  • sight (its analogue). Senses 4 and 6.
  • justification (what it receives). Sense 6.
  • obedience (what it grounds). Sense 7.
  • love, hope (its companions). Sense 8; Maurice’s sermon “The Three Things Which Abide” treats all three.
  • self-will (its opposite). Sense 2; see self-will.

Scripture. Hebrews 11:1, faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen; Hebrews 12:2, Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith (sense 1); Romans 4:5, to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness (sense 6); Ephesians 2:8, it is the gift of God (sense 5); Luke 17:6, the mustard seed and the sycamine tree (sense 3); 1 John 5:4, this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.
