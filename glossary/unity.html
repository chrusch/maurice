A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

unity

one · uniformity

Frequency in the corpus: unity 1,313. The main sources are the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “The Bond of Peace”, on Ephesians 4:3, and Theological Essays, “On the Unity of the Church”.

In brief. The oneness that God has already given to men, in families, in nations and in the Church, and whose ground is the unity of God himself, the Father and the Son in the Spirit. Maurice’s governing point is that unity is to be kept, not made. Every attempt to create it by scheme, system or force destroys what it means to secure. Unity is also distinct from uniformity: it holds diversity together and does not abolish it.

Shades of meaning

  1. Kept, not made. Paul bids us keep the unity of the Spirit: “It is something then that we have, and are likely to lose; not something that we may possibly get, if fortune favours us” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Bond of Peace”). The same holds of every human order: “There is a Family Unity; a Unity to be kept, not created; one which you destroy, if you seek to make it anew upon a scheme and platform of your own” (same). See family and nation.

  2. The unity of the Spirit. It is the common life the Spirit makes out of men’s different efforts: “By the Unity of the Spirit I understand that which is produced in us by the Spirit of God,—the infinitely various efforts of desire, faith, hope, which He awakens and cultivates in different men, and which He makes them feel to be the movements and struggles of a common life” (same). The bond of peace is what they rest in, as distinct from it “as the breath is from the air we breathe” (same). See Spirit.

  3. Unity in Christ, not in opinions. The principle of the Catholic Church is “that all unity is to be in Christ, and that intellectual notions and opinions ought not to divide men from Him” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Modern English Systems”). See Catholic and Head.

  4. The unity of God. Maurice honours the Unitarian witness to God’s oneness, “the unfathomable foundation of thought, and faith, and being” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Unitarianism”), but finds its full ground in the Name of Father, Son and Spirit. The worshippers who made the Trinity-Sunday collect “found that it must be in the Unity of the Spirit that the Father and the Son rejoice together for ever” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Conformity to the Mind of God”). See Trinity and Name.

  5. Unity against system. A living unity is opposed to the comprehensive system: the hope of reconciling diverse thought may “disconnect itself with the dreary vision of a comprehensive System, from which all life is excluded, if the central Unity be that of the living Being” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Dedication”). See system.

  6. Unity not secured by force. Persecutors acted from a true conviction that “nothing can be so precious to a nation, or to the Catholic Church, or to every human being, as Unity” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Bond of Peace”). Maurice denies only the minor premise: “Neither these measures nor any measures such as these ever did promote Unity” (same).

  7. Unity and uniformity. Outward sameness is not the thing itself: “we never can recover uniformity till we have the unity which it betokens; nay, that if we had the one, we might perhaps bear some deviations from the other” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Faith of Catholics”). In the heavenly multitude “There is no dull uniformity, no single tongue: but all is harmonious amidst diversity” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Sealed Nation and the Universal Family”).

Neighbouring words

  • union, communion, member (its personal form). See member · union.
  • Spirit (its author). Sense 2; see Spirit.
  • peace (its rest). Sense 2.
  • Trinity, Name (its ground). Sense 4; see Trinity.
  • uniformity (its counterfeit). Sense 7.
  • system, scheme, platform (attempts to make it). Senses 1 and 5; see system.
  • sect, division (its breach). Sense 3.
  • diversity, variety (what it holds together). Senses 2 and 7.

Scripture. Ephesians 4:3, Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace, the text of “The Bond of Peace”; Ephesians 4:13, till we all come in the unity of the faith; John 17:21, That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee; Psalm 133:1, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity, which the Epistles of St. John connect with the anointing of the Spirit.
