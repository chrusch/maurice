A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

repentance

repent · conversion · change of mind

Frequency in the corpus: repent, repentance and their forms in about 250 paragraphs. The country sermons “Repentance and Conversion”, “St. Peter’s Conversion”, “St. John Baptist’s Day” and “The Baptism of Repentance” are the main sources.

In brief. To repent is to change one’s mind about God: to stop thinking of him as an enemy to be fled from or bribed, and to believe him a Father. Conversion is the turning that follows, and the two belong together. Only then can a man truly say that he has sinned. Repentance is too hard for man to accomplish. It is Christ’s gift, and the call to it is itself the gift. The call is the same for the drunkard and the most religious. Weighing out contrition to earn acceptance is pride. Repentance may be national as well as personal, and it is always tied to the nearness of the kingdom.

Shades of meaning

  1. A change of mind about God. “To Repent is to change our minds; to think quite otherwise of some matter or some person than we have been used to think.” St. Peter’s hearers “thought of Him as an enemy from whom they were to fly if they could, and whom if they could not escape they must persuade by all arguments and bribes not to destroy them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XLI, “Repentance and Conversion”). Their suspicions were sown by “an Evil Spirit, a Slanderer” (same). See evil spirit and Father.

  2. And be converted. “These two words do not mean the same thing. But they commonly are found together in the Scriptures; one is not perfect without the other” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XLI, “Repentance and Conversion”). “If you do repent or change your minds about God, then you will turn or be converted to Him” (same). Maurice asks “whether every act of repentance is not one of conversion or turning to God” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”).

  3. Then first we say, We have sinned. Before turning, men may feel restless, accuse others, or feel “both remorse and shame for acts which had been done and could never be undone, but they will not have said We have sinned. No one says that till he feels that he has been disbelieving, distrusting, disobeying a Father” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XLI, “Repentance and Conversion”). See sin, sense 1.

  4. Christ’s gift. “Repentance is far too hard a thing for man to accomplish; any man who has tried to repent knows that. But Christ is revealed as a prince over our hearts, to turn them according to God’s good and gracious will” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, V, “St. Peter’s Conversion”). Christ “could not only call to repentance but give repentance” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture X). “He calls thee to repent; He whose call, if thou heedest it, is the gift of repentance” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture III). See Spirit.

  5. The same call to all. “This call to Repentance comes from Him and not from us; that as there are none who do not need it, so there are none who may not profit by it; that it is addressed to the drunkard and the harlot as well as to the most religious” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XLI, “Repentance and Conversion”). The Spirit “who shows us our sins, and awakens us to repentance is the Spirit of Truth” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXI, “Truth”).

  6. Not a present to bring. The casuist asks how much contrition is needed to make a true repentance and an acceptable sacrifice, and penitent hearts “have been, not tormented merely—that is nothing—but made utterly insincere and false” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VI). “As long as a man fancies that he has contrition, or any other present, to bring to God, in order to make himself acceptable, so long he is not really humbling himself” (same). See sacrifice.

  7. Your King is near you. John the Baptist joined “The Kingdom of Heaven is at hand” with a call to turn: Repent, he said in effect, your King is near you and knows your secrets; confess them, and “there will be a way between your heart and Him” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XII, “St. John Baptist’s Day”). See kingdom.

  8. National repentance. “This, then, is the ground upon which I speak of national repentance. It is not a fantastic, unreal repentance.” “What is best for England is best for each Englishman; and this, because what is best for England is best for every nation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “National Holiness”). See nation.

  9. Turning to be delivered. “When we turn to God, not that we may obtain indulgence for our sin, but that we may be delivered from it; not that we may gain some blessing for ourselves from which others are excluded” we find the promises fulfilled (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XLI, “Repentance and Conversion”). See Deliverer and absolution.

Neighbouring words

  • conversion, turning (its completion). Sense 2.
  • confession (its speech). Senses 3 and 7.
  • contrition, remorse (its counterfeits when weighed). Senses 3 and 6.
  • remission, blotting out (its promise). “Your sins may be blotted out. You may be made new and pure creatures” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XLI, “Repentance and Conversion”).
  • kingdom at hand (its occasion). Sense 7.
  • baptism of repentance (John’s sign). See baptism.

Scripture. Acts 3:19, Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, the text of Sermon XLI (senses 1 to 3, 5 and 9); Acts 5:31, to give repentance to Israel, and forgiveness of sins (sense 4); Matthew 3:2, Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand (sense 7); Psalm 51:17, a broken and a contrite heart (sense 6); Luke 15:18, the prodigal’s resolve (sense 3).
