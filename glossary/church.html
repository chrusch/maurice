A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Church

Churches · Catholic Church · Universal Church

Frequency in the corpus: Church 2,139; Churches 918; Catholic 205; religious world 20.

In brief. The society of men constituted in Christ and baptized into the Name of the Father, the Son and the Spirit, which exists to witness to all men of the order they already belong to. For Maurice the Church is not a company of the saved set apart from mankind, nor one religious body among others, but human society as God made it, restored to its centre. It is universal by nature and yet distinct, since its whole meaning lies in bearing witness to what the world denies.

Shades of meaning

  1. Society restored. The definition Maurice returns to sets the Church and the world as one society in two states: “The Church is, therefore, human society in its normal state; the World, that same society irregular and abnormal. The world is the Church without God; the Church is the world restored to its relation with God, taken back by Him into the state for which He created it” (Theological Essays, “On the Unity of the Church”).

  2. A witness. The Church exists for others: “The Church exists to tell the world of its true Centre, of the law of mutual sacrifice by which its parts are bound together” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). In the Pastoral Epistles it is the body “which existed to bear witness of the reconciliation between heaven and earth” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The Pastoral Epistles”).

  3. Grounded in the Name and the Head. The Church rests on what God has done, not on what its members feel or decide. “The Church to which I belong, the Church which I read of in the Bible, is baptized into the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Ghost. This name is its foundation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). It is “constituted in its Universal Head” (same sermon). See Head and Name.

  4. The universal society. In The Kingdom of Christ the Church is the third part of man’s spiritual constitution, after family and nation, and the one that enlarges both: of the two possible universal societies, the one destructive of family and nation “is that which in Scripture is called the world, the latter is that which in Scripture is called the Church” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”). Its universality depends on its distinctness: “In losing its distinctness it loses its meaning” (same paragraph).

  5. Known by its signs. The Church is a fact to be found, not a theory to be built. The Kingdom of Christ asks: “Are there any signs in the present day of the existence of a spiritual and universal body upon the earth?” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. IV), and answers with Baptism, the Creeds, forms of worship, the Eucharist, the ministry and the Scriptures. The Bible and the Church interpret each other: “The Church exists as a fact, the Bible shews what that fact means” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Scriptures”). See sign.

  6. Universal and national. The Universal Church is present in each national Church and not in a single empire over them. Maurice asks his hearers to “confess that true Universal Church to be implied in the existence of each particular and national one” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). Church and nation stand together against the world: “a nation just so far as it is a nation, is antisecular in one way, just as much as the Church is anti-secular in another” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Pure Theocratist”).

  7. The Church fallen into a world. When the Church sets itself up as a rival to the world or puts a false centre in place of Christ, it becomes a world itself: “The Church, exalting itself, has raised the selfishness of the world into a law, and has stamped it with divinity. The Church, humbling itself, has borne witness to the world of One who gave up Himself that He might take away its sin” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The World and the Church”). “Deprive the Church of its Centre, and you make it into a world” (Theological Essays, “On the Unity of the Church”).

Neighbouring words

  • world (the same society without its centre). Sense 1; see world.
  • kingdom (what it confesses). The Church is the kingdom acknowledged; see kingdom, sense 4.
  • body, members, Head (its figure). Sense 3; see Head.
  • sect, system, party (counterfeits). A sect claims to be the Church of the elect; a system replaces the Church’s facts with a scheme. Of Irving, Maurice says he “had changed his Church into a sect” by “representing the belief in God as dependent on the belief in the Church” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter).
  • religious world (its counterfeit). “The religious world, which is the counterfeit of the Church, and which is trying to reduce all Churches into a Babel of sects” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter).
  • nation, family (the other parts of the constitution). Sense 4 and 6.
  • Baptism, Creed, Eucharist, ministry (its signs). Sense 5; see the entries for each.

Scripture. Ephesians 1:22–23, the church, which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all; 1 Timothy 3:15, the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth, read in “The Pastoral Epistles” (sense 2); Matthew 28:19, the baptismal command, behind sense 3.
