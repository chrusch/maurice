A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

person

living Person · personal

Frequency in the corpus: Person and Persons (capitalised) 242; person and persons 946; personal, personally and personality 387. The phrase “living Person” recurs throughout.

In brief. Maurice’s constant contrast is between a Person and a notion. What God reveals is not a doctrine but a living Person; the foundation of the Church and of society is a Person, and not a notion; faith is trust in a Person, not in our faith or in a dogma about him. God is a living, personal Being who says I am, and the Spirit is a Person, not a floating influence. The habit of thinking of God and Christ as persons is for Maurice the root of all his theology.

Shades of meaning

  1. Not a doctrine. “That which is revealed is not a doctrine—not anything which can be expressed in terms—but a living Person. The letters do not make or give the Revelation. God gives it” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture I). Even the Jesuit’s name “suggested to an earnest, self-denying man, not a set of opinions, but a living Person, Whom they might proclaim to men everywhere as their Saviour and their King” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Homily II: On Theology and Government in India”). See revelation and opinions · notions.

  2. The ground of society. “A Person, and not a notion, he declares to be the ground of this eternal spiritual constitution; a Person, and not a notion, to be the bond of the spiritual and universal society of Jews and Gentiles” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Great Sacrifice and the Feast of Deliverance”). Christ came “to declare that the foundation of the human Temple was not abstract, but living; not an opinion, or a collection of opinions, but a Person; not a man, but the Man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Foundation and the Building”). See ground · root · foundation and society.

  3. The standard of life. “If there were a perfect treatise, one in which were no errors at all, it would not be the standard that I wanted … The standard must be a Life. It must be set forth in a living Person. If it is to do me any good, his life must in some way act upon my life” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). See life.

  4. Faith in a Person. Maurice doubts we can “afford to substitute faith in a certain notion or dogma, for faith in a living person” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Pure Protestantism”). “The Epistle bids us look from our faith to a Living Person Who is the only root of it, the only end of it. Our faith is not in itself, but in Him” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Christian Life”). “At no time could trust in an actual living person be merged in obedience to formal rules” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “Christ’s Death and Resurrection the Solution of the Problems of the Universe”). See faith.

  5. God who says I am. God is “not a conception of your minds, not one Whom you make what He is, by your mode of thinking of Him, but a living Person, Who is saying to you what He said to Moses in the bush” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “Stillness in the Confidence of God’s Triumph”). He who rules all “is not a destiny, but a loving will; not an abstraction, but a Person; not a mere sovereign, but a Father” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon VIII). The Muslim’s witness is “the recognition of Him as a living personal Being” (The Religions of the World, Lecture I). See I AM and Father.

  6. The Spirit a Person. Pentecost “spoke of a real, not of an imaginary gift—of a living Person, not of a floating influence” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “The Help of Believing in a Spirit Who Has Been Given to Us”). The Quakers, who began with the Spirit, lost “the idea of a constant living personal presence” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Forms of Worship”). See Spirit.

  7. What unites citizens. “It is not an abstraction, but a Person, who unites you to them, who admonishes you of your transgressions against them, who makes you understand and fulfil your obligations to them” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Body and the Members”). See nation and member · union · communion.

  8. Distinct persons. Of men too: we want “that a child should grow into a distinct living person” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “Christian Innocence”). The ordinances “which give to each man most the sense of his own distinct and personal relation to God, are those which most denote His equal relation to all” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Lusts of the Flesh and of the Spirit”). See baptism.

Neighbouring words

  • notion, opinion, doctrine, dogma (its opposites). Senses 1, 2 and 4; see opinions · notions.
  • abstraction, influence, destiny, formula (its other opposites). Senses 5 to 7.
  • living (its constant epithet). Throughout.
  • Name (how a Person is known). See Name.
  • ground, foundation, root (what the Person is). Sense 2.
  • trust, faith (the response to a Person). Sense 4.
  • Head (a Person over men). Of the Son of Man the preacher must ask whether it “does not denote a living Person, an actual Ruler, the Head of every man?” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Homily III: On Preaching to the Poor”); see Head.

Scripture. Exodus 3:14, I AM THAT I AM (sense 5); 1 Corinthians 3:11, other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ, the text of “The Foundation and the Building” (sense 2); Ephesians 1:3–4, the text of “The Great Sacrifice and the Feast of Deliverance” (sense 2); Hebrews 12:2, Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith (sense 4).
