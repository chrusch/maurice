A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

holy

holiness · a holy nation · consecration

Frequency in the corpus: holy and holiness in about 360 paragraphs outside the name Holy Ghost. The sermon “National Holiness” in Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2 is the main source, with The Doctrine of Sacrifice and The Lord’s Prayer.

In brief. Holiness for Maurice is not an attainment that sets a few men apart from the many. It is being called and claimed by God: Moses was holy because God separated him to be His minister, and Israel was a holy nation because God chose it and put His name upon it. The nation’s holiness came before the holiness of its saints. Sin is therefore the forgetting of a holy state, not the failure to reach one. Every Englishman may say, God has called me, therefore I am holy, and confess his sin as a departure from what God has declared him to be. Things are holy too, when they are bound to God’s service.

Shades of meaning

  1. Called and set apart. Holiness is God’s act before it is man’s. Moses “is called out by God to know His name and to do His work”; “This was his holiness: he was separated, set apart by God to act as His minister” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). He who set him apart showed him “that Righteousness, and not Self-Will, was governing the universe” (same). See righteousness and seeking.

  2. A holy nation. The nation “was holy, in precisely the same sense and for precisely the same reason as he was; nay, that it had a title prior to his, a title from which his own was derived. It was a holy nation, because God had called it out—had chosen it to be His—had put His name upon it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). Joseph was better than his brethren “because he believed that God had chosen it, and they forgot that He had; because he did, and they did not, believe it to be holy” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). See nation and family.

  3. Sin as forgetting a holy state. Moses felt Israel’s stubbornness so sharply because he believed them holy. “Had they not been a holy people, he would not have known in what their sin consisted. It was the forgetfulness of their holy state—the choice of another—which he confessed with such shame and sorrow before God” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). Had he thought them only outcasts with “a certain possibility in them of becoming holy”, his prayer would have been “an uneasy, restless struggle for a boon” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). See sin and repentance.

  4. God has called me, therefore I am holy. The reckless man, the formal man and the would-be saint all need the same help. Each has a right to say: “God has called me, therefore I am holy; therefore my stubbornness and wickedness and sin have come from my not acting as if I were what God has declared that I am” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). So “we must regard our nation as holy, in spite of its wickedness and stubbornness and sin” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”), and “if we believe that our nation has a holy calling, we shall believe every nation to have a holy calling” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). See baptism and covenant.

  5. Holy things: consecration. Under the Law the priests “were holy creatures; God has made them so; for He had bound them to Himself. They were holy, for He was holy; and everything they had to do with was holy” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XI). The same holds of the ordinary order: “Property is holy, distinction of ranks is holy: so speaks the Law, and the Church does not deny the assertion, but ratifies it” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon V), though beneath them lie “the obligations of a common Creation, Redemption, Humanity” (same). See sacrifice and constitution.

  6. Not a conventional word. Maurice will not let the Bible’s words be emptied. “So long as we have the least suspicion that Righteousness and Holiness are words of merely conventional force in the Bible, so long we shall not regard the Bible as a true book” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “National Holiness”). See Bible.

Neighbouring words

  • election · chosen (ground). God’s call is the root of holiness.
  • Name · Hallowed be thy Name (source). The holiness of God, which men are to hallow.
  • saint · saintship (false narrowing). Holiness as a rank above ordinary men.
  • consecration · dedication (of things). What makes a vessel or an office holy.

Scripture. Ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation (Exodus 19:6). Be ye holy; for I am holy (1 Peter 1:16). Ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation (1 Peter 2:9).
