A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

fellowship

communion

Frequency in the corpus: fellowship 506; communion 303. The central source is The Epistles of St. John, Lectures I and II, on 1 John 1:3.

In brief. Shared life with God and with other men; Maurice uses fellowship and communion as synonyms. It is the highest end of man, and it is possible because God first seeks it. Fellowship with God carries fellowship with men in it; its source is the communion of the Father and the Son, and its centre is Christ, who in his sacrifice claimed fellowship with every sinner. Life itself is fellowship, going out of oneself; death is the self wrapped in itself. The communion of saints reaches across death.

Shades of meaning

  1. Possible for man. John claims for his readers all he claims for himself: “Fellowship or communion with God, he is to tell them, is possible for man” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture II).

  2. The highest end; with each other in God. “And so we arrive at the third proposition, that the highest end of man’s existence is to have fellowship with this Life and Light. And then a fourth, which we shall have to speak of more hereafter, that fellowship or communion with each other is implied in this fellowship or communion with God and with His Son” (same). See light and life.

  3. God seeks it first. “It is not we who seek first for fellowship with Him, He seeks to have fellowship with us. The children begin to ask for their Father, because the Father has been first seeking for His children” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, IX, “The Spirit the Help to Prayer”). See prayer and Father.

  4. Life is fellowship; death is isolation. The natural man is “the man wrapt in himself, apart from his fellows, apart from God” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). Scripture calls this state death, “because all life consists in going out of ourselves, in receiving influences from earth and from heaven, in having fellowship with earth and heaven” (same). See death · eternal death and self-will.

  5. Its source in the Godhead. The submission of the Son to the Father, “that perfect unity of Purpose, Will, Substance, between them, whence the obedience and fellowship of all unfallen beings, the obedience and fellowship of all restored beings, must be derived” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII). See Trinity.

  6. Christ its centre, through the cross. “He is our Peace; the centre of union and fellowship between the tribes of the earth” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIII). “There can be no intercourse and fellowship between heaven and earth, except in Him” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVI). We glory in the Cross because “He bore it that He might claim fellowship with all of us” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XVIII, “The Glory of the Cross”). See Mediator.

  7. Fellowship with sinners. The perfect Man, who “never claimed to be anything but the member of a kind” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII), must be “in sympathy and fellowship with all sinful men as no other ever was” (same). See Son of Man.

  8. The communion of saints across death. At the Eucharist “we may be sure that there is fellowship with all, wherever they are, who have overcome” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV). So the congregation may pray that whoever dies in the year “may not really be removed from fellowship with them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXIX, “St. John the Evangelist’s Day”). See Eucharist.

  9. Fellowship across parties. The Prayer Book gave Maurice “the power, if I would avail myself of it, of claiming unity and fellowship with both” Dissenter and Romanist (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). The word extends to knowledge: the doctor is perplexed where the peasant is not, “just because the doctor is not in communion with the facts of life as the peasant is” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture I). See unity and witness, sense 5.

Neighbouring words

  • union, member (its bodily form). See member · union.
  • unity (its given condition). See unity.
  • society, Church (its outward form). See society.
  • sacrament, Eucharist (its pledge). Sense 8; “The Church claims to have communion with the life of God. This is the meaning of its Sacraments” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVIII); see sacrament, sense 2.
  • life, light (what it shares). Senses 2 and 4.
  • isolation, separation, self (its opposites). Sense 4.
  • sympathy (its feeling). Sense 7.

Scripture. 1 John 1:3, that ye also may have fellowship with us: and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ, the text of Lectures I and II (senses 1 and 2); 1 John 1:7, if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another; 1 Corinthians 10:16, The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? (sense 8); 2 Corinthians 13:14, the communion of the Holy Ghost; Philippians 3:10, the fellowship of his sufferings (sense 7).
