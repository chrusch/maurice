A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

king

kingly · dominion · sovereign

Frequency in the corpus: king and kings 1,537; kingly 19; dominion 284. The kingdom itself has its own entry (see kingdom); this entry covers the King and the royal and ruling offices of men. Key sources are The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, whose lecture titles follow “the King” through St. Luke, and the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “The Ministry of Sovereigns”.

In brief. Christ is the true King of men, and his royalty is the opposite of the world’s: he takes the nature of his subjects, stoops to the lowest of them, suffers with them and delivers them from their oppressors. The King is the Son, and his kingly power is exercised in his Father’s name. Earthly sovereigns are his ministers for good, and their royalty is a reflection of his. Man was given dominion over the earth at creation; it can be abused till it becomes slavery, and it is redeemed in Christ, who makes men kings and priests. The same word names what usurps that rule: the dominion of visible things, of evil spirits and of the lie.

Shades of meaning

  1. The true King of men. Against the empire “grounded on the dominion of a man raised to be a God” Maurice sets “the announcement that the true King of men is one who, being in the form of God, took upon Him the nature of His subjects, that He might deliver them from all their oppressors bodily and spiritual” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). See Son of Man and Incarnation.

  2. One who stoops and suffers. The blind beggar “discovered what a Son of David, a true King of Men, must be; one who can feel with his subjects, suffer with them, deliver them out of their plagues and torments” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXII). “You want a king who can stoop to the state of the lowest and most wretched of His subjects” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XVIII, “The Glory of the Cross”). See Deliverer.

  3. The King is the Son. In the temptation Christ claims the state of man: “There is the King; and the King is the Son. He can depend upon the Word of God” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture V). His acts of healing were “manifestations of His inward Spiritual power, of His Kingly power. That power is exercised in the name of His Father, and to glorify His Father” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVII). See Father and Son of Man.

  4. The King on the cross. Birth, cross, resurrection and ascension “were steps in the manifestation of the true King and Conqueror of men. All were steps in the redemption of humanity” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XII). The Passion is not to be separated “from all the other steps in the manifestation of the true King” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXVII). See sacrifice.

  5. Sovereigns as ministers. Maurice holds fast “that the Sovereign is the minister of God to each of us for good” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Ministry of Sovereigns”), because Paul believed “Jesus Christ to be the Son of God and the King of men” (same). Earthly royalty is derived: “The royalty of kings and emperors is but the reflection of the royalty which He manifested in its fulness, Who healed the sick, cast out devils, went about doing good” (same). See nation.

  6. Man’s dominion. Man’s mastery of the horse has been “the type of his dominion over all the animals, the sign and pledge that he is himself of a higher origin” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture VI). Abraham had to learn “how man comes to have that dominion over animals which the monuments of Nineveh express; how he is liable to abuse that dominion till it turns into slavery” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon III). Power over others is held in trust: “that dominion is lent thee by me, to defend life not to take it away” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XI, “The Deliverer from Crime”). See nature.

  7. Kings and priests. “It is a King that our spirits cry for, to guide them, discipline them, unite them to each other; to give them a victory over themselves, a victory over the world” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVIII). Christ shares his office: “The kingly right of government over the earth, which was assured to him at creation, is redeemed for him in Christ. Earth is the instrument of his purposes, not the mistress who may command him” (same). To reign with Christ is to serve: Peter “understood that he was sitting and reigning with Christ when he was witnessing to men of His love for them and His dominion over them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, V, “St. Peter’s Conversion”).

  8. Usurped dominion. The disease and madness Christ healed were “proofs that an evil spirit, a tyrant, had got dominion over men” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, IV, “Waiting for Christ”). Of an unbelieving generation: “Visible things had dominion over them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXXVIII, “The House of God”). God hates “whatever lie is usurping dominion over men” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XV). See evil spirit · devil and idolatry.

Neighbouring words

  • kingdom (his realm). See kingdom.
  • Son, Son of Man (his title of nature). Sense 3; see Son of Man.
  • Deliverer, Conqueror (his work). Senses 2 and 4.
  • priest (his paired office). Sense 7.
  • sovereign, ruler, minister (his human deputies). Sense 5.
  • servant (the law of his kingdom). “The Chief of all is the Servant of all” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Ministry of Sovereigns”).
  • tyrant, usurper, beast (the false king). Sense 8.

Scripture. Psalm 8:6, Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands, from the psalm of “Man’s Dominion” (sense 6); Romans 13:4, he is the minister of God to thee for good (sense 5); Revelation 1:6, hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father (sense 7); Philippians 2:6–7, being in the form of God … took upon him the form of a servant (sense 1); Luke 23:38, THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS (sense 4).
