A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

nature

the King of nature · the powers of Nature · evil nature

Frequency in the corpus: nature in well over 1,000 paragraphs, most often in the phrases the nature of God, human nature and evil nature. The sense of the visible world is drawn here from The Unity of the New Testament, the Lincoln’s Inn sermons and Theological Essays.

In brief. Maurice uses nature in two main ways. Of the visible world, he teaches that the King of men is also the King of nature, who made the world for man and ordered it by laws that show themselves fully only when they bear on man. Nature is not to be worshipped: the heathen powers of Nature or a Lord of Nature divide God into many forms, and a God who merely plays with natural powers is an enchanter. Man is the lord of nature, and its study is sacred. Of man’s inner life, Maurice distinguishes the evil or selfish nature, which Christ delivers us from, from the true state or constitution in Christ to which He restores us.

Shades of meaning

  1. The King of men is the King of nature. The parables assume that “King of Men to be also the King of nature, the Creator and Lord of the world in which man is dwelling”, who made the world “especially for the habitation of man” and ordered it “according to laws which could only perfectly exhibit themselves when they bore upon his character and his acts” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The Miracles”). So the parable of the Sower sets forth both “a mystery of the Kingdom of Heaven” and “a mystery of the kingdom of earth” (same). See kingdom and law.

  2. Not an enchanter of nature. The miracles show not a magician but a Lord. Men “might have believed on a great enchanter,—in one who could play with the powers of nature. They would not have believed in a Lord of their hearts and reins; they would not have believed in a King of nature and of man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Miracles of Christ Generally”). See sign and manifestation.

  3. Against the worship of nature. “As long as a Jove, or some Lord of Nature is worshipped, he must be divided into a multitude of forms” (Theological Essays, “On the Trinity in Unity”). The Gospel made a new Heaven in which “powers of Nature, capricious gods, sometimes oppressors, sometimes deliverers, governing by fear, not justice, had passed away” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “The New Heavens and the New Earth”). See idolatry and unity.

  4. The work of the Spirit on things. “Every operation in nature, the growth of every tree, the budding of every flower, should be referred to the influence of Him who first moved upon the face of the waters; but we do not call this a spiritual influence, because, though wrought by a spiritual Being, it is wrought upon unspiritual subjects, upon things, and not upon persons” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Forms of Worship”). See Spirit and person.

  5. Man the lord of nature; its study sacred. Man is “the lord of nature” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 4, “Why Christ Could Not Make His Authority Known to the Pharisees”), and because the body and the earth are redeemed as truly as the spirit, a believer “must regard every physical study as a sacred study” (Theological Essays, “On Regeneration”). See knowledge and body.

  6. Evil nature and true state. Of man’s inner life, Maurice will not let the “infection of nature” excuse sin: “to say that this infection forces us to commit sin is to say what the Jews of old said” (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”) when they blamed their fathers. “The source of evil lies in the will; that our wills have set themselves in opposition to God’s will; that He is working to bring us into our true state; that all natural instruments are His instruments for this end” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XIX). Butler’s great facts “imply an ascent out of that nature,—a necessity in man to acknowledge that which is above it, that which is above himself” (Theological Essays, “On Regeneration”). See will, evil spirit and constitution.

Neighbouring words

  • law · laws (nature’s order). The laws which show themselves fully only in man.
  • constitution · true state (man’s real nature). What man is in Christ, against his evil nature.
  • world · earth (habitation). The world made for man to dwell in.
  • nature-worship · Lord of Nature (idolatry). God divided among the powers of the visible world.

Scripture. What manner of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him! (Matthew 8:27). Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet (Psalm 8:6). The Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters (Genesis 1:2).
