A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

will

the Will of God · a loving Will · the will in man

Frequency in the corpus: will of God, His will, Divine Will and the will in man in about 300 paragraphs. Essay VII of Theological Essays, Letter X of What Is Revelation? and The Doctrine of Sacrifice are the main sources.

In brief. For Maurice the Will of God is the ground of everything right, and that Will is a will to all good, a loving Will, not a bare sovereignty. It cannot be known as mere decree; it expresses itself in the relation of Father and Son. In Christ the Divine Will appears as the will of surrender. The will is also the centre of man: the Gospel is addressed to it, God and the devil both claim it, and it must submit to one of them. God does not crush the human will but educates and raises it, until it is subdued to His and, in that subjection, set free.

Shades of meaning

  1. The ground of all good. It belongs to the very method of theology “that the Will of God should be asserted as the ground of all that is right, true, just, gracious” (Theological Essays, “On the Atonement”). The doctrine of the Atonement must start there, “supposing the Father’s will to be a will to all good” (same), and not from a wrath that the Son has to turn aside. See Atonement and ground.

  2. A Will that speaks in a relation. The Will of God is not an arbitrary decree standing behind the Father. “This eternal and absolute Will of God is the root from which all the order of the universe proceeds. But this Will can only express itself in a divine Relation” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XVII). The Sonship of Christ is therefore the basis of all intercourse between God and man. See Father and constitution.

  3. A loving Will, not a mere Will. Maurice judges Islam by this test. Because the Mahometan recognizes “a mere Will governing all things, and that Will not a loving Will”, he falls from a witness of a Personal Being into the worship of a dead necessity; and “because he does not acknowledge a loving will acting upon men’s wills, to humble them in themselves, and to raise them to God, therefore he becomes the enslaver of his fellows” (The Religions of the World, Lecture V). A will of bare sovereignty makes slaves; a loving Will makes sons. See Absolute and king.

  4. The will of surrender. In Christ the Divine Will is seen to be the opposite of the self-exalting will of the creature: “The Divine Will exhibits itself in Jesus as the Will of surrender, of abasement; the will of the creature is that which aspires to set itself above the stars!” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 3, “Tests of the Deliverer”). So the Will of God and sacrifice are one thing: sacrifice “must proceed from the Will of God, and is perfected when the will of man is subdued to it” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VI). See sacrifice and self-will.

  5. The will in man. The will is the man himself, and the battle of the spiritual life is fought over it. “It is the will in man which God and the devil are both claiming; and that it is the will which must submit to either” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 1, “The Conversion of St. Paul”). Against Mansel, who would keep revelation to the intellect, Maurice asks: “What is the whole Gospel but a message to the Will? What is Redemption but a redemption of the Will?” (What Is Revelation?, Letter X). See redemption and evil spirit.

  6. A will to be educated. God does not override our wills; He trains them. “Much becomes clear to us when we learn that He wishes to educate and raise our wills; that there is no love but what is kindled in them, but what is kept alive in them by His Holy Spirit” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Sleep of the Church”). Prayer is the school of this education: St. John is teaching “entire dependence on the Will of God, entire confidence in that Will” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVIII). See education and Spirit.

Neighbouring words

  • self-will (antonym). The will of the creature set up against the Will that made it; the sin of the world.
  • obedience (the will’s right state). The human will subdued to the Divine, and so made free.
  • sacrifice (the Will’s expression). Christ’s surrender of His will to the Father’s, which is the Father’s own Will of self-giving.
  • decree · election (false neighbours). Maurice refuses to let the Will of God shrink to an arbitrary decree.
  • Father (the Will’s character). The Will is known only in the relation of Father and Son.

Scripture. Not my will, but thine, be done (Luke 22:42). Lo, I come to do thy will, O God (Hebrews 10:9). Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven (Matthew 6:10).
