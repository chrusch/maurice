A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

body

resurrection · resurrection of the body · spiritual body

Frequency in the corpus: resurrection in several hundred paragraphs; the resurrection of the body, the spiritual body and the body redeemed in about 40. Essay VIII of Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”, and the Dedicatory Letter to The Doctrine of Sacrifice are the main sources.

In brief. Maurice holds the resurrection of the body without modification, against the charge that he made it merely spiritual. But the body raised is not a gathering of decayed relics at some distant day. It is the living powers and principles of which our bodies consist, redeemed from the corruption that belongs to them because we have sinned. Christ’s body saw no corruption and so showed what the human body truly is. His Resurrection was not proof that men are immortal; it declared men sons of God, and redeemed the body and the earth as truly as the spirit, which makes every physical study a sacred one. The Church too is a spiritual body, the members gathered in their one Head.

Shades of meaning

  1. Not a proof of immortality. The Unitarian took Christ’s Resurrection as proof “that men are immortal, that they do not perish altogether, when the breath leaves their bodies” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). Maurice answers that if He rose to prove that, “He would have proved what the conscience of men confessed, with trembling and horror” (same). Its meaning was “to show us that God claimed us as His sons and daughters” (same). See justification and Father.

  2. The body redeemed too. “The redemption of the body was quite as much a part of His work for man; its redemption from death, the grave, and hell” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). In the risen Christ who eats the broiled fish and honeycomb, “The body is real as well as the spirit. The body is redeemed from death as well as the spirit” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXVIII). Reading the Easter appearances as metaphors would weaken “the great doctrine of the Resurrection of the Body, which is so integral and mighty a portion of Christ’s Gospel to mankind” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “Christ Meeting with Doubters”). See redemption and death.

  3. Living powers, not relics. Orthodox teachers had emptied the doctrine “by substituting for the resurrection of the living powers and principles of which our bodies consist, the renovation of those elements which were the signs of its decay, its curse, its death” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter). Yet the notion of “a resurrection of relics” hides “a very deep conviction that the body of our humiliation must be identical with the body redeemed and renewed” (Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”). Mourners at a deathbed know that the friend “did not derive his powers of speech, any more than the celestial smile which still remains on the clay, from that clay” (Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”). See life and fact.

  4. The body that saw no corruption. Christ “showed forth in that body of His what the human body is” (Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”), so we should not make the corrupt accidents of our bodies the rule by which we judge the body. “Christ is the Lord of Man, the Life-giver of Man, the True Man; Adam is the root of his individuality, of his disease, of his death” (Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”). See Head and Incarnation.

  5. In the twinkling of an eye. Maurice reads the change St. Paul describes as happening at death, not after a long sleep: men “instead of sleeping in their graves, would be changed in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye” (Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”). The trump of the Archangel is “symbolical; that to give it an actual material counterpart would be gross and superstitious in the last and lowest degree” (Theological Essays, “The Resurrection of the Son of God from Death, the Grave, and Hell”). See eternal and sign.

  6. Body and earth regenerated. We have failed because “we have not confessed that the body and the earth are as much redeemed and regenerated by Him as our spirits, or intellectual powers” (Theological Essays, “On Regeneration”). Hence “a man who fully believes in Christ’s Regeneration must regard every physical study as a sacred study—physiology as the most sacred of all” (same). See nature and knowledge.

  7. The Church a spiritual body. The word body also names the Church, which the Corinthians failed to see: “they would not understand the functions of a Spiritual body; they were merely acting like a set of atoms accidentally associated” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The First Epistle to the Corinthians”). Of St. Paul absent from them: “It is a spiritual body. As spiritual creatures they have entered into it. Conditions of time and space do not circumscribe it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “Excommunication”). See Church and member.

Neighbouring words

  • flesh (what the body is freed from). The body is raised out of its thraldom to the flesh.
  • immortality (false substitute). Mere survival, which the conscience dreads.
  • relics · remains (false notion). The decayed elements that are not the body raised.
  • regeneration (wider work). The renewal of body, earth and spirit in Christ.

Scripture. I believe in the resurrection of the body (the Apostles’ Creed). It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body (1 Corinthians 15:44). We shall all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump (1 Corinthians 15:51–52). Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself (Luke 24:39).
