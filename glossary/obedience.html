A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

obedience

obey · obedient · cheerful obedience

Frequency in the corpus: obedience, obey and obedient in about 390 paragraphs. The Lincoln’s Inn sermons, The Doctrine of Sacrifice and The Unity of the New Testament are the main sources.

In brief. Obedience for Maurice is not the opposite of freedom but its condition. Man alone among creatures has the power of disobedience and so the privilege of cheerful, voluntary obedience; the stars and rivers obey by law, men by consent. The obedience of Christ to His Father is the ground of all: it rectifies the disorder that self-will has made. Obedience cannot purchase a blessing, and an obedience done to be seen is the Pharisee’s. True obedience is trust: it confesses its Master, consents to be raised, and becomes obedient.

Shades of meaning

  1. The condition of freedom. Charity “must be the very law under which God has placed him, obedience to which is the condition of his freedom” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “The Foundation and the Building”). God’s operations “are upon our wills, to make them obedient, to make them free” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII), the two aims being one. “Obedience and Freedom meet and embrace each other when we believe that He asks us to yield up our wills as sacrifices to His Who has first made the great sacrifice for us” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “The Honour and Degradation of an Official”). See law and will.

  2. Cheerful, voluntary obedience. Maurice reads Romans 13 as the picture of “a beautiful order extending from the highest to the lowest, through every rank of creatures, man being the one who has the power of disobedience, the privilege of cheerful, voluntary, obedience” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The Epistle to the Romans”). Nature obeys too, for “there is a living and eternal order which all the changes and vicissitudes of nature obey”, but in men “true power is exercised in obedience to this order” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface). Obedience given without love becomes servility: where a loving will is not acknowledged, “cheerful obedience to a master … becomes servitude to a tyrant” (The Religions of the World, Lecture V). See constitution and king.

  3. The obedience of Christ. The Son’s obedience to the Father is the heart of the Atonement and of sacrifice. “The Apostle bids us behold the Lamb of God who takes away that sin by obedience to His Father, by emptying Himself of Glory, by humbling Himself to the likeness of the lowest of His creatures” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIV). “That this obedience should be the means of rectifying the disorders of the universe, of bringing back the state of things which self-will has broken and disturbed, of re-establishing the kingdom and righteousness of God” is what we should expect (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII). In the Temptation “the Son of God once more claims the right to obey a commandment—the right to trust and to depend. Once more He claims that right for us” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture V). Obedience is a right, not only a duty. See sacrifice and Atonement.

  4. Obedience as trust, not merit. The Pharisee practised “a meritorious obedience” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 2, “The Sermon on the Mount: Its General Principle”), done to be seen of men; and the rich young man learns that keeping the commandments “cannot purchase the transcendent blessing he is in search of” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XXII). True obedience begins in faith. The spirit “believes that it has nothing in itself, that it has all in Him; consents to be raised to its true condition; becomes obedient. Any course but this must be one of self-will” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 6, “God the Justifier of Man”). See justification and faith.

  5. Obedience and truth. Christ shows that His truth is tested by obedience: He does not come in His own name, He does His Father’s Will, and “to fulfil that Will He becomes a servant. Here is Truth wedded to Obedience, the characteristic of the Woman” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XX). The masculine and feminine virtues meet in Him. See truth.

Neighbouring words

  • freedom · liberty (partner). Not obedience’s opposite but its fruit.
  • self-will (antonym). The disobedience of a creature that chooses to be apart from its Creator.
  • submission · subjection (near synonyms). Used where the stress falls on yielding the will.
  • servitude · slavery (corruption). What obedience becomes when the ruler is not known as loving.
  • merit (false neighbour). Obedience that seeks to purchase a reward is the Pharisee’s.

Scripture. He humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross (Philippians 2:8). By the obedience of one shall many be made righteous (Romans 5:19). Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered (Hebrews 5:8).
