A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Head

Head of every man · Head of the race

Frequency in the corpus: head 370, in all senses; Head of every man 22; Head of the race or of our race 13; Head of humanity, of man or of men 16; Head of the Church or of the body 15.

In brief. Christ’s relation to all men, from 1 Corinthians 11:3, the head of every man is Christ. For Maurice it is a fact about mankind, not a privilege of believers: Christ is Head of the Church because he is Head of every man. The word carries his doctrine of humanity, his doctrine of the Church, and his case against any human head set up in Christ’s place.

Shades of meaning

  1. Head of every man. The universal sense, and the most frequent: “It was a lie to say that Christ was not the Head of every man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Devil and God”). It is the Gospel for all nations: “A Gospel that God is the Father of all in Jesus Christ, Who is the head of every man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “A Straitened Faith and a Narrow Gospel”).

  2. Head of the Church because Head of every man. The order of the two claims matters. The Church is “that universal body politic of which Christ is the Head, because He is the Head of every man” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Body and the Members”). It is “first of all an order, a divine, eternal order, grounded on the revelation of Christ as the head of man” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The First Epistle to the Corinthians”).

  3. Head of the race. Christ stands to mankind as Adam was thought to stand: the one in whom the whole race is included. He is “marked out as the Head of the race” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture V). Hence justification is common: Christ’s resurrection “declared Him to be the Son of man, the Head of man, and therefore that His justification was the justification of each man” (Theological Essays, “On Justification by Faith”).

  4. The common Head the Gospel reveals. Religions divide; the Gospel discovers what men already share. “A Gospel is the unveiling or discovery to men of a common Head” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface).

  5. The false visible head. Any human head of humanity or of the Church is a counterfeit of this one. “He who professes to be the Visible Head of humanity has no power to bind its limbs together” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Preface); “a Church which tries to organize itself into an independent visible society with a visible head must by its very nature encourage idolatry” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Sacredness of Common Morality”).

  6. Human headships. Fathers and kings are heads in their own orders, and Maurice keeps the offices distinct. Abraham’s covenant is made “with a man expressly and emphatically as the head of a family” (The Kingdom of Christ I, ch. III); “The King who loves his subjects best, will know that he is the just head of a Nation, not the gracious parent of a Family” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Homily II: On Theology and Government in India”).

Neighbouring words

  • centre (gloss). The world lacks a centre and the Church witnesses to it; see Church, sense 2, and world, sense 3.
  • root (gloss). “The Root and Head of Humanity” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Judgment Day an Object of Intense Desire”).
  • body, members (correlates). Sense 2.
  • Son of Man (the name that goes with it). The Name Christ bears “is not that of some separate individual, but of the Man, the Brother and Head of Men” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XIX). See Son of Man.
  • King (parallel). Head of the body, King of the kingdom; see kingdom.
  • Adam (the old headship). Sense 3.

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 11:3, the head of every man is Christ, the source of sense 1; Ephesians 1:22, gave him to be the head over all things to the church; Colossians 1:18, he is the head of the body, the church; Ephesians 4:15–16.
