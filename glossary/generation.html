A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

generation

generations · from generation to generation · this generation

Frequency in the corpus: generation and generations in about 280 paragraphs, spread across the sermons, the lectures and The Kingdom of Christ.

In brief. Maurice uses the word in three ways. First, God is the Same from generation to generation, a living God and not one who acted once in the past. Second, the forms He has given, Creeds, sacraments and common prayers, bind each generation to every other, so that the language of one generation can be the language of all. Third, each generation has its own questions, its own sins and its own judgment: no man is of use to his generation unless he takes up the questions it is asking, and the judgment the Baptist and the Apostles preached fell on the generation they spoke to.

Shades of meaning

  1. The Same from generation to generation. The treasure of Israel “was not a treasure given generations ago, and then withdrawn. That which was given generations ago was the testimony concerning a Being Who was the Same from generation to generation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 3, “God’s Government of Us; its Ground and its End”). “The prophets always describe Him as the Living God—the God from generation to generation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel 5, “Christ Among the Doctors”), and so the question is whether He is with us as He was with them. See life and I AM.

  2. Forms that bind the generations. God “has embodied them in forms which from generation to generation have been witnesses of his love to the humble and the meek” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”). The Creed is “an heirloom of the Church, which has been preserved to men by the providence of God, and which each generation of her members is bound to watch over” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Of the Catholic Church and the Romish System”). Since the sin of division is the same in every age, “there seems no reason why the language of one generation, in confessing this sin, should not be the language of all” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Forms of Worship”). See Creed, worship and sign.

  3. Serving one’s own generation. “No man, I think, will ever be of much use to his generation, who does not apply himself mainly to the questions which are occupying those who belong to it” (The Kingdom of Christ I, Dedication). Maurice praises Coleridge for this, and adds that “as we who belong to a younger generation have inherited these questions, we inherit also the wisdom which dealt with them” (same). See seeking and history.

  4. The judgment on a generation. Maurice reads the New Testament’s warnings literally, as judgments on a particular age. The Baptist, our Lord and every Apostle “was a preacher of judgment, of a judgment nigh at hand, of one coming upon the generation of which they were speaking” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XV), and he thinks the Church has lost the sense of judgment because “we have not taken their words literally enough” (same). Times may be at hand “to men in every generation which may render it most needful that they should try to enter into the meaning of the times which were at hand in his generation” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture I). See judgment and dispensation.

  5. Sins and legacies handed down. Selfishness “accumulates from age to age. The sins of the fathers are visited upon the children to the third and fourth generation” (Theological Essays, “On the Evil Spirit”). Yet goodness is handed down too: the world casts out the self-sacrificing man, but each generation “accepts some new precious legacy from the self-sacrificing man, whose faith it has cast out as evil” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XVI). See sin and sacrifice.

Neighbouring words

  • age · ages (near synonym). The period of a dispensation, whose end the prophets announce.
  • fathers · children (the link). The family is the bond through which generations inherit.
  • tradition (what is handed on). The forms each generation is bound to watch over.
  • contemporaries · the time (one’s own generation). The questions a teacher must answer.

Scripture. Lord, thou hast been our dwelling place in all generations (Psalm 90:1). His mercy is on them that fear him from generation to generation (Luke 1:50). This generation shall not pass away, till all be fulfilled (Luke 21:32).
