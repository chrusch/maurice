A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

righteousness

righteous · the Righteous Being

Frequency in the corpus: righteousness 839; righteous 594.

In brief. First of all the character of God, “the Righteous Being”, who can only do right and whose whole government aims to make men right. Human righteousness is not a possession of one’s own but trust in that righteous God and participation in a righteousness that is in another, in Christ. For Maurice the Bible’s theology is the unveiling of the Righteous Being to man, and righteousness and love turn out to be one.

Shades of meaning

  1. The Righteous Being. The Jew “believed that the God and Judge of all the earth was a righteous being, and could only do right; and that the whole scheme and purpose of His government was to make men right” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture IV). The Bible’s theology is “the unveiling of the Righteous Being to the heart and conscience of the only creature that is capable of being righteous” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “Theology and Morality”).

  2. A God who treats man as made for right. Genesis sets before us “a righteous Being, who holds discourse with His creature, who treats him as a being made for right, and capable of following right” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon I).

  3. Trust in a righteous God. Human righteousness begins as trust: “The righteous man of the Old and New Testament equally is the man who trusted in a righteous God; the unrighteous man is the distrustful man” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIII). “If that sensualist claims the deliverance which Christ offers him, if he seeks the righteousness which is not his own, he becomes a saint” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Sacramental Character of the Bible”).

  4. Righteousness in a Person. God’s righteousness is shown not chiefly in law but “in the Person of Jesus Christ; and that, so manifested, it is effectual to make men righteous” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon X). To exchange this for rules is ruinous: to substitute “the righteousness of letters for the righteousness of a Person, the righteousness which merely condemns the sinner for the righteousness which saves him out of sin, is to destroy the life of the Church as well as the life of the individual” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The Pastoral Epistles”).

  5. Declared in forgiveness. Maurice rejects the scheme of “a propitiation that does not declare God’s righteousness in the forgiveness of sins, but which makes it possible for Him to forgive sins, though He is righteous” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon X). The same revelation is also judgment: “the revelation of God’s righteousness for the redemption of sinners is at the same time the revelation of the wrath of God against all unrighteousness” (Theological Essays, “Eternal Life and Eternal Death”).

  6. One with love. In Christ’s sacrifice, “all the seeming contradiction between Righteousness and Love disappears” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIII). See love.

  7. Acts from a person. Righteous deeds come from a righteous nature, born of God: “Righteous acts spring from a righteous person, as sound healthy branches proceed from a sound healthy root” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XI).

Neighbouring words

  • trust, faith (its human form). Sense 3.
  • love (its identity). Sense 6; see love.
  • law, letter (its lesser form). Sense 4; see law.
  • justification (its declaration to man). Sense 5.
  • wrath (its other face). Sense 5.
  • Mediator, Christ (its embodiment). Sense 4; see Mediator.
  • distrust (unrighteousness). Sense 3.

Scripture. Romans 3:21–22, now the righteousness of God without the law is manifested … even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ, the text of Sermon X in The Doctrine of Sacrifice; Genesis 18:25, Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?; 1 John 2:29, every one that doeth righteousness is born of him (sense 7); Romans 1:17–18, the revelation of righteousness and of wrath (sense 5).
