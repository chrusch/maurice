A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Father

fatherly · our Father

Frequency in the corpus: father 2,381 (the Father 801; our Father 101); fatherly 27.

In brief. The name of God that Christ reveals, and the ground of every other name. For Maurice the Godhead is not self-willed power but a Father and a Son in a relation of love and obedience. Because God is Father in his only begotten Son, he is the Father of all men, and the one who knows this has a right, and a duty, to arise and go to him. The fatherhood of God is also the pattern of the family, and every human father bears his name.

Shades of meaning

  1. Father of the Son. Maurice reads the Godhead from the obedience of the Son. The Apostles “learnt from Him not to think of the Godhead as self-willed power or sovereignty. They thought of a Father and a Son” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture X). The same lecture: “They could not see the Will of the Father except in the submission of the Son.”

  2. Father of all. What is true for one is true for every man: “I must assert that God is my Father in Jesus Christ His only begotten Son; and if mine, then the Father of every one of those who are related to me in this or in any land” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Fatherly Character of God the Only Ground of Repentance, Consolation, Hope”). To see the Son of Man is to see him: “He that has owned Jesus as the Son of Man, has in Him seen the Father of All” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Advent of Christ, and the Eucharist”). The Sermon on the Mount teaches “that God is the Father of all; that His Kingdom is over all” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture X).

  3. The ground of repentance. The prodigal does not make himself a son before he returns. The Lincoln’s Inn sermon puts the words in the mouth of anyone: none can say, “I must make myself a son; I must establish my relationship to God before I claim the full sense and virtue of them” (same sermon). The same argument in The Kingdom of Christ: “If we are not allowed to call ourselves children of God, how can we be told to arise and go to our Father?” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”).

  4. The Father sought and revealed. Mankind had been looking for him: the Name hallowed in the Lord’s Prayer is “the revelation of a Father whom men were feeling after and could not find” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon II, “Hallowed Be Thy Name”). Behind all human spirits there must be “a Father of Spirits from Whom they issue forth” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Fatherly Character of God the Only Ground of Repentance, Consolation, Hope”).

  5. Our Father. The plural is a law of prayer: “thou must call upon a Father; thou must not dare to say my, but our” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “Forms of Worship”). “When He bade them say, ‘Our Father,’ He explained the nature of prayer” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XV).

  6. The earthly father. The household reflects the divine order. Under the old covenant “each head of a household was to present Him in that character through his own acts” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Dedicatory Letter).

  7. The false father. The word has a dark use, and Maurice sets it beside the true one. “Every one should be told that he has a Father in Heaven, whose name he is to hallow; and then he will discover that there is a father of all the evil deeds he has ever thought, the father of all that is dark, superstitious, foul, in the universe” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Devil and God”).

Neighbouring words

  • Son, only begotten Son (the one in whom he is known). Sense 1; see Son of Man.
  • sovereignty, self-willed power (the false idea it replaces). Sense 1; see self-will.
  • children, sons, prodigal (his claim on men). Sense 3.
  • Name (his revealed character). Sense 4; see Name.
  • love, fatherly love (his nature). “Thy fatherly love to me is not determined by my sense of it, my belief of it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Fatherly Character of God the Only Ground of Repentance, Consolation, Hope”). See love.
  • family, household (the human image). Sense 6.
  • Spirit (the bond with the Son). See Spirit, sense 2.

Scripture. Luke 15:18, I will arise and go to my father, behind sense 3; Matthew 6:9, Our Father which art in heaven; John 5:19, The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do, quoted in Lecture X of St. John (sense 1); Hebrews 12:9, the Father of spirits; John 8:44, Ye are of your father the devil (sense 7).
