A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

revelation

reveal · unveiling

Frequency in the corpus: revelation 1,398; reveal and its forms 497.

In brief. God’s disclosure of himself to men: not the supply of information about God, nor a religion, but the unveiling of a Person, the Father, in the Son, by the Spirit. Maurice insists on the literal sense of the Greek apokalypsis: the drawing back of a veil from what is already there. His controversy with H. L. Mansel in What is Revelation? (1859) turns on whether such a direct manifestation of God is possible at all.

Shades of meaning

  1. The unveiling of a Person, not a system. The central sense: “The answers, I repeat it, were not veils; they were a Revelation, or Unveiling; and that Revelation, or Unveiling, was not of a System or of a Religion, but of Him who had said, “Let not the rich man glory in his riches” (What is Revelation?, Sermon II, “Christ among the Doctors”).

  2. A direct manifestation of God. The issue with Mansel: Mansel maintained “the “very reverse” of the doctrine that Revelation means a direct manifestation of the Nature of God” (What is Revelation?, Preface), and Maurice wrote the book to hold to that doctrine.

  3. The ground of action, history and knowledge. Revelation is not a rare supernatural addition but the condition of man’s life: “Thus Revelation, or the declaration of God’s mind and will—of God himself—to man, is assumed as the ground of action, and history, and knowledge” (The Religions of the World, Lecture V).

  4. The Bible as the history of an unveiling. “The Bible may be read as a continuous history, and that it is the history of the unveiling of God to the creature whom He has made in His image” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). The same lecture speaks of “a gradual unveiling of the divine purpose and divine life to man, a gradual education of men” (Lecture I).

  5. The revelation of a Father through a Son. Its content is filial: the Christian revelation “professes to be the revelation of a Father through a Son, who has taken the nature of men upon Him” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture II). In The Doctrine of Sacrifice the veil “has been withdrawn which hid His divine nature, His relation to the Eternal Father, from us” by Christ’s self-sacrifice (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon VII).

  6. Revelation to the conscience. Revelation addresses the inner man and changes him: “when there comes to the conscience the revelation of a reconciled and reconciling God … all is changed. That which was sought in nothingness, is found in a Father” (What is Revelation?, Sermon I, “The Magians”).

  7. Apocalypse: the unveiling of the Son of Man, and of the Name. In the lectures on the Apocalypse the book is “the book of Unveiling; the Divine mind and purpose are gradually revealed in the dispensation of the ages. An unveiling of the very name of God, the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, is involved in that” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture X).

  8. Unveiling in history: judgments as revelations. Historical crises are unveilings of Christ’s rule: the fall of Jerusalem “was an unveiling of Jesus Christ—a clear manifestation of His dominion over men, of His presence in the midst of them” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, IV, “Waiting for Christ”).

  9. Revelation as distinct from religion. Religion is man’s seeking; revelation is God’s finding. The phrase “Natural and Revealed Religion” itself became impossible for him, though “Butler’s teaching respecting a revelation of God through the Constitution of Nature and through Man” remained precious (What is Revelation?, Letter V).

Neighbouring words

  • unveiling, manifestation, discovery (glosses). Unveiling is his English for the Greek; manifestation and discovery (in its old sense of disclosure) are near-synonyms.
  • religion, system, notion (opposites). Sense 1 and sense 9. See religion, system.
  • Father, Son, Name (content). Senses 5 and 7.
  • conscience (the faculty addressed). Sense 6. See conscience.
  • history, education, dispensation (the form it takes). Sense 4.
  • Absolute, Infinite (Mansel’s terms). Sense 2.
  • sacrifice (the act in which God is revealed). Sense 5.
  • judgment (an unveiling). Sense 8. See judgment.

Scripture. Revelation 1:1, The Revelation of Jesus Christ (KJV; Greek apokalypsis, unveiling); 2 Corinthians 3:18 and 4:6, the veil taken away and the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ; Jeremiah 9:23–24, let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me, the text Maurice quotes in sense 1.
