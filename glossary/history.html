A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

history

the Bible a history · the history of mankind

Frequency in the corpus: history in about 1,000 paragraphs, with God, revelation or the Bible close by in about 130.

In brief. For Maurice history is where God shows himself. The Bible is to be read as a continuous history, the history of the unveiling of God to the creature made in his image, and above all the history of a nation. That national history interprets the history of every nation, since each has a calling from God. The history of mankind is an upward education through work, suffering and sin. Other books give the records of a religion; the Bible gives the history of God’s dealings, his goodness, forbearance and longsuffering to a people. Maurice will not deny history any more than theology, since history has expounded theological truth.

Shades of meaning

  1. The Bible as history. “The Bible may be read as a continuous history, and that it is the history of the unveiling of God to the creature whom He has made in His image” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture I). He calls the Scriptures a history because he finds in them “a gradual unveiling of the divine purpose and divine life to man” (same). Men will trace it “as the progressive history of God’s revelations to a family, to a nation, and to mankind” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Sacramental Character of the Bible”). See Bible · Scripture and revelation.

  2. God’s dealings, not a religion. “The history of the Nation was the history of a God showing kindness to an indifferent race, forbearance to a provoking race, longsuffering to an obstinate race. In other books you have the records of a religion” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “God’s Government of Us; its Ground and its End”). The Scriptures do not set forth “the history of a man seeking for God, but of God seeking for men” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “National Holiness”). The prodigal’s story would end in the stye “if it were only a history of man’s dealings. Thank God it is not so. It is the history of a Father’s dealings with a child” (The Gospel of the Kingdom of Heaven, Lecture XIX). See religion and seeking.

  3. A national history. “There is no topic which has more engaged my attention in these volumes than the national history of the Bible” (The Kingdom of Christ I, Dedication). “In the Old Testament every truth is brought out in relation to the events of a national history. Their own time interpreted the Scriptures to the Reformers, and the Scriptures in turn interpreted their own time” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Pure Protestantism”). See nation.

  4. The key to all nations. St. Stephen “vindicated the truth of the history of his own nation, by showing how it helped to explain the history of all nations. Each one had a calling from God” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Circumcision and the Uncircumcision Merged in Christ”). The Bible is “the history of the establishment of a universal and spiritual kingdom, of that kingdom which God had ever intended for men” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “The Scriptural View of This Constitution”). See kingdom and Catholic.

  5. An upward history. The disobedience in Eden is the commencement “of the actual history of mankind. A history, not—if the Bible is to be taken literally and in its order—of a downward, but of an upward progress; an education of men, through work, suffering, sin, to the discovery of their real capacities” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Old and New Paradise”). See education.

  6. History expounds theology. Finding sin among the orthodox and virtue among heretics, “I am bound to believe God was using both. I dare not deny History any more than the Theological truth, which, I believe, History has expounded. That truth will suffer if I do” (Theological Essays, “Note on the Athanasian Creed”). The inquirer seeks “some law, which may connect together the different facts he has observed in ecclesiastical or general history” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The Epistle to the Hebrews”). See fact and principle.

  7. The book of human history. The sealed book of the Apocalypse may be called “the book which contains and interprets human history; the book which claims the kingdoms of the earth for God” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture V). God has given judgments in “the history of His Church” against every attempt to steady his ark (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Advent of Christ, and the Eucharist”). See judgment.

  8. History as a trial of faith. Nothing causes so much unbelief “as a comparison of the hopes which Scripture seems to hold out of the effects that should follow the revelation of Christ with the history of the world since He appeared in it” (Theological Essays, “On the Personality and Teaching of the Holy Spirit”). The essay answers it from the Spirit’s continuing presence and teaching. See Spirit and generations.

Neighbouring words

  • revelation, unveiling (what history shows). Sense 1.
  • nation (its unit). Senses 3 and 4; see nation.
  • education (what history is from God’s side). Sense 5.
  • religion (its contrast). Sense 2.
  • law (what connects its facts). Sense 6.
  • generations (its succession). See generations.

Scripture. Acts 7:2–53, Stephen’s speech (sense 4); Acts 17:26, hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation (sense 4); Romans 15:4, whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning (sense 1); Revelation 5:1–5, the book sealed with seven seals (sense 7); Exodus 34:6, merciful and gracious, longsuffering (sense 2); Luke 15:11–32, the prodigal son (sense 2).
