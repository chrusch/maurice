A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

brotherhood

brother · the Universal Brother · fraternity

Frequency in the corpus: brotherhood, universal brother and fraternity in about 40 paragraphs; brother and brethren in many hundreds.

In brief. Brotherhood, in Maurice, is grounded on Fatherhood and on Christ the Universal Brother, the Head and bond of all men. Without confessing a Father there is no brotherhood, which is why he takes the French cry of Fraternity seriously and calls it a duty to find out how it may become real. Under the Gospel brotherhood takes the place of neighbourhood: before, it was sin to hate a neighbour; now it is sin to hate a man. Brotherhood is no feeling about the Christian body at large but help to the particular brother, and it binds rich and poor together. Its enemies are the self-will that destroys a world made for brotherhood, and those who will not belong to a brotherhood but assert their independence.

Shades of meaning

  1. From Fatherhood. “The acknowledgment of brotherhood must start from the acknowledgment of Fatherhood. If we believe that we have a Father Who has as much to do with one latitude as with another, Who has established a Family which He means to fill the earth, Who has made us one body in His Son,—then there is an extinction of time and space which no electricity can cause” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Fatherly Character of God the Only Ground of Repentance, Consolation, Hope”). We cannot fault a people for hoping for brotherhood on earth; we can only tell them “that there is no brotherhood, unless we begin with confessing a Father” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon IV). See Father and family.

  2. The Universal Brother. “Jesus Christ, born of the seed of David, circumcised according to the law, enters into the wants of men, the sorrows of men, the death of men. He is the Universal Brother” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VI). There must be “some universal Brother, to whom each particular brother owed his place in the family and the affections which corresponded to it” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV). Christ is “the Head and bond of a universal brotherhood” (Theological Essays, “On Regeneration”). See Head and Son of Man.

  3. Brotherhood in place of neighbourhood. In Christ “we are come into a new and higher state; the state not only of neighbourhood but of brotherhood.” “Before, it was a sin to hate neighbours; now, it is a sin to hate men; because this brotherhood of men with men is made known” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VI). “For to hate a brother is to walk in darkness. It is to hide ourselves from Him who is our great common brother” (same). See light and love.

  4. Fraternity. Lecturing on the French Revolution, he has “often to touch upon this subject of Fraternity or Brotherhood. I hope we none of us merely touch upon it; I hope we wish in our hearts to understand it that we may practise it” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture VI). Of 1789 and 1848, a special duty is laid upon us “to find out what they mean, and how that of which they speak may become a reality for us” (same). See society and nation.

  5. Grounded in God’s will. “The ground of that brotherhood, which the passage is setting forth, lies in the will of Him who has created all things” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon XV). The baptized convert “could not doubt, that from the time of his baptism he was free of that brotherhood of which his Lord was the head” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Signs of a Spiritual Society”). See baptism.

  6. The particular brother. “You may have very ecstatic feelings about the Christian brotherhood at large; but are you ready to help that particular brother, who is lying destitute there, not with feelings, but with a little of the actual food and raiment that he is in need of?” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture XIII). See love · charity.

  7. Rich and poor in one. The Gospel “has blended into one brotherhood the rich and the poor, the mighty and the mean among mankind” (What Is Revelation?, Letter VIII). “Christ’s brotherhood with man answered to all the anticipations which prophets had formed of their king” (The Unity of the New Testament, “St. Matthew”). See king.

  8. Destroyed by self-will. We are conscious of “capacities of sympathy and fellowship, which are destroyed by self-will”, and of “the conditions of a world, created for brotherhood, destroyed by the same self-will” (The Lord’s Prayer, Sermon VIII). The Corinthians did not feel brotherhood: “they were merely acting like a set of atoms accidentally associated” (The Unity of the New Testament, “The First Epistle to the Corinthians”). Those who left St. John’s Church “did not choose to belong to a brotherhood; what they cared was to assert their independence of others” (The Epistles of St. John, Lecture IX). See self-will and sect.

Neighbouring words

  • Fatherhood (its ground). Sense 1; see Father.
  • neighbourhood (its older, narrower form). Sense 3.
  • fraternity (its political name). Sense 4.
  • family, body (its forms). Senses 1 and 5; see family and member.
  • fellowship (its life). Sense 8; see fellowship.
  • atoms, independence (its denial). Sense 8.

Scripture. Hebrews 2:10–11, he is not ashamed to call them brethren (senses 2 and 5); 1 John 2:9–11, He that saith he is in the light, and hateth his brother, is in darkness (sense 3); 1 John 3:17, whoso hath this world’s good, and seeth his brother have need (sense 6); Matthew 23:8–9, all ye are brethren … one is your Father (sense 1); Romans 8:29, the firstborn among many brethren (sense 2); Luke 10:29–37, the good Samaritan (sense 3).
