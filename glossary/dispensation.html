A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

dispensation

old dispensation · new dispensation

Frequency in the corpus: dispensation and dispensations in about 145 paragraphs. Spread across The Kingdom of Christ, the Lincoln’s Inn sermons and Lectures on the Apocalypse.

In brief. A dispensation is one of God’s ordered ages of dealing with men, and especially the Jewish age before Christ and the age that followed it. Maurice uses the word freely but distrusts it. The new dispensation does not swallow up the old: the same God, the same Christ and the same commandments run through both, and the old witnesses to distinction while the new declares union. Its end came with the fall of Jerusalem, and each later age may also have its end. The word goes wrong when “the Christian dispensation” stands in for the living Kingdom of Heaven, or when the Quaker reads “spiritual dispensation” as a reason to cast off signs and sacraments.

Shades of meaning

  1. An age of God’s dealings. “Surely as an age, or, as we sometimes call it, a dispensation of God, advances towards its consummation, the need may become greater” to understand the times St. John foresaw (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture I). The fall of the holy city is “nothing less to the prophet than the end of an age or dispensation. It is the greatest catastrophe that has yet befallen the universe” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture VIII). Christ manifested himself “in the latter days of the old dispensation”, and the apostles “were not deceived in their expectation that the end of an age was at hand” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXII). See eternal, where the age and the eternal are distinguished.

  2. The new does not cancel the old. Jews, Romanists, Protestants and “a large and increasing school of liberal writers” all try “to assert that the new dispensation has swallowed up or subverted the old” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture X). Maurice will not. Of the commandments said to belong “to what people call a former dispensation”, he says: “I hold that they belong to us, I hold it to be just as true of England as it was of Judea” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, X, “The Law of Inheritance”). See law and nation.

  3. The same Christ in both. “The new dispensation was not new because the Christ was now, for the first time, making Himself known. He had been before all worlds; His delights had been with the sons of men” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Christ, Not Christianity, the Deliverance of Mankind”). Nor was the Old a dispensation of fear and the New of love: “I have spoken of the Old dispensation. Is all changed, as we are sometimes told, in the New?” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God”). See Word.

  4. Distinction, then union. The old dispensation bore witness “not against universality, but for distinction; the witness that man is a spiritual being; that the constitution of man is a spiritual constitution” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Blessing and Curse of the Law”). The Law “was a secondary subordinate dispensation, of infinite worth as a means of leading us to that primary truth of our relation to God in this Mediator” (The Doctrine of Sacrifice, Sermon IX). See covenant and constitution · order.

  5. A dispensation of the Spirit. “The Christian kingdom cannot be described as a dispensation of the Spirit if these anticipations were not fulfilled” (Theological Essays, “On the Personality and Teaching of the Holy Spirit”). Pentecost pointed “to the coming in of a universal human dispensation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Universality of the Whitsuntide Message”); the testimony of Scripture “is that the baptism with this Spirit is the end of all God’s dispensations” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “The Help of Believing in a Spirit Who Has Been Given to Us”). See Spirit.

  6. The Quaker reading. Fox believed Christ “came to establish a Spiritual and Universal Dispensation”, and therefore that outward institutions such as circumcision, passover and priesthood were done away (The Kingdom of Christ I, “On the Principles of the Quakers”). Maurice answers that the Quakers “suppose that the Christian Covenant, because it is spiritual, dispenses with that method which was sanctioned by divine authority in the earlier dispensation. We believe that the Christian Covenant, because it is more spiritual than the Jewish, requires another application of the same method” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Eucharist”). See sign and sacrament.

  7. An abstraction for a kingdom. To turn the Kingdom of Heaven into the Christian dispensation “is betaking ourselves to the abstract for the sake of explaining the concrete, to a phrase at once scholastic and indistinct” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “Homily III: On Preaching to the Poor”). In the same way, “We say that we live under a Christian dispensation, that we are brought within the range of Christian influences, and so forth”, which is “good if we render it back into the honest and simple language of Scripture” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture XXIII). See kingdom and system.

  8. A stewardship committed. In St. Paul’s own sense, a dispensation is an office entrusted: “a dispensation was committed to him; it was woe to him if he did not preach the Gospel” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “Faith and Science United in the Work of the Missionary”). Maurice wishes for teachers “who have not merely a dispensation committed to them, but who respond to the mind and will of Him Who has committed it” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “The Honour and Degradation of an Official”). See ordination · ministry.

Neighbouring words

  • age, aeon (its truer name). Sense 1; see eternal.
  • covenant, testament (its content). Senses 2 and 4; see covenant.
  • law, grace (the usual contrast). Senses 2 to 4; see law.
  • economy, system (its scholastic cousins). Sense 7; see system.
  • Kingdom of Heaven (the living name). Sense 7; see kingdom.
  • stewardship (Paul’s sense). Sense 8.

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 9:17, a dispensation of the gospel is committed unto me (sense 8); Ephesians 1:10, the dispensation of the fulness of times (sense 1); Ephesians 3:2, the dispensation of the grace of God which is given me to you-ward (sense 8); Galatians 3:24, the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ (sense 4); Matthew 24:3, the end of the world, which the RV margin gives as the consummation of the age (sense 1).
