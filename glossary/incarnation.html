A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

Incarnation

incarnate

Frequency in the corpus: Incarnation 171.

In brief. God’s union with man in the person of his Son: for Maurice a fact, not a dogma, and the centre from which theology should start. It reveals God’s character, so no language about his love can be too strong. It is the ground of the Church and of the sonship of all men, and it hallows the earth. He blames Calvinism for moving it from the centre and making it a remedy that follows the Fall.

Shades of meaning

  1. The way to know God. “We accept the fact of the Incarnation, because we feel that it is impossible to know the Absolute and Invisible God as man needs to know Him, and craves to know Him, without an Incarnation” (Theological Essays, “The Incarnation”). The same paragraph adds two more reasons: men need a Son of Man “in all points, tempted like as we are”, and a redemption “for humanity from all the plagues by which it is tormented”.

  2. The revelation of God’s character. “If we receive the Incarnation of Christ as the revelation of God’s mind and character to men, any language which has ever been used or can ever be used to denote the fulness and universality of the Divine Love and Compassion, instead of being exaggerated, instead of requiring to be modified and qualified, must be tame and cold” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “Christmas Charities”). See revelation.

  3. The centre of divinity. Maurice reports, and shares, the conviction “that the doctrine of the Incarnation had been strangely kept out of sight in all Protestant systems; that it is the centre of all divinity; the deeper mystery of the Trinity being at once the foundation upon which it rests, and the truth, to the full knowledge and fruition of which it is to lead us” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “On the Tendency of the Religious, Philosophical, and Political Movements”). See Trinity.

  4. Not a consequence of the Fall. Calvinism “makes the fall of man the central point of its divinity: it treats the incarnation, and all the facts which manifest the Son of God to men, as merely growing out of this” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The English Church and the Systems Which Divide It”). In those systems “the idea of the Incarnation was deposed from the place which it had occupied in the older divinity of the Church” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Unitarianism”).

  5. The foundation of the Church. “The Fathers actually regarded the Incarnation—Luther wished to regard it—as the foundation of the Church; Calvin sought for this foundation in individual election” (The Kingdom of Christ I, “Pure Protestantism”). See Church.

  6. Sonship for all. Its purpose was to claim men as sons: “He came to assert for those, whom he was not ashamed to call His brethren, the dignity of Sons of God. And as this was the ultimate object of His Incarnation and Humiliation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “Christmas Charities”). The privilege is common: nothing may be claimed “which the Incarnation and Death of Christ have not made the common inheritance of all” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “The Three Things Which Abide”).

  7. The redemption of the earth. Those who despise the earth “cannot think the Incarnation of Christ is really what in our creeds and confessions we declare it to be: else they must suppose that it was the vindication and redemption of this earth for its Creator” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel VI, “The New Heavens and the New Earth”).

  8. A fact, not a dogma. When Stephen saw the Son of Man, “It was not a doctrine of the Incarnation that he acknowledged in that hour, a mere doctrine would have stood him in little stead. It was a Person” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel V, “The Witnesses for the Glorified Son of Man”).

Neighbouring words

  • Mediator, union of Godhead and manhood (its result). See Mediator.
  • Trinity (its foundation). Sense 3; see Trinity.
  • Fall, election (the rival starting points). Senses 4 and 5.
  • Son of Man (the one incarnate). Sense 8; see Son of Man.
  • sonship, brethren (its gift). Sense 6.
  • earth (what it hallows). Sense 7.
  • Advent (its season). “The Church does not distinguish the Advent of our Lord from His Incarnation” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Judgment Day an Object of Intense Desire”).

Scripture. John 1:14, the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us; Hebrews 4:15, in all points tempted like as we are, quoted in sense 1; Hebrews 2:11, he is not ashamed to call them brethren, behind sense 6; Hebrews 2:14, that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, quoted in the essay on the Atonement.
