A Glossary of Maurice’s Terms

absolution

absolve · Absolver

Frequency in the corpus: absolution, absolve and their forms 58. The key sources are The Kingdom of Christ* II, “The Ministry”, and the Lincoln’s Inn sermon “The Eucharist Considered in its Relation to Confession and Absolution”.*

In brief. The setting free of the conscience from the bonds of sin. Maurice treats absolution as the Church’s chief work for men and as the office every Christian minister has claimed in some form. But the true Absolver is Christ, the Son who makes men free by bringing them into sonship; the minister absolves only as his representative, in the congregation, joined with the Eucharist, never as a vicar with powers of his own. A daily absolution is what every man needs. The counterfeit is a priestly absolution that does not set free at all.

Shades of meaning

  1. What the Church exists for. “What else does the Church exist for, so far as men are concerned, but to absolve or set free their consciences from the chains with which they are bound? What greater negligence can it be charged with than that of not performing this office? What greater sin than that of forging chains instead of breaking them?” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel II, “The Faith of Catholics”). See conscience and freedom.

  2. A universal claim of ministers. “The most remarkable power which these ministers have claimed, and that on account of which the greatest homage has been paid to them, is the power of absolving or setting free. This claim has in a manner been universal. Luther believed that he was to absolve as much as Tetzel” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Ministry”). Even those who say the minister’s sole office is to preach say so “because he believes this is the way to absolve” (same).

  3. Christ the true Absolver. The aim is “bringing men more directly in contact with the true and unseen Absolver; the end of making His ministers understand that they are nothing except as representatives of Him” (same). The absolution we need is sonship: “It is the Son Who makes us free, because He brings us the adoption of sons” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel III, “The Bible the Book of Freedom, Personal, National, Universal”). See Mediator and Son of Man.

  4. A daily need. In the ordinary struggle to do right we learn that “we want an ever-present helper and deliverer, a daily absolution, a renewal of strength hour by hour” (same). In the daily service, whenever the minister pronounces that God pardons and absolves, “he means, God would set you free from your falsehood” (Sermons Preached in Country Churches, XXI, “Truth”). See Catechism · Articles · Prayer Book.

  5. Joined to the Eucharist. “The same person to whom the function of absolving is committed has also the function of administering the Eucharist. These two duties never have been separated” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Ministry”). Since in the Eucharist the human agent is “most entirely lost and forgotten” (same), absolution must be the same kind of act, pointing away from the minister. See Eucharist.

  6. Common, not private. Confessing God as Father, Son and Spirit, “we can confess, He enables us to confess together, He gives us absolution together” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel I, “Christ the Sower”). The minister in the congregation is “there to represent its unity” (The Kingdom of Christ II, “The Ministry”). See unity.

  7. The counterfeit. The confessor-priest’s absolution is “Not actual freedom of conscience! Not the loosing a burden too heavy to be borne. The priest is able to say certain words which betoken that he absolves. He dare not trust those words to do their work” (Sermons Preached in Lincoln’s Inn Chapel IV, “The Eucharist Considered in its Relation to Confession and Absolution”). “His idea of Absolution is a direct interference with the meaning of the Sacrament” (same). See Catholic, sense 5.

  8. The white stone. In the Apocalypse the overcomer “shall have the white stone of Absolution, the true spiritual emancipation which the Balaamite and Nicolaitane emancipation has counterfeited” (Lectures on the Apocalypse, Lecture III). See Name.

Neighbouring words

  • freedom, emancipation, set free (its meaning). Senses 1 and 8.
  • conscience (what it frees). Sense 1; see conscience.
  • confession (its occasion). Senses 6 and 7.
  • forgiveness, remission, pardon (near synonyms). Sense 4.
  • minister, priest (its instrument). Senses 2, 3 and 5; see ordination · episcopacy.
  • Eucharist (its companion). Sense 5.
  • adoption, sonship (what it gives). Sense 3.

Scripture. John 20:23, Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them; John 8:36, If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed (sense 3); Revelation 2:17, I will give him a white stone (sense 8); Galatians 4:5, that we might receive the adoption of sons (sense 3).
