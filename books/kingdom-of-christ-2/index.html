Volume II

or, Hints to a Quaker respecting the Principles, Constitution, and Ordinances of the Catholic Church

The second volume continues the search for the signs of a universal and spiritual society (the Creeds, forms of worship, the Eucharist, the ministry, and the Scriptures), weighing at each step the objections of the Quaker, the Protestant, the rationalist and the Romanist.

It goes on to the relations of the Church with national bodies, and closes with the English Church and the systems that divide it: the Liberal, the Evangelical, and the High Church. Throughout, Maurice directs the reader away from sectarianism and toward the living reality of God’s Kingdom.

    2. Chapter V. On the Relations of the Church with National Bodies

      Oaths, marriage, property, and just war rest on a national order visible already in the Ten Commandments and in pagan Greece and Rome, carried forward into the nations of Christian Europe.

      1. Introductory
      2. Section I. Objections of the Quaker

        The Sermon on the Mount fulfils rather than abolishes judicial oaths and just retribution, answering the Quaker’s case against war, oaths, and a paid clergy.

      3. Section II. The Pure Theocratist

        The Covenanter and Nonjuror would have the nation punish heresy by the sword as ancient Israel did, while the Separatist would keep Church and State wholly apart, but each misreads their true relation.

      4. Section III. The Separatist
      5. Section IV. The Patrician

        Those who idealize the early Church’s first centuries as a golden age of discipline and unity forget its persecutions, heresies, and corruption, so national churches mark not decline but further providence.

      6. Section V. The Modern Statesman

        A statesman who claims sole authority over education, fearing the Church more than divided sects, cannot properly nurture family life, science, art, or morality, as a national Church can.

      7. Section VI. The Modern Interpreters of Prophecy

        Restoring the Jews would testify to the sanctity of nations without displacing the universal Church, Christ’s return will unveil rather than replace the kingdom already here, and Rome’s corrupt system calls for reform, not abandonment.

  3. Part III. The English Church and the Systems Which Divide It

    Read through its Liturgy and Articles, the English Church holds Catholic, Calvinist, Lutheran, and Quaker truths together without their rival systems, its bond with the nation forged by history rather than contract.

    1. Chapter I. How Far This Subject Is Connected with Those Previously Discussed

    2. Chapter II. Modern English Systems

      Liberal, Evangelical, and Catholic parties each hold a truth that collapses into its opposite once made a system, so a national Church free of party spirit can better serve dissenters, the poor, and the colonies.

  4. Note on the Athanasian Creed

    The Creed’s language of equality and subordination completes rather than contradicts Nicene doctrine, and its damnatory clauses warn against a sin anyone can commit, not license to judge others.
