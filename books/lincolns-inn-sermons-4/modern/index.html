Sermons Preached in Lincoln's Inn Chapel

Volume IV

Sermons, Volume 4, by Frederick Denison Maurice, restated paragraph by paragraph in modern English. This paraphrase follows the order and content of the original without adding interpretation. Drafted with claude-sonnet-5-5; reviewed by a human editor.

Contents

The one-line summaries are guides drafted with the help of AI, not Maurice’s words.

  1. God the Uprooter of Sects Sunday after Ascension

    The Pharisees’ sect is the plant God did not plant, since He planted a family for all nations; we must root out party spirit in ourselves and never found a rival party.

  2. The Meaning of the Gift of Tongues Whit-Sunday

    Pentecost’s tongues are a sign, not a vocabulary: what a missionary needs is sympathy and the Spirit uniting all nations in Christ, a gift for every age that lies and parties make seem withdrawn.

  3. The Uniting Name Trinity Sunday

    Baptizing nations into the Name of Father, Son and Holy Spirit, not merely praising a better teacher, gives them a living Person as their bond; the Church’s divisions come from forgetting this.

  4. The Circumcision and the Uncircumcision Merged in Christ First Sunday after Trinity

    Jew and Gentile are each renewed not by circumcision or its absence but in Christ, the living root of humanity; so England’s Christian faith cannot hinge on excluding or admitting Jews to Parliament.

  5. Daylight in the Heart the Preparation for the Day of Christ Second Sunday after Trinity

    Sincerity is clearness of heart, like sunlight, that comes from looking to Christ the Light rather than to ourselves or public opinion; it keeps us from stumbling until the day of Christ.

  6. The Ministry of Sovereigns Third Sunday after Trinity

    Even Nero is God’s minister for good, so all power is a trust answerable to a real Judge; faith in Christ, who makes the chief servant of all, underlies constitutional liberty.

  7. Why Christ Could Not Make His Authority Known to the Pharisees Fourth Sunday after Trinity

    Christ meets the priests’ demand for His authority with a question about John because only those who take John’s call to repentance as meant for themselves can understand His kingdom.

  8. The Eucharist Considered as a Declaration of Christ’s Death to Mankind Fifth Sunday after Trinity

    The Lord’s Supper, common bread and wine, proclaims to all mankind that the Lord died for all, so it is no private benefit; Romanist secrecy and Protestant exclusiveness both miss this.

  9. The Eucharist Considered as a Participation in the Unselfish Life of Christ Sixth Sunday after Trinity

    Communion is not a way to save oneself but a share in the Son of Man’s self-sacrifice: whoever seeks his own life loses it, and whoever gives it up for Christ finds it.

  10. The Eucharist Considered as Interpreting All Other Acts of Devotion Seventh Sunday after Trinity

    Prayer springs from the grace of Christ, the love of God and the Spirit’s communion, even where Christ is unknown, and the Eucharist, which declares this, interprets prayer rather than the reverse.

  11. The Eucharist Considered in its Relation to Confession and Absolution Eighth Sunday after Trinity

    Read in light of the Eucharist, the Prayer Book’s counsel for troubled consciences means leading the burdened out of self to Christ, not a priest’s probing of past sins.

  12. The Eucharist Considered as Christ’s Voice to a Lukewarm Church Ninth Sunday after Trinity

    The self-satisfied, neither-hot-nor-cold Laodicean Church mirrors our own age, yet Christ still stands knocking at the door, and the Lord’s Supper renews His promise of fellowship that humbles all in order to exalt all.

  13. The Book of Proverbs Illustrated by St. John Twenty-first Sunday after Trinity

    Wisdom in Proverbs is a living Person, found rather than made, and Jesus is that Light of the world; following Him, even by the Cross, keeps a man out of darkness.

  14. Mercy and Truth Not Opposed in the Nature of God or in the Transactions of Men Twenty-second Sunday after Trinity

    Mercy and Truth are never rivals in God, as theories of punishment and indulgence assume; together they purge iniquity, and the fear of that undivided Lord, not fear of penalty, turns men from evil.

  15. Christian Services and Christian Rewards Twenty-third Sunday after Trinity

    Paul’s sowing and reaping describes obedience to a law, not a trader’s bargain: the Christian’s reward is love returned, fresh work for the Master, and rest in God, so selfishness is what must go.

  16. The Eternal Not Opposed to the Present Twenty-fourth Sunday after Trinity

    Things seen are temporal and things unseen eternal, but both surround us now; eternity is no distant future but a present treasure in the unchanging Word, who frees us from Self, Sin and Death.

  17. The Renovation of the Will the Great Subject of Prayer Twenty-fifth Sunday after Trinity

    The Psalmist’s confession that his soul clings to the dust, followed by the cry to be quickened, shows that the deepest prayer asks for a renewed will, which only God’s own will can free.

  18. Homily I: On National Celebrations

    The State Services may rightly be laid aside, not because God has ceased to govern history, but because their language rang false; nations may celebrate again once a true Catholicism in Christ unites them.

  19. Homily II: On Theology and Government in India

    Theology and government have never stayed apart, and India shows it again; the nation should preach a Gospel rather than propagate a faith, with ruler and priest each witnessing to one Kingdom.

  20. Homily III: On Preaching to the Poor

    Preachers to rich and poor alike should do what John the Baptist did, announcing that the King of Heaven is at hand, rather than tailoring tricks to classes, and so call every man to repent.

  21. Note on the Second Homily

    Fresh evidence backs the Homily on India: government aid to secular teaching cramps missionaries, and Colonel Edwardes’s plan to seize temple property would repeat Alexandria’s failure, not Paul’s healing witness at Ephesus.
