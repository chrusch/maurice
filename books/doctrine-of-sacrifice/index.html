The Doctrine of Sacrifice Deduced from the Scriptures

A Series of Sermons

First published
1854
Length

Preached at Lincoln’s Inn in the spring of 1854, these sermons follow the idea of sacrifice through the Scriptures: from the offerings of Cain and Abel, Noah and Abraham, through the Passover, the Levitical law and David, to “the Lamb before the foundation of the world,” and on to the sacrifice of Christ as redemption, propitiation, peace-offering and victory.

The book appeared in the months after Maurice’s dismissal from King’s College, and is dedicated, in a long letter, to the members of the Young Men’s Christian Association, before whom his Theological Essays had been publicly attacked the previous winter.

Contents

The one-line summaries are guides drafted with the help of AI, not Maurice’s words.

  1. Dedicatory Letter

    Maurice rebuts Dr. Candlish’s charge that his teaching secretly agrees with the Unitarians, tracing his real debts to Irving, Erskine, and the Quakers, and grounding the book in the doctrine of sacrifice.

  2. Contents
  3. Sermon I. The Sacrifices of Cain and Abel

    Cain and Abel’s sacrifices differ not because God arbitrarily favours one gift over another, but because trust marks Abel’s offering and self-will marks Cain’s.

  4. Sermon II. Noah’s Sacrifice

    Noah’s altar and burnt offering, and God’s sweet-smelling pleasure in it, show that sacrifice springs from trust in a righteous, life-giving God rather than fear or bargaining, though its perfect fulfilment still lies ahead.

  5. Sermon III. The Sacrifice of Abraham

    When God asks Abraham to offer Isaac, the true sacrifice demanded is Abraham’s own will, already surrendered before the ram appears in the thicket.

  6. Sermon IV. Sacrifice of the Passover

    The Passover and the dedication of the firstborn declare Israel a redeemed and consecrated nation, so that every person and people, then and now, must choose to whom they are devoted.

  7. Sermon V. The Legal Sacrifices

    The Levitical sacrifices, offered voluntarily yet fixed by God’s own law at one tabernacle, teach that atonement is God’s gift to a repentant will, not a bargain struck through a priest’s private lore.

  8. Sermon VI. David’s Sacrifice

    David’s broken and contrite heart proves a more complete sacrifice than a flawless animal, since in it he surrenders himself to God, and shows why Israel’s true King must be a man of sorrows.

  9. Sermon VII. The Lamb Before the Foundation of the World

    Peter’s Lamb foreknown before the world’s foundation reveals sacrifice as the eternal, filial harmony of Father and Son, not a plan improvised, as Milton pictures it, only after Adam’s fall.

  10. Sermon VIII. Christ’s Sacrifice a Redemption

    Sacrifice belongs to God’s own nature, not merely to human sin, so Christ’s redemption buys men out of real bondage to inherited falsehood and idolatry, rather than paying off an angry God.

  11. Sermon IX. Christ’s Sacrifice a Deliverance from the Curse of the Law

    Christ became a curse to redeem us from the law’s curse, not by absorbing the Father’s wrath, but by freeing the sin-bound conscience the law could only condemn, never save.

  12. Sermon X. The Sacrifice of Christ a Propitiation

    Propitiation is not a means of making God favourable but God’s own declaration, in Christ’s blood, that he has already made peace and continually meets his people at the mercy-seat.

  13. Sermon XI. The Sacrifice of Christ the Purification of the Conscience

    Where the blood of calves and goats under the old covenant could only purify the flesh, Christ’s willing self-offering as the true High Priest cleanses the conscience itself, once for all.

  14. Sermon XII. Christ Made Sin for Us

    Paul’s stark phrase that Christ was made sin should not be softened to a mere sin-offering, for it describes his sinless, entire sympathy with sinners so that we might become God’s righteousness.

  15. Sermon XIII. Christ’s Sacrifice the Peace-Offering for Mankind

    Christ’s cross is the peace-offering that breaks down the wall between Jew and Gentile, reconciling both to God and to one another and opening access to the Father through one Spirit.

  16. Sermon XIV. Christ’s Sacrifice a Power to Form Us After his Likeness

    The mind of Christ, who emptied himself, took a servant’s form, and obeyed unto death on the cross, is the very pattern God calls believers to share and by which he was exalted.

  17. Sermon XV. Christ’s Death a Victory Over the Devil

    The devil is the Accuser whose slanders use fear of death to enslave the conscience, but Christ’s own death, freely embraced in love, breaks that power and proves death no longer separates us from God.

  18. Sermon XVI. Christ the Advocate

    Christ the Advocate does not plead with a reluctant Father to forgive but reveals, as the Father’s faithful witness, a love that was always there and needs no persuading.

  19. Sermon XVII. Christ the High-Priest

    A true high priest must share the infirmity of those for whom he offers sacrifice, and Christ, learning obedience through his own anguish and suffering, becomes both perfect priest and sacrifice at once.

  20. Sermon XVIII. The Adoration of the Lamb

    Every real chain of sin has been broken by Christ’s blood for every person, so that all who believe are made kings and priests to God, not merely excused from punishment.

  21. Sermon XIX. The Word of God Conquering by Sacrifice

    The rider on the white horse, faithful and true and clothed in his own blood, wages a righteous war against selfishness in every heart, proving sacrifice the true law of the universe.

  22. Note

    Maurice acknowledges a debt to Archdeacon Hare’s sermon on sacrifice for some ethical passages, and welcomes the independent agreement of Mr. Thompson’s Bampton Lectures on the same subject.
