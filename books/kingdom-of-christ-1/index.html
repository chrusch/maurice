What is the Kingdom of Christ? Is it simply the Church? A future hope? A private spiritual experience? Or has Christ already established a universal kingdom into which humanity is called?

In this, his greatest theological work, Maurice develops one of the central ideas of his theology: that Christianity is not fundamentally the creation of a religious party, but God’s own act in Christ to reconcile humanity to Himself and establish His enduring Kingdom. Rather than dismissing Quakers, Protestants, Roman Catholics, or other traditions, Maurice seeks the truth each has preserved while showing how every partial system points beyond itself to the larger reality of Christ’s universal reign.

This first volume opens with a dialogue with a Quaker, examines Quakerism, “pure Protestantism,” Unitarianism and the philosophical movements of the age, and begins the inquiry into the signs of a spiritual society with Baptism.
