This volume follows the Church’s calendar from Advent Sunday 1856 through the week before Easter. Maurice refuses to treat doctrine, sacrament and season as separate compartments: Advent’s meaning and the Eucharist’s meaning explain each other, and the struggle against flesh, world and devil in Lent is the same warfare named differently in every text he opens.

Two convictions run through every sermon: that God has already, once for all, reconciled the world to Himself in Christ; and that Christ is not a distant historical figure but the Son of Man continually present and reigning, so that the gospel is always good news being enacted now rather than a doctrine merely believed.

About this digital edition

How the text was made, and the printer's errors corrected

The original book

Sermons, Volume 1 by Frederick Denison Maurice.

This edition

Produced 2026-09-04 from a page scan of the printed book, as part of a project to make F. D. Maurice’s works freely available in every useful digital form. The guiding principle throughout was fidelity to the printed text: Maurice’s own spelling, capitalization, punctuation, ligatures, and typography rule, except where a printing error was identified and deliberately emended (each such change is listed below).

How this text was made

The transcription pipeline combines automated tools, AI models, and page-by-page human review:

  • OCR and conversion. The scan was OCRed (ocrmypdf/Tesseract) and converted to structured text (marker), then cleaned by a language model (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001) working chunk by chunk.
  • Human review of every machine change. Every word the cleanup model altered was reviewed against an image of the printed line. Footnotes were verified against the printed page and renumbered uniformly.
  • Quotation marks were normalized to the printer’s own conventions and validated by a nesting checker; every remaining irregularity was inspected against the page image. The final text has fully paired quotation marks.
  • Italics were restored (the OCR text layer records none) by a vision model (claude-sonnet-5) cross-checked against a deterministic measurement of letterform slant on the page images, with per-word statistical calibration derived from the book itself. Every page was then reviewed by a human editor against the print. This edition carries 250 italic spans.
  • Small capitals and ligatures (æ, œ) are reproduced as printed.
  • A whole-book audit (claude-sonnet-5) checked structure, headings, footnotes, and Greek; its findings were reviewed by a human editor against the print before any were applied.

The text contains 1 footnote and 65 small-caps renderings of the print.
