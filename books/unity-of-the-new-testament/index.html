The Unity of the New Testament

A Synopsis of the First Three Gospels and of the Epistles of St. James, St. Jude, St. Peter, and St. Paul, with a Commentary on the Epistle to the Hebrews

Maurice’s conviction here is that the Gospels and Epistles, so often read as fragments or set against one another, share one continuous purpose. He looks beneath both the critics who divide the books, such as Strauss, and the harmonists who defend them, finding that both have already conceded that the first three Gospels give us a teacher of Nazareth while His divinity is the contribution of John and Paul.

He calls his method a Synopsis rather than a commentary: not a hunt for isolated lessons, but patient attention to how each Gospel and Epistle serves the design of the book it belongs to, and how all those designs converge on a single Living Person, from the first line of Matthew to the last page of Hebrews both Son of God and King of Men.

About this digital edition

How the text was made, and the printer's errors corrected

The original book

The Unity of the New Testament by Frederick Denison Maurice.

This edition

Produced 2026-08-05 from a page scan of the printed book, as part of a project to make F. D. Maurice’s works freely available in every useful digital form. The guiding principle throughout was fidelity to the printed text: Maurice’s own spelling, capitalization, punctuation, ligatures, and typography rule, except where a printing error was identified and deliberately emended (each such change is listed below).

How this text was made

The transcription pipeline combines automated tools, AI models, and page-by-page human review:

  • OCR and conversion. The scan was OCRed (ocrmypdf/Tesseract) and converted to structured text (marker), then cleaned by a language model (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001) working chunk by chunk.
  • Human review of every machine change. Every word the cleanup model altered was reviewed against an image of the printed line. Footnotes were verified against the printed page and renumbered uniformly.
  • Quotation marks were normalized to the printer’s own conventions and validated by a nesting checker; every remaining irregularity was inspected against the page image. The final text has fully paired quotation marks.
  • Italics were restored (the OCR text layer records none) by a vision model (claude-sonnet-5) cross-checked against a deterministic measurement of letterform slant on the page images, with per-word statistical calibration derived from the book itself. Every page was then reviewed by a human editor against the print. This edition carries 433 italic spans.
  • Small capitals and ligatures (æ, œ) are reproduced as printed.
  • A whole-book audit (claude-sonnet-5) checked structure, headings, footnotes, and Greek; its findings were reviewed by a human editor against the print before any were applied.

The text contains 14 footnotes and 39 small-caps renderings of the print.
